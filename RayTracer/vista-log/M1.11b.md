# Vista M1.11b — The display look

The slice's goal, deliverable and verify are in [`vista-plan.md`](../vista-plan.md) §10, with a status line that links here.
Section references (§n) are to the plan; "the cost ledger" is [`cost-ledger.md`](cost-ledger.md); sheets cited by name are in
[`vista-views/sheets/`](../vista-views/sheets/README.md).

*M1.11b status (2026-10-21): done.* The PNG's new defaults are **AgX's punchy look** (`--look punchy`) and **+1 EV** of the incident
meter's own compensation (`--compensation 1`), chosen from the sheet below. Nothing on the GPU changed and no EXR moved; `--look base
--compensation 0` writes `main`'s PNG byte for byte. `Vista.Tests` has 690 tests (684 before); `--gpu-selftest` passes (55 checks).
Both choices are provisional: they and M1.10a's meter are re-judged at M1.17,
once real materials exist.

*What was built:*

- **The punchy look** (`DisplayTransform.AgxPunchy`, `DisplayLook`): Filament's `agxLook` (`filament/src/ToneMapper.cpp`, Wrensch's
  after Blender's) ported onto M1.10a's AgX, where Filament applies it: after the sigmoid, before the outset. An ASC CDL power of 1.35
  per channel, then the saturation about the luma (Rec.709's weights, taken before the power, as Filament's) scaled by 1.4. One
  change, in the reference values too: the channel is clamped at 0 before the power (see the gotchas). Middle grey falls from sRGB
  128 to 88, about a stop; the look deepens the mid-tones and adds colour, so it needs the compensation below.
- **The meter's own compensation** (`IncidentMeter.DefaultCompensationEv` = +1 EV; `ExposureInfo.MeterCompensationEv`): stops the
  meter adds beside the camera's EV, `--compensation <EV>` (±10) on the command line, in a watch's options file and as a sheet axis.
  Unset, the incident meter adds +1 EV and the average meter 0, so `--meter average --display aces` still writes M1.10a's old look.
  The camera's EV stays the camera's (`exposure.compensationEv`) and the two add up in `exposure.appliedEv`.
- **The options:** `--look punchy|base` (the ACES fit has no looks and ignores it), `--compensation`, and the sheet axes
  `look=base,punchy` and `compensation=0,0.5,1`; one render serves every cell. **Sidecar schema 14:** `image.look` (`none` under
  ACES) and `exposure.meterCompensationEv`. Sheet labels read both, older sidecars as the base look and no compensation.

  `m1-11b-down-slope-looks.png`.
- **Why punchy +1:** on the five views that are not in shadow it is as bright as M1.11a's look (the frame's mean within 3 of 255) with
  1.6–2.5 times the saturation: blue skies, golden haze on the ridges, warm lit faces at golden hour. Plain AgX at +1 is brighter and
  paler still (it brightens without adding colour); punchy at +0 and +0.5 is dark. The sheet's steps end at +1, but the ridges'
  brightest tenth is already at 225, so a further half stop would start to wash the brightest view out.
- **What it costs:** down the slope every variant is dark and punchy darkens it most (mean 23 at +1 against 34): the frame is mostly a
  valley in shadow since M1.11a left that camera 552 m up (M1.11c re-places it), and the look's power deepens shadows. The sky-only
  view (the part of the sky 70–90° from the sun) turns deep blue with its red channel at 0: the saturation pushes red below zero,
  where the outset clamps it.
- **The alpenglow sweep** (`atmosphere/zermatt-july.recipe.json` from `atmosphere/range.camera.json`, the sun at +2° to −1.5°; 320×180):
  under punchy +1 the frame's mean runs 84, 81, 79, 81, 96 and 136 (base +0: 88, 85, 84, 86, 98, 132) at saturation 0.54 to 0.31
  (base +0: 0.29 to 0.17). `m1-11b-alpenglow.png`.
- **The defaults beside M1.11a's look** (`m1-11b-before-after.png`): every view's base +0 and punchy +1 cells, one EXR per row.
- **The old look is unchanged:** the four measurement views rendered by `main` (bfa57bfc) are byte-identical, PNG and EXR, to this
  build's `--look base --compensation 0`. A `--preview` with neither option (the default camera at golden hour) writes the sheet's
  punchy +1 cell byte for byte, and its sidecar (schema 14) records `image.look` punchy and `exposure.meterCompensationEv` 1.
- **`--gpu-selftest`** passes (55 checks; none new: the look and the compensation are CPU-only).
- **The punchy look against its source:** three.js r169's own `AgXToneMapping`, loaded from unpkg and compiled by WebGL 2 in a browser

- **CI** (`Vista.Tests`): the punchy look against those values; middle grey at sRGB 88, greys grey and monotonic, more colour than the

  ACES recorded with no look.

*M1.11b decisions (revisit freely):*

- **The look is AgX's, the compensation the meter's.** The punchy look is a grade of AgX's base, so it lives on the curve; the ACES fit
  ignores it. The +1 EV belongs to the incident meter (its card is right for a sunlit grey, but under the punchy look a landscape at
  it reads a stop dark), so the average meter's default stays 0 and M1.10a's `--meter average --display aces` stays the old look.
- **Filament's look on three.js's AgX, not Blender's.** Blender's punchy is an OCIO look on a 3D LUT; Filament's is a closed form on
  the same pipeline three.js ported, and the plan named its numbers. Filament's own sigmoid fit differs from three.js's (Wrensch's);
  only the look was taken from Filament, so the base stays M1.10a's.
- **Left for the review:** the deep-blue sky under punchy; the dark down-slope view (judge it again after M1.11c re-places the
  camera); whether +1 is enough once materials brighten the ground (M1.17 re-judges both).

*M1.11b gotchas:*

- **Three.js's sigmoid dips below 0:** Wrensch's fit gives −0.0023 at the bottom of the encoding (10 stops under middle grey), where
  the look's power would be NaN (GLSL's `pow` of a negative is undefined, C#'s NaN). Filament's own fit never goes below 0.0028. The
  port clamps the channel at 0 first; near black the look then lifts the colour by 0.4 times the (negative) luma, 2·10⁻⁷ after the
  outset and the power, invisible in 8 bits.
- **A sheet varies one axis:** the six-cell choice is two sheets per view (one per look, the compensation as the axis), tiled with
  `--sheet-from`; the script checks the six cells' EXRs by hash.

**M1.11b hand-off.** To understand M1.11b, start by reading `DisplayTransform.AgxPunchy` and `Agx(Vector3, DisplayLook)` in
`Vista.Core/Output/DisplayTransform.cs`, then `IncidentMeter.DefaultCompensationEv` and the exposure block of
`Vista.Core/Cli/PreviewSession.cs` (`meterCompensation`), then `Vista.Tests/DisplayCurveTests.cs`.

*Commands* (from `RayTracer/`):

```
dotnet run --project Vista.App -c Release -- --preview --recipe r.json --seed 42 --out p.png [--look punchy|base] [--compensation 1]
dotnet run --project Vista.App -c Release -- --sheet --recipe r.json --seed 42 --look punchy --vary compensation=0,0.5,1 --out s.png
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File vista-views/look/render.ps1 [-Before ..\main\Vista.App.dll] [-Width 480] [-SweepWidth 320] [-Spp 64] [-Compensations 0,0.5,1] [-Out folder]
```
