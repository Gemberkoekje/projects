# Vista M1.13a — Valley haze layer

The slice's goal, deliverable and verify are in [`vista-plan.md`](../vista-plan.md) §10, with a status line that links here.
Section references (§n) are to the plan; "the cost ledger" is [`cost-ledger.md`](cost-ledger.md); sheets cited by name are in
[`vista-views/sheets/`](../vista-views/sheets/README.md).

*M1.13a status (2026-10-25): done.* **A valley haze layer in the air:** more of the haze under an inversion's lid, uniform below it
and gone within 132 m above it, so its top reads crisp from ten kilometres; every march takes its exact mean over each step, so the
lid cuts no band into a slope, and without it the air is M1.13's bit for bit. **The weather chain sets it** from the night's cold
pool: as deep as the night's cooling (0.75 of the day's range) is undone by the inversion's rise, 446 m at Zermatt on an October
dawn; broken up by the sun's heat since sunrise against the pool's deficit, gone about four hours after sunrise in spring and kept
past noon over a snowy floor; forming again after the day's high; its haze the valley's own, held and grown under the lid (25 km to
see under it at that dawn against 82 km above). A pinned visibility under the lid stands at any hour (`--valley-haze`). Seen from
shoulders just above the pool, the ranges' feet fade into a pale band while the peaks stay crisp (`m1-13a-morning.png`; with a
strong layer, beside the user's paintings, `m1-13a-paintings.png`). **The scattering tables grew** (ten orders, 24 heights, 12
view-to-sun texels) because a dense layer holds light through more scatterings: a strong layer's view went from 10.5% dark against
the reference to 1.1%, and the clear air's segments are closer too; the LUT build takes 4.0 s instead of 1.0. `Vista.Tests` has 829
tests (809 before); `--gpu-selftest` passes 70 checks (63).

*What was built:*

- **The layer** (`Vista.Core/Atmosphere/AtmosphereModel.cs`; its twin in `Vista.App/Shaders/VistaAtmosphere.hlsli`): more of the
  same haze trapped under an inversion, added to the haze's own profile. Three fields: the lid's altitude (`ValleyHazeTopMetres`,
  the middle of its fall), its softness (`ValleyHazeSoftnessMetres`, a logistic of 30 m: 90% to 10% of the layer in 132 m, crisp
  from ten kilometres) and its extinction at 550 nm under the lid (`ValleyHazeExtinction550`, per metre, 0 for none). It has the
  haze's Ångström exponent, albedo and asymmetry, so the haze's coefficients serve it and its density is counted in the haze's unit
  (its own density at sea level); only where a pinned visibility leaves no haze of the air's own does the clear day's 0.1 set that
  unit, so the layer keeps its coefficients in otherwise clean air. Its column has a closed form (a softplus), which the zenith
  columns use.
- **Every march takes the layer's exact mean over each step** (`AerosolStepDensity`, `ValleyHazeMean`): the layer's integral
  through the altitudes a step spans, `w·(softplus(x_a) − softplus(x_b))` over their difference, taken along the step's two halves
  so a chord that dips to its lowest inside a step is still right. The columns LUT, the sky view and ground irradiance, the
  single- and multiple-scattering tables and the aerial-perspective march all take it (the step's ends come from
  `AtmosphereLuts.SegmentEnds`, the same arithmetic as `Segment`); the haze's own profile and the molecules and ozone are still
  sampled at the middle, so without a layer every table and every image is M1.13's bit for bit (CI). A sample per step would miss
  a layer's optical depth by up to 0.03 where the lid crosses a step's middle (a 3% jump in the light from beyond: a band across a
  smooth slope); the means keep it within 10⁻⁴ in eight steps (CI). The reference mode needs nothing new: delta tracking samples
  points, and the layer, like the haze, thins upward, so the densest point of a segment is still its majorant.
- **The scattering tables grew** (`AtmosphereScattering`): ten orders instead of five, 24 heights instead of 16 and 12 view-to-sun
  texels instead of 8 (the checks below; the decision is the first of this slice's).
- **The weather chain** (`Vista.Core/Weather/ValleyHaze.cs`, wired in `LinkedSliders.Resolve`): the night's cold pool and what
  is left of it at the hour.
  - *The night's cooling* at the valley floor below the free air: 0.75 of the day's normal range (the climate's own measure of clear,
    still nights; Vista's skies are clear, and a clear night cools more than the normals' average of clear and cloudy ones).
  - *The pool's depth*: where that cooling is undone, by the inversion's own rise (10 °C/km, a valley inversion's typical one,
    Whiteman 2000) and the free air's fall (6.5 °C/km), at most half the world's relief (its crest, a new 95th percentile beside the
    footing in `WorldFooting`, over its lowest valleys): 446 m at Zermatt in mid-October, 480 m in April, under 200 m at Lofoten,
    800 m at Yosemite in July.
  - *What is left of it*: from sunrise to the day's high, the share of the pool's heat deficit (`ρ·c_p·ΔT·D/2`) the sun has not yet
    made up: clear-sky sunlight since sunrise (Meinel and Meinel 1976's air-mass attenuation), times the floor's absorption (0.8,
    or 0.2 under snow), the share that heats the air (0.3) and the valley's amplification (2, Whiteman 1990): Whiteman and McKee
    1982's thermodynamic model of break-up, simplified. At Zermatt in April the pool is gone about four hours after sunrise, as deep
    valleys' inversions are; a snowy January floor keeps half of it at noon. After the day's high it forms again with the night's
    cooling on the daily curve (or keeps the morning's remnant, if more). The lid stands at the valley floor plus the depth times
    what is left: a breaking pool sinks (Whiteman 1982's descending top) and a forming one deepens.
  - *The haze under the lid*: the valley's own haze (the fog/haze slider's at the floor) held under the lid as the night goes on (up
    to twice as much by dawn) and grown by the hour's humidity against the day's (M1.11's growth law); the layer is what that adds.
    At a mid-October dawn at Zermatt: 0.082 per km at 550 nm on the valley's 0.027, 25 km to see under the lid against 82 km in
    the valley's own haze.
- **The slider** (`Slider.ValleyHaze`, the slider file's `valleyHaze`, `--valley-haze <km> | off`; advanced): the visibility under
  the lid, auto or pinned. A pinned visibility takes the night's whole pool at any hour and adds what the valley's own haze lacks to
  reach it; `off` pins 500 km, which adds nothing. Its reading says what the chain did ("auto: a cold pool 446 m deep at dawn from
  the night's 7.4 °C of cooling (0.75 of the day's 9.8 °C range), 98% of it left at 07:30 after 0.03 of its 1.67 MJ/m² from the
  sun on a floor absorbing 80% of it; under a lid at 2046 m the haze adds 0.082/km at 550 nm to the valley's 0.027"); the sidecar
  (schema 22) records it, the layer in the weather record, and the model's new fields.
- **Two views where the layer shows** (`vista-views/haze/misty-ridges.camera.json`, `across-valley.camera.json`): shoulders just
  above the pool, about 500 m over the valley floor, looking across valleys at the ranges beyond; found by a scratch search over the
  seed-42 world for ground 450–650 m over its footing from which the valley floor is seen at 2–10 km with ground rising 300 m and
  more over the eye beyond it. The committed views stand 3–4 km up, where a pool a few hundred metres deep fills only the valley
  floors.
- **The checks' tools**: `--atmosphere-check --valley-haze height,perKm` adds a layer to the segment check; `--gpu-selftest` checks
  the LUTs' and the aerial march's parity with a strong layer too (seven checks, 70 in all).

*M1.13a decisions (revisit freely):*

- **The scattering tables grew with the layer**: ten orders (5 before), 24 heights (16) and 12 view-to-sun texels (8). With a strong
  layer (6 km under a lid 446 m up) the old tables left the misty-ridges view 10.5% dark against the reference (the lowest third
  17%): single scattering alone was within 1% (0.1% overall), so the light was lost in the multiple scattering. Ten orders recovered
  half of it (twenty no more than ten); 32 heights alone overshot (+5.5%) and 16 view-to-sun texels alone did not (−9.1%); 24 × 12
  with ten orders came to −1.1% and 32 × 16 to +0.1%. 24 × 12 is the middle: the orders' build 1.0 → 4.0 s (3.7 s of LUTs on the
  self-test's scene), the tables' memory 2.25× (the summed orders kept: 46 → 104 MB; 260 MB more for the build's scratch), and
  the segment check better everywhere, clear air too. 32 × 16 would take 6.7 s and 4× the memory for the last 1%. Three constants
  (`AtmosphereScattering.HeightSize`, `NuSize`, `Orders`, with their twins in `VistaAtmosphere.hlsli`) take it back.
- **The pool's depth comes from the night's cooling and the inversion's rise, not from a depth constant**: the cooling share (0.75
  of the day's range) and the inversion's 10 °C/km set it, so a dry valley with a big range (Yosemite) gets a deep pool, a windy
  coast (Lofoten) a shallow one, and a world with little relief no deeper than half of it.
- **Break-up by the sun's heat, not by the clock**: the morning share comes from the heat the sun has delivered since sunrise against
  the pool's deficit, so the season, the latitude and a snowy floor decide how long a pool lasts, and the evening share from the
  daily curve's cooling. A temperature fraction alone (the curve's warming since dawn) would have broken every pool by mid-morning,
  snow or not.
- **The haze under the lid is the valley's own, held and grown**: no new aerosol type; a pinned visibility (the fog/haze slider) of
  clean air leaves an auto valley haze nothing to hold. Trapping up to twice the daytime haze is a guess at the night's local smoke
  and dust kept under the lid (valley PM10 in winter inversions runs two to three times the day's); the humidity's growth is M1.11's.
- **A pinned valley haze stands at any hour, with the night's whole pool**: so a sheet or a painting's composition can have the mist
  at noon. The pinned value is the visibility under the lid, the fog/haze slider's unit; `off` is a visibility no shorter than the
  valley's own.
- **A uniform layer, not one thickening toward the floor**: seen from just above the lid, a line of sight's path through a uniform
  layer grows toward the range's foot, and the foot fades as in the user's paintings; seen from inside, everything veils with
  distance. The paintings' mist is densest at the water; a gradient inside the pool is M4's (with terrain-following mist).
- **The lid's softness is fixed** (30 m): from 10 km a lid of 132 m reads as a crisp top; it is the photo's band. Not a slider.

*M1.13a gotchas:*

- **The time of day now moves the air's tables**: the auto valley haze follows the hour, so a watch session's time-of-day change
  rebuilds the atmosphere tables and the scattering orders (about 4 s with the larger tables) instead of the sky view alone; the
  watch test's expectation changed with a comment. A pinned valley haze does not follow the hour, and keeps M1.8's staging.
- **High views hide the layer**: the committed views stand 3–4 km above the valley floors, and a pool of 300–500 m fills only the
  floors' bottoms; at 320 px a strong layer moved the default view's mean by 1.8 levels, most of it the meter. The layer shows from
  shoulders just above it (the new views), which the auto camera (M1.14) can now look for.
- **A footing made by hand has no crest**: `WorldFooting`'s new crest is 0 unless `Of` measured it, and the valley haze then takes no
  relief cap (a hand-made footing without a crest says nothing about relief).
- **TaskStop ends a background shell, not always its dotnet child**: when the user asked for the GPU back, a reference render
  went on running until it was found by its command line and stopped.
- **Bash heredocs ate backslashes again** in Python edit scripts (`\\n` inside C# string literals); they went through files.
- **`sed -i` rewrote CRLF files as LF**; git normalizes them on commit (`core.autocrlf`), so the diffs stay the changed lines.

*Known limits:*

- **One lid for the whole world**: the layer depends on altitude alone, so every valley fills to the same altitude, a hanging valley
  above it holds none, and the land beyond the tile (the ground sphere) lies under it too. Mist that fills some valleys and not
  others, follows the terrain or lies in patches is M4's.
- **The air in a ridge's shadow is lit** (M1.4's limit): the layer glows in a valley the sun does not reach. On the misty ridges at
  07:30 with a strong layer the shadowed reference is 1.4% darker overall, 2.8% in the frame's middle third (`m1-13a-shadow.png`);
  a lower sun and a deeper valley would show more.
- **No daytime lid**: by late morning the chain leaves no layer, while a real summer afternoon holds its haze under the convective
  boundary layer's top, 1.5–2.5 km up with a crisp edge: the band in the user's 11:08 photo over the foreland, which a pinned layer
  stands in for (M4's seasonal aerosol load).
- **The far field is mountains to the horizon**: from 10 km the layer lies in the valleys as streaks, not as the continuous band the
  photo's lowlands make (`m1-13a-photo.png`).
- **A dense layer's multiple scattering is still a few percent off** inside it: with the larger tables the segment check looking along
  a strong layer toward the sun misses by 6.7–8.2% on average (its worst segments by up to a third), where the haze's forward lobe in
  light scattered many times is sharper than the tables' sphere quadrature of 32 × 32 directions (probably; not separated). Seen from
  above it, the views are within 1–2% (the image check).
- **The pool knows no valley's shape**: its depth and break-up take one amplification (2) for every valley, its cooling a share of the
  normals' range for every night; there are no clouds or wind to stop a pool forming.
- **The layer has the haze's colour**: no spectral change for the humid particles under the lid (as M1.11's growth).
- **The high views barely change**: the committed views stand above the pool; the new shoulder views show it.
