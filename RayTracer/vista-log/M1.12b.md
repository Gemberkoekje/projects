# Vista M1.12b — Forest as material

The slice's goal, deliverable and verify are in [`vista-plan.md`](../vista-plan.md) §10, with a status line that links here.
Section references (§n) are to the plan; "the cost ledger" is [`cost-ledger.md`](cost-ledger.md); sheets cited by name are in
[`vista-views/sheets/`](../vista-views/sheets/README.md).

*M1.12b status (2026-10-23): done.* Forest grows below the place's tree line as a material of the ground's rules: dark conifer canopy on the
lower slopes, in closed stands with clearings, ragged at its upper edge, never on crags, talus or where water stood. Its spectrum
is a canopy's as a whole (a few percent in the visible), shaded by MODIS's kernel-driven BRDF (the needles' volume scattering and
the crowns' shadows and hot spot), and its crowns are a detail kind of the mesh, domes 8 m apart and 2 m high, bounded only where
forest grows, so a forested crest bumps the skyline at 4K. `--forest off` renders `main`'s images byte for byte (PNG and EXR, the four
ledger views) and `main`'s meshes triangle for triangle. **Seed 42's world sits high** (§11): its lowest ground stands 391 m above
Zermatt's valley floor, so only 3.2% of it lies below the 2276 m tree line and the committed views barely change; with the world's
base at 1000 m a third of it is forest and the over-forest view reads like the user's April photo (`m1-12b-photo.png`). Where the
world should sit is the user's call. A sample costs 4–6% more on the ground (the cost ledger). `Vista.Tests` has 769 tests (758
before); `--gpu-selftest` passes 62 checks (61 before).

*What was built:*

- **The tree line** (`Vista.Core/Weather/TreeLine.cs`): where the place's warmest month, along the lapse rate, falls to 10 °C, the
  warmest month at the Alps' climatic tree line (its growing season averages about 6.4 °C, Körner and Paulsen 2004). Zermatt's is
  2276 m, 615 m below its grass line (4 °C of warmest month apart); `GroundCover` carries it beside the grass line, with
  `Forest` (`--forest off`: none).
- **Where forest grows** (`MaterialRules.Evaluate`, the shader's `MaterialWeightsOf`, the same operations): of the ground rock and scree
  leave, forest below the tree line (±80 m, 100 m lower on a fully poleward face, moved ±80 m by the 60 m breakup noise and ±30 m by
  the 11 m one, so its upper edge breaks into groups), on slopes under 38–48° (past where grass thins out, 36°), where no water stood
  (the wet rule's depth), and where the forest's own noise (250 m, a third stream, `MaterialNoise.ForestBreakup`) is above a narrow
  clearing threshold (−0.5 to −0.35) that moisture moves: the drainage's upstream area from 100 m² (a spur's crest) to 10⁴ m² (a
  hollow) shifts it by ±0.1, a typical hillside cell (about 10³ m²) neither way, so spurs open a few more clearings and hollows stay
  forested. The open ground left is M1.12's: meadow below the grass line, else soil. Rock and scree do not change:
  the forest takes only what they leave. Twenty-one rows of constants now (336 bytes; the shader's buffer holds 512).
- **The canopy's spectrum** (`MaterialCatalog.Ground`, id 5, `forest`, tier 5): a conifer canopy as a whole seen from above, needles,
  shadowed crowns and gaps together, so a few percent in the visible (0.019 at 400 nm, 0.045 at the green peak, 0.021 at 660 nm) and
  its red edge past the hero wavelengths; authored from that class's typical shape, not fitted to a sample. It goes through M1.12a's
  bins with the rest: 0.57 ΔE₇₆ and 0.15% in luminance off dense 1 nm integration (grass 0.54; the engine's table
  2.87), most of it the red edge past the last wavelength.
- **The canopy's shading** (`CanopyKernels` in `VistaMaterials.hlsli`, its twin `Vista.Core/Rendering/CanopyBrdf.cs`): MODIS's
  kernel-driven BRDF, ρ_iso·(1 + kv·K_vol + kg·K_geo), RossThick's volume scattering of the needles and LiSparse-Reciprocal's crowns
  (spheroids twice as high as wide) casting shadows on the gaps between them, hidden in the hot spot. kv 0.5 and kg 0.25, typical of
  conifer forest in the visible (not fitted to a site), and ρ_iso the spectrum over the kernels' white-sky albedo (1 + 0.189·kv −
  1.378·kg), so the canopy's albedo under a white sky is its spectrum's. Both zeniths are held within 70°, where MODIS fits the
  kernels (beyond, LiSparse's secants run away), and the factor never below 0. The integrator adds `Canopy·K` to its BRDF beside
  Oren–Nayar's backscatter; Russian roulette still decides on ρ_iso. `--canopy rough` shades the forest as M1.12's rough diffuse
  ground at its own roughness (σ = 1); Lambert's diffuse shades it as Lambert's.
- **The crowns** (`Vista.Core/World/CanopyDetail.cs`): a detail kind of the mesh. One crown per 8 m cell at a jittered spot, a dome
  (a cone rounded over its whole radius) of its own height and width, the crowns meeting each other and the gaps in wide rounded
  creases, the gaps at the ground, centred on its mean: relief 2 m, amplitude 1.42 m. Its envelope was measured as M1.9's (2 M random
  segments at eleven lengths, ×1.15; the curvature bound below, 37.6 ×1.15) and a test checks it on fresh segments. Below the mesh the
  shading adds the branches' texture (the bump's 0.4–1.6 m octaves, 0.3 of each wavelength) and a 40 m tone of stands (±25%).
- **The forest in the mesh** (`MaterialField.WithForest`, `ForestPlacement`, `MeshSettings.Forest`): where forest grows is the
  scene's, not the world's (the place's tree line against the altitude the scene gives height 0), so the world's own field has no
  forest and a scene's mesh carries a field that shares the world's maps and adds the forest's detail weights: the rules' forest at
  each sample, averaged over 7×7 as rock's and scree's, dropped under 5%, faded out over the grid's outer band (beyond the grid the
  detail reads the edge's weights), and never more than rock and scree leave. `WorldCache` keys the hierarchy on the placement.
- **The canopy's bound only where it grows.** The crowns stand higher than the other kinds' detail (1.42 m against rock's 1.13 and the
  smooth noise's 1.26). The world's own bound leaves the canopy out unless a forest grows (`MaterialDetail`), the bound by region below
  the grid counts it only in boxes it reaches, and the hierarchy's grid-level vertices take the bound without it (`OpenBound`, with the
  smooth kind's gradient as M1.12 measured it) wherever no cell of their box holds forest (`CanopyIn`, a summed-area table of the
  cells). So with `--forest off` the mesh, and the image, is M1.12's.
- **CLI, sidecar, sheets:** `--forest on|off`, `--canopy kernels|rough` (and the sheet axes `forest` and `canopy`); `--canopy-compare`
  (the forest's luminance by its kernels over rough, over the pixels whose material is forest), and `--diffuse-compare` and it take
  `--altitude` and `--sun-elevation`; sidecar schema 19 (`materials.forest`, `treeLineMetres` and where it came from, the canopy's
  amplitude and gradient, a dry run's forest share; `image.canopy`); the labels name both; the material map and `--debug material` in
  dark green; the self-test's parity probe returns twelve floats a place.

*M1.12b decisions (revisit freely):*

- **The tree line from the warmest month at 10 °C**, as M1.12's grass line from 6 °C: one climate number per place, in the same table.
  Körner and Paulsen's growing-season mean (6.4 °C) needs the season's length, which the normals table does not hold; in the Alps the
  two agree. Maritime places get the same caveat as the grass line (Lofoten's tree line comes out low).
- **Forest takes the open ground, never rock or scree**, so M1.12's rock and talus stand where they stood and only meadow and soil
  below the line become forest; a talus below a face stays scree even in a forested valley (real forests do grow onto old talus;
  M5 can).
- **Moisture moves the clearings, not the line.** Dry spurs open into clearings and hollows stay forested, through the forest's own
  250 m noise; the tree line itself follows only the climate, the aspect and the breakup.
- **Closed forest or open ground, not half of each** (tuned on the first sheets): with trees only to 33–43°, a wide clearing edge
  (−0.55 to −0.2) and moisture moving it ±0.25 from 300 m² to 3·10⁴ m² (which counted nearly every hillside cell as dry), the forest's
  weight sat between 0 and 1 over wide areas, and the shading's sharpening broke it into meadow flecks; the gully walls of the
  eroded slopes, too steep for those trees but not for grass, added more. With trees to 38–48°, a narrow edge and the moisture centred
  on a typical slope, seed 42's forest at a base of 1000 m grew from 26.6% to 34.5% of the world, in closed stands with clearings.
- **The kernels, not Oren–Nayar, by default**, though the two differ by only 3–5% on the forest's mean luminance: the kernels
  are what remote sensing fits to real canopies (and their white-sky albedos, a CI test, are the published ones), Oren–Nayar's is a
  facet model of rough ground. The difference is largest looking toward a moderate sun; under a low sun the valley forest is in the
  ridges' shadow, lit by the sky alone, and the two agree.
- **Domes, not cones; 2 m, not 3.6 m.** Pointed crowns 6 m apart and 3.6 m high (curvature 43 in lattice units) took a forested crest
  2 km off at 4K to 77.8 M triangles (34.7 M without forest), past the 48 M one acceleration structure takes; domes 8 m apart and 2 m
  high take it to 38.2 M (after the tuning below). The canopy's own height above the ground is not in the mesh either: its edge would be a 20 m wall, and the
  weights' bound (an amplitude times how fast its weight changes) would refine every forest edge in the world.
- **The crowns fade out at the grid's edge.** Without it, the forest weights of the grid's edge cells carried on to the horizon and the
  far field was refined as forest (the rock-face view's far field 3.5 M → 8.1 M triangles).

*M1.12b gotchas:*

- **The world sits high** (§11): since M1.11d's uplift the eroded seed-42 world's lowest ground stands 391 m above height 0, so at
  Zermatt's valley floor (1608 m) its valleys lie near 2000 m and only 3.2% of it is below the tree line. The default views barely
  change; the sheets show the world at a base of 1000 m too.
- **A preview's sidecar is `<out>.json`**: a camera file named like the render's output is overwritten by its sidecar.
- **The kernels' clamp is not their domain:** with a zenith held at 70° the formulas take a phase angle the clamped zeniths cannot
  have; harmless (the factor stays finite and positive), but a C# twin compared with unclamped kernels would differ there.
- **`--sheet-from` labels are comma-separated**: a label with a comma splits into two.

*Known limits:*

- **The forest is a material**: close up it is rounded domes, not trees (M5's, which keeps this beyond the distance its trees take
  over). The canopy's height above the ground is not in the mesh, so a forest edge has no wall of trunks, and a forested ridge's
  skyline is the ground's plus the crowns' 2 m relief.
- **One canopy everywhere**: conifer, in July. No larch turning gold, no broadleaf below the conifers, no krummholz above them.
- **No snow yet** (M1.13): the user's April photos show snow lying on the open ground above and among the forest.
- **The forest's bump is the first surface's only**, as M1.12's.
- **Valley floors stay forest where no water stood**: real Alpine floors are farmland and towns (not modelled).
