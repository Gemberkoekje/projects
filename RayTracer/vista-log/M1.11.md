# Vista M1.11 — Linked sliders & the weather chain

The slice's goal, deliverable and verify are in [`vista-plan.md`](../vista-plan.md) §10, with a status line that links here.
Section references (§n) are to the plan; "the cost ledger" is [`cost-ledger.md`](cost-ledger.md); sheets cited by name are in
[`vista-views/sheets/`](../vista-views/sheets/README.md).

*M1.11 status (2026-10-21): done.* Every deliverable and check is met; nothing in `RayTracer.Core` / `RayTracer.Gpu` changed, nor the
integrator's shaders. `Vista.Tests` has 669 tests (613 before); `--gpu-selftest` passes (54 checks, none new); the watch check grew two
steps. The look changes in two ways, both from the defaults: **golden hour is now the setting sun at +2°** (the measurement views'
golden hour drops from 8.5° to 2.3° apparent), which the height sheet picked over the +4° the plan expected; and **the haze follows the
day's humidity** (Zermatt's July gives optical depth 0.119 against M1.3's fixed 0.1, a visibility of 84 km). Two judgements are left for
the review: the +2° itself (the sheet is `m1-11-golden-hour-heights.png`), and that humidity alone moves the haze only a little through the
year (79–84 km at Zermatt) and in the opposite sense to a real alpine summer's haze (below).

*What was built:*

- **The linked sliders** (`Vista.Core/Sliders/`): ten sliders, each **auto** or **pinned** — basic: season, the time-of-day preset, snow
  (a snowline), fog/haze (a visibility); advanced: location (a place or a custom latitude), altitude, date, the solar time (pinned by the
  clock or by the sun's height), temperature (°C; °F on request) and humidity. Two pairs are coarse and fine: season ⇄ date (a pinned date
  decides the auto season, a pinned or default season the auto date) and preset → solar time. `LinkedSliders.Resolve` runs the chain in
  order, place → altitude → season ⇄ date → preset → solar time → the climate's day → temperature → humidity → freezing level → snow, the
  day's humidity → haze; an auto slider follows what is upstream of it, a pinned one keeps its value and stops only itself. `SliderPins.Reset`
  re-links one. **Set pieces push defaults** onto auto sliders (`SliderPushes`; only the frozen lake so far: winter, and the valley at most
  −5 °C). **Contradictions** render as asked with an "implausible" hint in the warnings: a frozen lake with a pinned temperature over +2 °C
  or a pinned summer, a pinned season the pinned date contradicts, and seasonal snow more than 1500 m under the freezing level when the
  snowline or the temperature is pinned.
- **The slider file** (`*.sliders.json`, `--sliders`, schema 1): only the pins; a slider it does not name is auto. The slider options
  (`--location`, `--latitude`, `--season`, `--date`, `--time`, `--sun-elevation`, `--altitude`, and new `--temperature` (°C, or °F with
  an `F`), `--humidity`, `--snowline`, `--visibility`) pin on top of it. A recipe keeps the place and time fields it held since M1.2,
  which pin the same sliders, but a preview takes its pins from the recipe or from a slider file, never both (an error says so). The
  watch reads the slider file as a third input; a sheet varies any slider (`--vary sunElevation=2,4,6`, `--vary date=…`) or whole slider
  files (`--vary sliders=a.json,b.json`).
- **The weather chain** (`Vista.Core/Weather/`): the **climate normals** of the eleven places, monthly mean daily lows and highs for
  1991–2020 entered by hand with a source each (`ClimateTable`), interpolated from mid-month to the day and moved from the station to the
  scene's valley floor along 6.5 °C per km; a custom latitude uses North, Cahalan & Coakley's (1981) zonal-mean fit (`LatitudeClimate`). The
  **daily curve** puts the low at sunrise and the high at 14:00 solar, two half cosines (`DailyTemperature`, as the WAVE crop model). The
  **humidity**: the dew point is the day's low (FAO-56's rule where humidity was not measured) and the Magnus form (Alduchov & Eskridge
  1996) turns it and the temperature into a relative humidity (`Humidity`). The **freezing level** is the valley floor plus the temperature
  over the lapse rate. The **snow**: a degree-day snowpack run over three years of the climate's days at every altitude (3 mm of water a day,
  falling as snow at a daily mean of 1 °C or less, melting 4 mm a degree above 0 °C), the snowline being the lowest altitude holding more
  than 10 mm on the date (`SnowCover`); a temperature off the climate's at the hour (pinned or pushed) moves it by half of the freezing
  level's move. The **haze**: aerosol extinction grows with humidity as (1 − RH)^−0.4, scaled so 50% is M1.3's clear day (0.1), capped at
  95% (fog is M4's), from the **day's** humidity (at the day's mean temperature); a pinned humidity grows it instead, and a pinned
  visibility (the WMO's meteorological optical range, ln 20 over the extinction at 550 nm) sets it (`Haze`).
- **Time-of-day presets by sun height** (`Almanac`): dawn the rising sun at −4°, golden hour the setting sun at +2°, dusk the setting sun at
  −4° (geometric), day and night solar noon and midnight. Where the sun never reaches a preset's height that day it takes its nearest
  approach, noon or midnight, with a hint (M1.2's polar fallbacks, now for every height). A pinned `sunElevation` replaces the preset's height.
- **The sidecar, schema 12:** `sliders` — the slider file, where the pins came from, the pins, every slider's value, whether it was pinned
  and what an auto value followed (`values`), the chain's working (`weather`: the station, its period and source, the day's low and high at
  the station and at the valley floor, the climate's temperature at the hour, the dew point, the humidity the haze grew by, the freezing
  level, the climate's snowline, the haze's optical depth, and the models' constants), and the implausible hints. The console prints a
  `weather:` line. Sheet labels name pinned sliders, and the time of day and the season always, so a before/after across builds compares an
  older recipe's fields with a newer slider file's pins as one key.

*M1.11 decisions (revisit freely):*

- **The pins live in a slider file**, beside the recipe (what the world is made of: set pieces) and the camera, as the plan's M1.8 and M1.16
  entries expected; changing it never starts a new scene. A recipe keeps the place and time fields it has held since M1.2 (they pin the same
  sliders, so every existing recipe, view and test reads as before), but never beside a slider file. The recipe's schema stays 4: its file
  format did not change (its time and season became optional, auto when absent, which renders as before for every set piece but the frozen
  lake). A schema 5 that refused those fields broke every recipe written without a `schema` field, which counts as the current one.
- **Ten sliders, two of them pairs.** The plan's basic "season" and "time of day" are the coarse ends of season ⇄ date and preset → solar
  time, so a preset is a slider of its own that a set piece can push, and the fine slider follows it until pinned.
- **The snow slider is a snowline**, metres above sea level, where seasonal snow begins (12 000 m: none); the recipe's glaciers stay
  whatever it is (§4.5). M1.13 places snow against it and the freezing level, both in the sidecar.
- **The fog/haze slider is the visibility at the valley floor**, in kilometres, the meteorological optical range weather reports give
  (WMO-No. 8: ln 20 over the extinction); the slider's unit for fog too (M4).
- **The haze follows the day's humidity**, not the hour's. The hour's humidity runs from saturated at dawn (the dew point is the day's low)
  to dry in mid-afternoon, and a haze that followed it made every time-of-day change rebuild every air table (the session test caught it;
  by M1.8's timings about 1 s more a change, the scattering orders' cost); the haze layer (1.2 km deep) sees the day's air, and a saturated dawn at the ground is dew and fog, M4's. So the
  time of day stays a fast change (§3.4), as M1.8 built it. The humidity slider still shows the hour's.
- **Snow is a degree-day snowpack** over the climate's year, so it lags the temperature as snow does (spring keeps snow below the freezing
  level, autumn's lies above it), from two numbers a literature range backs (snow at 1 °C, Jennings et al. 2018; 4 mm per degree-day,
  Hock 2003) and one that nothing backs, the same 3 mm of precipitation every day everywhere; temperature moves it by half the freezing
  level's move.
- **The haze's growth exponent is 0.4** (continental, rural and alpine air, 0.3–0.45 in Zieger et al. 2013; marine air is 0.55–0.7); its
  colour (Ångström exponent) stays 1.3, since measured growth hardly depends on the wavelength.
- **Stand-in stations:** Skrova (Lofoten; Reine has no max/min normals), Hjarðarland (Iceland; Þingvellir's record ended in 1983),
  Tulloch Bridge (the Highlands; the nearest to Glen Coe is a summit station) and Punta Arenas (Torres del Paine, 230 km away; Puerto Natales'
  30-year table is incomplete and inconsistent). The table's comment names each; spot checks of three rows against the published sources
  (Zermatt, Furano, Yosemite) matched to the digit.
- **Golden hour at +2°**, not the +4° the plan expected, by the sheet above; dawn and dusk at −4° as planned.
- **Implausible hints only where a pin or a push meets** another: auto values are the chain's own and never contradict themselves.

*M1.11 gotchas:*

- **A recipe without a `schema` field is the current schema** (the record's default): a schema bump that forbids fields breaks every
  hand-written recipe, the tests' among them. The recipe stayed at schema 4.
- **Records holding lists compare by reference:** two equal `Almanac`s are not `Equals` (their hints are two lists); compare the sun.
- **PowerShell's variables ignore case:** a loop's `$before` overwrote the `-Before` parameter (main's build) and `$a` the `-A` parameter, so
  the before/after ran this build against a PNG's path. Name locals apart from parameters.
- **Backticks inside a double-quoted bash string run as commands:** an inline Python edit of the README ran the render script with nonsense
  arguments (it refused them). Edit through a script file.
- **`--labels` is a comma list:** a label with a comma ("down-slope, July") splits into two and the tiling fails at the end of a long run.
- **The October down-slope view is in shadow at every golden-hour height** (the sun at 251–255° behind the ridge): useless for judging the
  light, so the sheet's fourth row is the ridges in October.
- **The haze from the hour's humidity** was the first build: the session test caught a time-of-day change rebuilding the air's tables (see
  the decision above).

*Known limits:*

- **Humidity alone moves the haze**, so at Zermatt it barely changes through the year (79–84 km) and, if anything, is hazier in winter, when the
  day's humidity is highest; a real alpine summer is hazier than its winter from aerosol (convection lifts the valleys' haze, winter
  inversions trap it below the peaks). An aerosol load by season, and inversions, are M4's (fog), if the review wants them sooner.
- **One precipitation for every place:** Fiordland's 6 m a year and Yellowstone's half metre snow alike, so maritime snowlines are too high
  and dry ones too low; the normals have no precipitation.
- **The dew point follows the same lapse rate as the air**, so a pinned altitude leaves the relative humidity almost unchanged (real dew
  points fall about 2 °C per km, not 6.5).
- **The snow and the freezing level are only recorded** until M1.13 renders them; the snow slider changes no pixel yet.
