# Vista M1.16 — Preview & tweak window

The slice's goal, deliverable and verify are in [`vista-plan.md`](../vista-plan.md) §10, with a status line that links here.
Section references (§n) are to the plan; "the cost ledger" is [`cost-ledger.md`](cost-ledger.md).

*M1.16 status (2026-10-28): done.* **`Vista.App --window` drives all of M1 by hand.** A WinForms window on one session that keeps its
stages, as a watch does: a progressive preview that restarts after each change and refines pass by pass; the set pieces (a new
scene, whose camera the auto camera chooses) and the seed; every linked slider, basic and advanced, showing its value, auto or pinned
(and where an auto value came from), a reset, and an implausible mark; the camera's nudges in metres, turns in degrees, focal length,
focus, aperture and exposure, never into the ground or the forest; the auto camera; the debug views and the denoiser; the estimate
and the 4K final with a live ETA. The scene is the CLI's own files, written before each run and saved where the user says, and every
run is the CLI's own command line, parsed by the CLI's parser. **Checked** by `--window-check`, which walks the real window through M1:
its converged preview was a cold CLI preview of the saved files byte for byte three times a run; on the kept stages the estimate took
18 s and said 16–24 s, and the 4K final ran 21.1 s; every step rebuilt only what it should. No image moved. **Found:** a change that
moves the air (the time of day, the date, the temperature) takes about 9.5 s in the window, 8.7 s of it rebuilding the air's tables
(parked for M1.17's performance pass).

*What was built:*

- **The window** (`Vista.App/Window/TweakWindow.cs`, `--window [--recipe name.recipe.json] [--seed n]`): the preview on the left with
  its size, samples and the CLI command that makes it, the controls in a column on the right, a status line that says what each run
  built and reused and where the sun stands. *Scene:* ten set-piece boxes (a conflicting piece swapped out: lake and frozen lake), the
  seed and "new seed". *Sliders:* season, time of day, snow and fog/haze; behind "Advanced" the location (the eleven places or a
  custom latitude), altitude, date, solar time, temperature (with "show °F"), humidity, valley haze and mist. Each row shows the
  value as the sliders resolve it (in the window, at once, as the run will), "auto" in green or "pinned" in blue, where an auto value
  came from (its tool tip), a reset, and "⚠ pinned" in orange when an implausible hint is about it; the hints sit under the sliders.
  *Camera:* where it stands and how high above the ground, nudges left, right, forward, back, up and down by 0.5 m to 500 m (1.5 m
  is five feet), pan, tilt and roll by 0.5° to 15°, focal length, focus, aperture and exposure, the auto camera. *View:* the beauty,
  the denoised beauty (Open Image Denoise on the GPU) or a debug image (albedo, normal, depth, material, tier, variance, detail), and
  the preview's size (320 to 960 px, 640 by default) and samples. *Final:* its size (1920 to 3840 px), samples, the denoiser, a noise
  target, its file; Estimate, Render final and Cancel; the estimate's range and the final's time against it. *File:* open and save a
  scene, copy the preview's command line, open the working folder.
- **The scene as the CLI's files** (`Vista.Core/Tweak/TweakScene.cs`, `TweakFiles.cs`): a `TweakScene` is the recipe (its set pieces
  and name, never pins of its own: an older recipe's are moved into the slider pins on loading, which the CLI renders alike), the seed,
  the camera (none for a new scene), the slider pins, and the preview's and the final's sizes. It is written as `name.recipe.json`,
  `name.camera.json`, `name.sliders.json` and `name.options.txt` (the seed and the preview's size, as `--watch --options` reads them),
  only the files that changed. A run's arguments are built from the scene and the files' paths and parsed by `PreviewCommandLine`
  itself, so the window's run is the CLI's: the window shows that command, and a final writes its own scene files beside its PNG, so
  its sidecar names files that make it again.
- **The runner** (`Vista.Core/Tweak/TweakRunner.cs`): one thread owns the session and its renderer and runs the window's jobs. The
  newest preview runs and stops a stale one (between stages or dispatches); the auto camera, the estimate and the final queue ahead of
  previews and only Cancel stops them; every job ends with exactly one result; the image a preview shows is read back from the file it
  wrote, before the next job can overwrite it. No GPU: every job says why, and the window stays open.
- **The progressive preview** (`Vista.Core/Rendering/TraceFeed.cs`, `Vista.Core/Cli/PreviewFeed.cs`): the planner carries a feed. It
  refuses its next batch once the feed is cancelled, and after each pass over the whole frame (every pixel the same samples) the D3D12
  host reads the sums back and hands the feed the image so far, at most every 0.2 s. The session meters the exposure first when a feed
  wants frames (the incident meter reads only the air's tables), so each frame is exposed and display-encoded as the PNG will be. The
  passes are the final's own planner's, so the last image is the render itself: a feed changes no byte of the files.
- **The camera's collision** (`Vista.Core/Tweak/CameraMoves.cs`): the eye stays at least eye height (1.7 m) above the world grid's
  surface, plus the most the ground's detail rises there (the rock's, from the run's sidecar), plus the forest's crowns below the tree
  line, and over the world's grid; a nudge below that floor is lifted to it, and the window says why ("lifted 999851.1 m, clear of the
  ground"). There is no water yet (M3), so nothing is underwater.
- **The estimate on the kept stages** (`PreviewSession.KeepsFinalStages`, `FinalEstimate.StillToBuild`): a window's estimate renders
  its noise preview before its probe, so the final's mesh, its acceleration structure and the air's tables are still the renderer's
  when the final starts, and its setup counts only what the final will still do (the pools, the memory plan, the renderer's own setup
  and readback). The session's `WorldCache` keeps two meshes (the preview's and the final's) instead of one. The memory plan's own time
  is now counted in every estimate's setup (`memoryPlan`), the CLI's too.
- **The implausible sliders** (`SliderState.ImplausibleSliders`): each implausible hint names the pins it is about (a date and a
  season that disagree, a warm pinned temperature beside a frozen lake, snow below the freezing level), so their rows are marked.
- **`--window-check`** (`Vista.App/Window/WindowCheck.cs`, `vista-views/window/check.ps1`): the walk below, with pictures of the window
  after each step and the slice's two sheets.

*M1.16 decisions (revisit freely):*

- **The files are the state.** The window holds a scene and writes it as the CLI's own files before each run; its runs are the CLI's
  command lines, parsed by the CLI's parser. Nothing the window renders exists only in the window, so its verify (a converged preview
  equal to a CLI preview of the saved files) holds by construction and is checked anyway.
- **Progressive by the final's own passes**, not a separate progressive mode: the planner already covers the frame in passes of equal
  samples, so the image after each pass is the render at fewer samples, and the last is the render. A pass is read back at most every
  0.2 s (a 640×360 preview at 64 spp traces in about 0.56 s, so it shows one or two frames).
- **Exposed before it traces** when frames are wanted: the incident meter reads the air's tables, not the image, so metering first
  changes no number; the frames then look as the PNG will. With the average meter (which needs the image) there are no frames.
- **The newest preview wins; explicit jobs are never stopped by a slider.** A preview stops a running preview, never an estimate, a
  final or the auto camera; those run first, in order. The window holds a change back until its controls have been still for 0.2 s, so
  a slider's drag is one run, not one a step.
- **A new scene's camera** is the auto camera's pick for the mountain set pieces (about 40 s, 15 s on a cached world) and the default
  camera otherwise; a slider moved while the auto camera chooses renders once the pick is in. The auto camera button re-chooses on the
  same world.
- **The window's preview is 640 px wide by default**, twice the CLI's: the window shows it two or three times larger, and 320 px read
  as a smear there. It traces in about 0.56 s at 64 spp (a nudge, with its mesh, 1.6 s); the options file records the width, so the
  CLI renders the same size.
- **The collision is conservative**: the floor adds the most the rock's detail rises anywhere and the forest's crowns anywhere below
  the tree line, not the detail under the camera, so the eye can stand a few metres higher than it must; never lower. The window's
  pictures and the CLI keep their own camera (the CLI only warns, as before).
- **Focus and aperture** are edited and saved (they are the camera file's), but there is no depth of field yet; their tool tips say so.
- **Denoising in the window on CUDA**, the CLI's CPU default unchanged (M1.6: the same error, 16–21× faster).
- **The window's estimate reorders, the CLI's does not**: M1.15 measured the CLI's estimate with its probe first; only a session that
  keeps the final's stages renders its noise preview first. Two kept meshes (not one) cost a preview's mesh in memory beside a 4K one.

*M1.16 gotchas:*

- **The mesh follows the camera's heading** (its frustum, since M1.9): a pan builds the mesh again, as a nudge does. `WorldCache`'s
  comment said otherwise; the check's first run expected it, and was wrong.
- **A change of the air costs about 9.5 s in the window**, nearly all of it the air's tables rebuilt (the incident meter builds them
  before the image, 8.7 s of each step), at half the GPU's time. Since M1.13a the auto valley haze follows the hour, so the time of
  day moves the tables, and M1.13b's tables are larger (10 orders). §3.4's "two speeds" puts the time of day with the camera, as a fast
  change; it is not one now (M1.13a found a pinned valley haze kept the tables over a change of time; not measured since M1.13b's
  pools). Parked for M1.17's performance pass.
- **`string.Create` with an interpolated string and a plain literal joined by `+`** does not compile (the handler needs every part
  interpolated): the literal needs its own `$`.
- **WinForms**: a public property on a control needs `[DesignerSerializationVisibility(Hidden)]` (WFO1000 is an error here); a form's
  method named `Handle` hides `Control.Handle`; a `CheckedListBox` in multi-column mode scrolls sideways and truncates (replaced by
  check boxes); file dialogs and the clipboard need an STA thread, so the window runs on its own; `DrawToBitmap` draws a window opened off
  the screen, so the check's window never takes the user's screen or focus.
- **A control the window sets must not feed back as an edit**: setting the focal length's box from an auto camera's 47.3 mm would round
  it and pin the camera to 47 mm; the window sets every control with its change events muted.
- **Rebuilding `Vista.App` while the check runs** fails or breaks it: the check spawns the built app for its cold CLI runs.

*Known limits:*

- **The air's changes are slow** (above); a slider dragged across the time of day renders once it is still, about 9.5 s later.
- **No depth of field** (focus and aperture are kept for when it comes), **no water** (nothing is underwater yet, M3).
- **A final shows no progressive image** (a 4K readback a pass would cost more than it shows); it shows its progress and live ETA.
- **One renderer, one acceleration structure**: after a final, the next preview builds its own again (with its trace, about 0.9 s at
  640 px).
- **The window's layout is fixed at 96 DPI scaled**: it scales by the monitor's DPI but has no layout for narrow screens.
