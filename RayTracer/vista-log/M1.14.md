*M1.14 status (2026-10-27): done.* **`--auto-camera` frames the mountain set pieces** without a camera placed by hand: the shot is
planted on the finished world (its main massif and peak, the range's axis, a heading toward the subject with the sun aside, a
viewpoint across from it), a few hundred candidates around it are scored from **composition buffers traced on the CPU** over the
height field (depth, normal, the ground's class, its height above the valley floor), after what the user's paintings do (clear
layers, a framing mass at one edge, the peak near a third, low ground below, a diagonal line) and §4.8 (the skyline near a third,
the sky's share, side or back light, the peaks in frame), plus the subject catching the sun; the best ten are **rendered small for
the squint test and the values by layer**, and the best that keeps every limit is the pick: the near field until M5 (2% of the frame,
snow excepted), the lens, and its 4K mesh 15% under the 48 M ceiling. On eight seeds × both set pieces at golden hour every pick
frames a peak or a skyline against the sky where the default camera mostly faces a dark slope, its near field at 0%, its mesh 14–39 M
triangles, in about 40 s; the CPU buffers agree with the renders'; the same pick twice; no other image moved. **Limits:** most picks
stand 40–150 m above the ground (the near-field limit), the subject is lit only where the plant's heading allows, and the shadows
are the display's (M1.17).

*What was built:*

- **The composition buffer on the CPU** (`Vista.Core/Composition/TerrainSight.cs`, `CompositionBuffer.cs`): a tracer over the eroded
  world grid and the far field's base terrain around it (the pools' 128 m grid out to 65 km), bent onto the Earth as the renderer's
  mesh is (a point `s` along the ground sinks by `s²/2R`), that traces a camera's buffer at a low resolution: per pixel the depth,
  the normal, the ground's class (`GroundClass`: sky, rock, scree, meadow, forest, snow; the scene's material rules at the hit,
  `MaterialClassifier`) and how high the ground stands above its valley floor (the pool map's floors, M1.13b). A ray steps half its
  clearance (at least 1 m, and 0.05% of its distance), then bisects the crossing; past the far field it ends on ground when it is
  still below the highest terrain. The rays are the integrator's, through each pixel's centre (`CameraRays`). The finalists' buffers
  come from the renderer's own depth, normal and material-id outputs (`CompositionBuffer.FromRender`).
- **The scores** (`CompositionScorer.cs`, `ValueStructure.cs`), each 0 to 1, after the user's paintings and §4.8:
  - *Sky*: the sky's share near 35%. *Horizon*: the skyline's mean row near the upper third.
  - *Peak* (snow-capped mountains): the highest summit in frame off-centre near a third, 20% down from the top (never cut by the
    edge), standing above the rest of the skyline (15% of the height scores fully), white just under the skyline; the planted main
    peak itself earns 15% more. *Range* (an alpine range): summits along the skyline (each 3% of the height above its saddles, four
    score fully), the range across the frame (the skyline's ground at least half as far as the planted subject), the highest summit
    placed as a peak's.
  - *Layers*: ridges in front of farther ground (a step up a column to ground 1.6 times as far is a silhouette; two make three
    layers), the ground's depths in three groups (1-D k-means on log depth) each at least 8% of it, the nearest lowest in the frame.
  - *Framing*: the nearest group (or ground nearer than 30% of the median) covering a good part of the columns at one edge, little
    of the middle two fifths, less of the other edge.
  - *Lower third*: low ground (under 60 m above its valley floor, farther than 250 m) in the lower third, where water will lie (M3).
  - *Leading line*: the share of the edges below the skyline (Sobel on log depth and on the normal's steepness) running 20°–70°.
  - *Sun*: side light (60°–120° from the heading) fully, back light from 40° 0.85 while the sun stays out of the frame, flat light
    with the sun behind the camera 0.4, the disk in the frame 0.1; the higher the sun the less it counts (a quarter from 50°).
  - *Light*: the subject catching the sun: of the ground in the band just under the skyline (12% of the height) around the summit
    (the whole skyline for a range), the share facing the sun by at least 6° with no terrain toward it within 30 km (a shadow ray on
    the CPU, `TerrainSight.Lit`); half of it lit scores fully. None with the sun below the horizon.
  - *Squint* (finalists): the image's display values box-averaged to 32 pixels across and blurred, in three groups (k-means): how
    well they separate (0.85 nothing, 0.97 fully; an even spread is 0.89) times their spread (half the range fully).
  - *Values* (finalists): the background lighter than the middle ground lighter than the foreground, each step fully at 0.1.
  - Weights (`CompositionWeights`): peak 0.20, light 0.18, layers 0.16, squint 0.10, horizon 0.08, framing 0.08, sun 0.08, values
    0.08, sky 0.06, lower third 0.06, leading line 0.06; the total is their weighted mean over the scores there are.
- **The limits** (`CompositionLimits`), which rank a candidate below every one that keeps them: the near field until M5 (ground but
  snow nearer than 100 m on at most 2% of the frame), the lens (ground nearer than 25 m on at most 0.2%), and the pick's 4K mesh at
  most 15% under the 48 M triangles one acceleration structure takes (40.8 M).
- **The plant** (`ShotPlanter.cs`): the world on a 128 m grid smoothed over 1.5 km; its highest point in the world's inner 64% is the
  massif, the highest sample within 2.5 km of it the main peak, the 10th percentile within 8 km the floor, and the high ground's long
  axis the range's. Headings: for a peak the sun 40°, 60°, 90° or 135° to either side; for a range broadside to the axis and 20° and
  40° off it. Each is tried at its distance (a peak four times its relief, 4–12 km; a range five times, and enough for its length in a
  35 mm frame, 6–16 km; shortened by a fifth at a time to stand 1 km inside the world), 60 m up, where it sees the main peak, framed
  and scored at 96 px: the best is the plant.
- **The search** (`AutoCamera.cs`): places on rings around the viewpoint (out to 0.6 of its distance: 1 + 6 + 10 + 14 + 18), two on
  the line to the subject (at 0.65 and 1.4 times the distance) and, when the pools stand, up to six shoulders 10–120 m above a pool
  at least 30 m deep (M1.13a's painters' place); each at a person's eye, 40 m and 150 m up (shoulders: eye and 25 m), lifted clear of
  the ground (`Collide`: the eye 1.7 m above the highest ground within 5 m), kept where it sees the main peak; at each place the two
  focal lengths (35–135 mm for a peak, 24–70 mm for a range) whose frame suits the subject's size there (a peak's relief half the
  frame's height, a range's length three quarters of its width), the subject on the left or the right third, the summit 18% or 30%
  down. Every candidate's buffer is traced at 128 px and scored; the best ten that keep the limits, at most two at a place, are the
  finalists.
- **The finalists** (`PreviewSession.ChooseCamera`): each rendered at 192×108, 16 samples, its own mesh, exposed by the incident
  meter and display-encoded as the preview is; its buffer from the render rescored, the squint test and the values added; then, best
  first, each one's 4K mesh counted (`ViewMesher.CountTriangles`: the refinement alone) until one keeps the ceiling. The best that
  keeps every limit is the pick.
- **The command line and the sidecar**: `--auto-camera [--finalists n]` (0: the buffers' best wins; a dry run renders none), against
  `--camera`; the pick written as `<preview>.camera.json`; the sidecar (schema 24) records the plant, the settings, weights and limits,
  the candidates and how many kept the limits, the pick and the finalists with every score, the ten best by their buffers and each
  stage's seconds.
- **The checks' tools**: `vista-views/autocam/render.ps1` (the sheets below) and `--gpu-selftest`'s new check of the CPU buffer against
  the render's outputs.

*M1.14 decisions (revisit freely):*

- **The candidates' buffers are traced on the CPU, not rendered**: the plan's "low-resolution G-buffer renders" of a few hundred
  candidates would each need their own view mesh and acceleration structure (the mesh follows the camera's place), and would hold the
  user's GPU for minutes. Over the height field the CPU traces them all in about a second, anywhere (a dry run, CI), with the GPU free.
  The finalists are rendered, for what only light shows (the squint test, the values by layer), and their own buffers rescored; the
  two agree closely (the checks below).
- **The subject must catch the light** (added after the first sheet): the plan's "side or back light" put half the picks' faces in
  shadow at golden hour (seeds 2, 3, 5 and 7 planted with the sun 40°–60° off the heading: back light, the faces toward the camera
  dark, the picture's focal point black under the display's punchy shadows). The light score asks the ground just under the summit to
  be in the sun, at 0.18 against the sun score's 0.08 (0.12 before): a low sun now lights the subject from the side or behind the
  camera's shoulder. The sun score keeps its side and back light; it only weighs less.
- **The peak is the frame's highest summit, whichever it is** (the gotcha below); the planted main peak earns 15% more.
- **The camera may stand above the ground**: at a person's eye, 40 m and 150 m (a vantage over the near slope, a drone). Until M5
  the near-field limit keeps the ground within 100 m out of the frame, and from eye height that leaves only the edges of drops, so
  most picks stand 40–150 m up; the paintings' lake-shore viewpoints come with M3's water and M5's near field.
- **Both set pieces make the same world** (`TerrainProfile.Mountains`); what differs is the intent: snow-capped mountains frame one
  dominant peak (35–135 mm, its relief half the frame), an alpine range a skyline of summits across the frame (24–70 mm, the range's
  length three quarters of the width).
- **The limits' values**: the near field 2% of the frame (about 180 pixels of a 128 px buffer; snow excepted, M1.13), the lens 0.2%,
  the triangles 15% under the 48 M ceiling (40.8 M, against M1.12b's 38.2 M largest 4K view).
- **The triangles are counted for the finalists in rank order, until one keeps the ceiling**: a 4K count takes 4–5 s, and the best
  finalist usually keeps it.
- **The pick is a camera file** (`<preview>.camera.json`), and the camera options (a sheet's axes) apply on top of it, so a pick can be
  rendered again, at 4K, or nudged.

*M1.14 gotchas:*

- **The planted main peak is seldom the highest summit in frame**: from most places a nearer summit of the same massif stands as
  high or higher, so a peak score that asked for the planted one scored nearly every finalist near 0 (seed 42: 0.00–0.31). The score
  takes the frame's highest summit, whichever it is, and gives the planted one 15% more.
- **Counting a 4K mesh's triangles by building it took 6.5 s a finalist** (65 s for ten): the refinement with its vertex tables and
  index lists. `ViewMesher.CountTriangles` runs the refinement alone, and only the finalists in rank order are counted, until one keeps
  the ceiling (usually the first): 4–5 s a pick.
- **PowerShell 5.1 formats numbers in the machine's culture**: on this Dutch Windows `{0:0.00}` writes `0,88`, and the sheet's
  comma-separated labels split there ("30 labels for 10 images"). The render script sets the invariant culture first.
- **PowerShell 5.1 reads a script without a byte-order mark as ANSI**: a `Get-Content -Raw` edit of `render.ps1` turned its `·` into
  `Â·`, which the sheet font draws as a box. The script is saved with a BOM, like the others.
- **`powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass` is refused by the session's permission classifier**: the machine's policy already lets local
  scripts run (RemoteSigned for the user), so `& vista-views/autocam/render.ps1` or `powershell -File` does it.

*Known limits:*

- **The camera stands high**: fifteen of the sixteen picks are taken from 40 or 150 m above the ground. From a person's eye the
  ground at one's feet fills the lower frame, which the near-field limit keeps out until M5; only the edges of drops qualify (seed 7's
  peak). The paintings' lake-shore viewpoints need M3's water and M5's near field.
- **The plant fixes the light**: the candidates stand around one heading, so where the plant's heading puts the subject against the
  sun (seeds 3 and 5 at golden hour, the sun 60° and 40° off), the picks find a lit crest at the frame's edge rather than a lit face
  (their peak score 0).
- **Snow is let through the near field**: the plan's exemption for snow lets a pick put a big smooth snow surface in its near corner
  (seed 4's peak), which close up has no detail yet (M7).
- **The scores are a first set of weights**: §11 expects composition scoring to be tuned heavily; the weights and limits are in the
  sidecar, and the finalists' sheet shows how one run ranked.
- **One golden hour**: the sheets are all a July evening at Zermatt; dawn, day and the other places were not judged.

## The follow-up: a detail budget (2026-10-27)

*Status (2026-10-27): done.* The user's question of 2026-10-27, the nearest faces lacking detail, was answered two ways (vista-plan
§10, the follow-up's entry): the mid-distance detail itself is a slice of its own before M11, and until it exists **the auto camera
keeps frames whose lit bare ground the final frame shows finer than the terrain carries out of its picks.** For every pixel of ground
on the world grid it compares the 8 m grid's cell with the ground one final-frame pixel covers (the depth times the pixel's angle at
3840×2160): where the cell spans more than **12 pixels** and the sun lights the ground, rock, scree and snow count; a frame where they
cover more than **5%** breaks the new limit and ranks below every frame that keeps it. On M1.14's seed-42 finalists it breaks exactly the
four with big lit slopes in a corner (6, 7, 9 and 10: 6.2–14.5% of the frame) and passes the others (0–1.7%); of the sixteen seed picks
it changes three (seed 4's and seed 6's peaks, seed 1's range: 26.2%, 15.4% and 27.8% before), and every pick now stands at 0–4.8%.
The limit's two numbers are the user's to judge on the sheets (`m1-14-budget.png`, `m1-14-budget-changed.png`).

*What was built:*

- **The measure** (`CompositionScorer.Detail`, `DetailMap`): per ground pixel, the cells' span in final-frame pixels
  (`ITerrainProbe.DetailMetres` over the depth times `2·tan(fov/2)/FinalFrameHeight`), kept where the sun lights the ground (the light
  score's shadow ray, `TerrainSight.Lit`); beyond the world grid (the far field, softened by the haze) and with the sun down it looks at
  nothing. `CompositionScores.DetailShare` is the share of the frame that counts; `CompositionLimits.DetailCellPixels` (12),
  `DetailMaxShare` (0.05) and `DetailClasses` (rock, scree, snow) set it, and `FinalFrameWidth`/`Height` (renamed from the triangle
  count's frame, which they serve too) the final frame: an 8K target halves the metres a pixel covers and tightens the budget on its own.
- **`--debug detail`**: the beauty with the ground that counts tinted magenta and lit meadow and forest past the threshold yellow; the
  sidecar's `detail` (schema 25) holds the share, whether it breaks, the shares at 2–16 pixels a cell and the share by class.
- **`--detail-budget on | off`** (with `--auto-camera`): off measures the budget without ranking by it, M1.14's search as it was.
- **The script's new sheets** (`autocam/render.ps1`): `budget` (a run with the budget off, its finalists with the budget's image) and
  `changed` (with `-Before`, the picks this run's seeds changed, before and after).

*Decisions (revisit freely):*

- **12 pixels a cell and 5% of the frame**, from the calibration: the smallest budget that ranks down exactly the frames the plan named
  (finalists 6, 7, 9 and 10, the snow dome) while leaving thirteen of M1.14's sixteen picks. 12 pixels of an 8 m cell is about 3 km at
  50 mm and 4K, the band the user named ("100 m to 3 km"); at 6, ten picks would go, among them most of the lit golden faces. The
  user judges both numbers by eye on the sheets.
- **Snow counts**, beside the rock and scree the plan names: the plan's verify asks for seed 4's snow dome to rank down, and a lit snow
  slope close up shows no more relief than lit rock (its close-up detail is M7's). Meadow and forest do not: M5 replaces them, and the
  sheet shows them yellow so their share can be seen.
- **Metres per pixel along the ray, not on the slope**: the plan's measure, the depth times the pixel's angle. A face seen edge-on packs
  its cells tighter, so this counts it a little more than it shows; the faces that matter fill the frame face-on.
- **The far field is never counted**: its base terrain lies 16 km and more away under the haze; counted, every long lens on a distant
  lit range would break the budget.
- **A limit, not a weight**: the plan's "keeps such frames out of its picks", like the near field.

*Gotchas:*

- **A sheet's ranking must be reproducible without the old build**: the follow-up changes which finalists M1.14's search ranks, so the
  budget sheet runs the search with `--detail-budget off`, which ranks exactly as M1.14 did, rather than reading an old run's files.
- **`& script.ps1 -Sheets a,b` passes an array**: PowerShell binds the unquoted list as two strings, which the `[string]` parameter
  joins with a space, so no sheet matches; quote it (`-Sheets 'a,b'`) or call the script through `powershell -File`.
