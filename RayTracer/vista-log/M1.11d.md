# Vista M1.11d — Uplift & rock hardness

The slice's goal, deliverable and verify are in [`vista-plan.md`](../vista-plan.md) §10, with a status line that links here.
Section references (§n) are to the plan; "the cost ledger" is [`cost-ledger.md`](cost-ledger.md); sheets cited by name are in
[`vista-views/sheets/`](../vista-views/sheets/README.md).

*M1.11d status (2026-10-22): done.* The incision now lifts the ground by an **uplift map** (the base terrain's lowest octave, to the
power 1.5, up to 8 m a step) instead of in proportion to the base terrain's own height, and the rock has **strata**: seeded beds of
hard and soft rock whose hardness sets the erodibility and the talus slope in both the incision and the GPU erosion. From the aerial
camera the four seeds show massifs with lower, broader ground between them where M1.11a showed an even field of similar peaks
(`m1-11d-seeds.png`), and the talus facets are gone: on the eroded world the most crowded ±2° window of slopes holds 8.6–10.8% of
them, where M1.11a's held 36.4–39.0% (all at 51°, its 45° talus stretched by the mapping back). The local relief at 1 km spreads more
(its 90th over its 10th percentile 2.31–3.23 against 1.90–2.29); at 5 km it spreads less (1.50–1.65 against 1.81–2.46), back toward
the base terrain's 1.37–1.51, with a higher median on three seeds. The drainage holds: the longest lake-free channel runs 34–52 km
(M1.11a: 38–51) and 96.0–97.5% of the grid drains into a channel of 1 km² (97.6–98.5%). The default and down-slope views are
re-placed 3 m above rock (the default facing the range, down the slope a lit face falling toward the golden-hour sun), the sky view
moved with the default. **With M1.11a's settings this build makes `main`'s four worlds bit for bit**, through the new shader and
strata paths. `Vista.Tests` has 717 tests (706 before); `--gpu-selftest` passes (55 checks, the erosion parity now with the rock's
strata).

*What was built:*

- **The uplift map** (`Vista.Core/World/UpliftMap.cs`): the incision's uplift per step at a place is `IncisionUplift` (8 m) times the
  base terrain's large-scale shape (`TerrainProfile.Shape`: its first `UpliftOctaves` octaves, here the 6 km one, under the same warp
  and rotations, normalised by their own weights to [0, 1]) raised to `UpliftContrast` (1.5, done as `s·√s` so no `Math.Pow` moves its
  bits). A pure function of the seed and the position, 0 on the grid's edge (the base level). In the steady state of uplift and
  incision a river's slope grows with the uplift, so where the map is high the valleys stand high and the massifs rise; where it is
  low the ground is cut down toward the base level. `UpliftOctaves = 0` is M1.11a's uplift (in proportion to the base terrain's own
  height on the incision grid), kept so this build can make M1.11a's world for the before measures.
- **The rock** (`Vista.Core/World/RockHardness.cs`):
  - `RockHardness`: beds stacked on a stratigraphic height σ, each with its own thickness (a quarter to two and a half times
    `StrataThickness`, 60 m; thin beds more common) and hardness (uniform, pushed toward hard or soft by a smoothstep), from the
    seed's own stream (`"terrain.strata"`), tabulated every 2 m over ±16 km (`TableEntries`, interpolated); a regional dip (a seeded
    direction as a rational point on the circle, a slope up to `StrataDipDegrees`, 8°) and a slow fold (120 m at 9 km) tilt the beds:
    σ = y + `Fold(x, z)`; a lateral `Mottle` (two octaves, ±`HardnessMottle`, 0.08) shifts a bed's hardness from place to place.
    Hardness = clamp(bed(σ) + mottle, 0, 1).
  - `StrataFrame` (Low, Bottom, Scale, UpliftSteps): the field is defined where the rock was when the incision began. The uplift
    carries the rock up with the ground and the incision maps its heights back to the base terrain's range, so the rock now at height
    y at (x, z) began at `(y − Low) / Scale + Bottom − UpliftSteps·U(x, z)`; a world without an incision has the identity frame.
  - `StrataGrid`: the field laid out on a grid as the GPU reads it, per cell the bed table's coordinate at height 0 and the mottle,
    so the hardness of the rock at height y is one multiply-add and one interpolated table read: `Of(table, y·TableScale + Offset[c],
    Mottle[c])`.
- **The incision with uplift and rock** (`StreamPower.Incise`): each step lifts every cell by the uplift map, and the rock at a
  cell's surface (its height less the uplift so far, in the rock's frame) sets that cell's erodibility, `IncisionRate·(1 + (0.3 − 1)·h)`
  (`HardErodibility` 0.3), and its talus slope toward its receiver, the tangent from tan 35° (`IncisionTalusDegrees`) to tan 60°
  (`IncisionTalusHardDegrees`) with the hardness. It returns the frame its mapping back left the rock in; `Coarse` returns the uplift
  field it used, which `WorldBuilder.Prepare` interpolates for the frame on the world grid.
- **The GPU erosion reads the rock** (`Shaders/VistaErosion.hlsl`, mirrored by `ErosionReference`): two new buffers, `Strata` (u10,
  per cell offset and mottle) and `Bands` (u11, the bed table), and per pass the hardness of the rock at its current height:
  `CSWater` erodes rock at `RockErodibility` lerped to `RockErodibility·HardErodibility`, `CSThermalOut` lets rock stand to the drop
  lerped from tan 40° (`RockTalusDegrees`) to tan 65° (`RockTalusHardDegrees`). The constants grew to eight 16-byte rows (128 bytes;
  `RockErodibilityHard`, `RockDropEdgeHard`, `RockDropCornerHard`, `TableScale`, `TableLast`, padding). An `ErosionState` without
  strata has every rock the softest; with `HardErodibility` 1 and one talus angle the hardness changes no bit (a test).
- **The world keeps its rock** (`ErosionResult.Hardness`, `.Strata`): the hardness of the bedrock at its surface (under any loose
  material), in half precision, and the frame; both in the world's hash and in the cache (format 3: the frame in the header, the
  hardness after the drainage, 285 MB a world against 252; `PassesVersion` 3). The sidecar (schema 16) records the new settings and
  `world.erosion.strata`. A dry run writes `<out>.hardness.png` beside the drainage image (`DebugImages.HardnessImage`: soft rust,
  hard blue-grey, over a hillshade).
- **The measures** (`Vista.Core/World/ReliefStats.cs`): the local relief (highest minus lowest) in squares of 1 km and 5 km around
  points 250 m apart, the highest point of each 1 km square (the summits), each as 10th / 50th / 90th percentiles; the slopes at 8 m and
  at 64 m (central differences), with the share in the most crowded ±2° window from 20° up (the facet measure: a terrain of talus
  planes piles its slopes there) and, given each cell's talus angle, the share within ±2° of it. `--erosion-measure` prints them for the
  coarse grid before and after the incision, the base terrain and the eroded world, with the hardness's spread; writes the uplift map
  and the hardness map beside the other images; and keeps the world it eroded in the cache (`VISTA_WORLD_CACHE` moves it), so a render
  after it reads the same world.
- **The views re-placed** (`vista-views/default.camera.json`, `down-slope.camera.json`, `sky.camera.json`): the default stands 3 m
  above rock at (2625, −4375), 1607.4 m, looking 20° west of north at the range (sky 46%, the range 1.6–3.9 km away); down the slope
  stands 3 m above a lit 37° face at (−2850, 0), 1900.8 m, looking down it at −30° toward the golden-hour sun (azimuth −98°, every
  pixel terrain); the sky view stands at the default's spot pitched up 45° (M0's spot is 83 m underground on this world). Both were
  chosen by a search over the eroded seed-42 world (sight lines and sun-lit share, scripted outside the repo) where the GPU erosion
  moved the ground less than a metre, so CI's check over the incised ground (2.5–2.7 m clear) holds. `--gpu-selftest` renders from the
  new default pose.

*M1.11d decisions (revisit freely):*

- **The uplift map is the base terrain's first octave, to the power 1.5**, chosen by the sheets above: one octave makes massifs (two
  make ridges), and 1.5 keeps relief on the lower ground where 2 or 3 flattens it into plains.
- **The incision stays from the base terrain, with its steady state pulled up by uplift**, rather than from flat (M1.11a's
  decision stands); the uplift is strong enough (8 m a step at the map's top) that the incised range is 1.3–1.5 times the base
  terrain's, so the mapping back now compresses it (by 0.67–0.78) where M1.11a's stretched it (by 1.13–1.19).
- **Hardness is strata in the rock's frame**, a table of beds a lookup reads, not a per-cell map frozen at the start: the GPU
  erosion reads the bed at each cell's current rock height, so a soft bed cut through exposes the hard one below; and the materials
  can find the same bed at any height from the frame (M1.12).
- **One hardness drives both stages**, with each stage's own talus range (the incision's on 64 m cells, the erosion's on 8 m) and
  one erodibility ratio. Hard beds hold up to 60° / 65°, soft weather back to 35° / 40°.
- **The mapping back to the base terrain's range stays**, so no view rises or sinks by hundreds of metres from a settings change;
  the frame records it, so the rock follows.
- **M1.11a's uplift stays reachable** (`UpliftOctaves = 0`), so the before measures come from one build and are checked to be `main`'s
  worlds; neutral hardness is a setting (`HardErodibility = 1`, one talus angle each), not a code path.
- **The near-ground views stand 3 m above rock the GPU erosion barely moves**, so CI's check over the incised ground (which it can
  compute) means something for them; the self-test checks them over the eroded ground. The base terrain may rise above them
  (a dry run without a cached world warns), so CI's base-terrain check covers only the high views.

*M1.11d gotchas:*

- **The incision's talus holds only toward a cell's receiver.** A peak with soft rock on one side drops to that side's talus, and
  then its hard flank is cut from the summit down, not left standing: a cone half hard, half soft does not keep a steep hard side
  (the first test's design). Test talus angles on a ramp whose fixed edges also keep the range from being stretched.
- **The mapping back stretches every test shape alike**: a uniform cone incised at 30° and at 60° comes back as the same cone. Keep
  the highest point and the outlet fixed, or compare within one run.
- **The constant buffer must be whole 16-byte rows**: five new floats needed three of padding (`CheckConstantLayouts` throws on
  first use, not at build).
- **Uplift large enough to matter makes every hillslope a talus plane**: hillslope cells only rise and are clamped; it is the
  hardness's range of talus angles that breaks the planes up, not the uplift.
- **The 5 km relief spread fell** while the sheets read as more hierarchy: M1.11a's spread came from squares cut down to the base
  level beside squares left high, not from massifs; read it with the median.
- **`--labels` splits at commas** (again): a label "rust soft, blue-grey hard" failed the maps sheet.
- **`sed -i` from bash writes LF** over CRLF files (git normalises them; the files were converted back).

*Known limits:*

- **The lower ground is flat in places** (seed 42's south-west, seed 2026's centre): where the uplift map is near 0 the rivers cut down
  to the base level and the pools fill; M3 may want lakes there.
- **The beds are even**: on some faces the hard and soft beds make a regular staircase (`m1-11d-views.png`, down the slope); real
  strata vary more in thickness and in how they weather. M1.12's materials will show them first.
- **The uplift map follows ridge lines**: the first octave is ridged noise, so the massifs are long crests rather than round domes.
- **The incision's talus angles are not the final slopes'**: the mapping back compresses them (35–60° become about 26–50° on the 64 m
  grid), and the GPU erosion's own 40–65° acts after it on the 8 m grid.
- **The fluting** M1.7's passes cut is still there on the big faces; harder beds interrupt it.
- **The base terrain no longer stands in well** for a world that is not cached: the eroded world differs from it by up to a kilometre.
