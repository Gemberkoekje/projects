# Vista M1.12c — The world's footing and folded strata

The slice's goal, deliverable and verify are in [`vista-plan.md`](../vista-plan.md) §10, with a status line that links here.
Section references (§n) are to the plan; "the cost ledger" is [`cost-ledger.md`](cost-ledger.md); sheets cited by name are in
[`vista-views/sheets/`](../vista-views/sheets/README.md).

*M1.12c status (2026-10-24): done.* **The world stands at the place's altitudes:** the auto altitude now puts the world's lowest
valleys (the 5th percentile of its ground), not its height 0, at the place's valley floor, and the weather chain reads the air
there; seed 42's height 0 sits at 912 m at Zermatt (it sat at 1608 m), so its valleys meet Zermatt's floor and about 30% of the
world is forest (29.4%; seed 7 30.9%; the Swiss Alps are about 30%), where M1.12b's sat at 3.2%. **The rock's beds are folded:** a
dip and a strike of their own in each region about 10 km across (40° at the regions' centres), long folds 600 m high and 5 km
apart whose axes wander, formations of beds about 300 m thick each its own kind of rock, and a seeded 30% of the ground where the
beds fade into massive rock. The beds' outcrops on the rock no longer follow the contours: of their lines, 11.7–12.6% run within 10°
of the local contour (M1.12b: 15.1–16.6%; lines in random directions: 11.1%), and from the user's photo's height the slopes no longer
wear concentric bands (`m1-12c-photo.png`). Drainage holds; the facet measure rises from 8.6–10.8% to 12.4–13.8% (the slopes are
steeper and gather more closely, not at their own talus angle: 5–7% there, as before). With the new strata off this build erodes
`main`'s world bit for bit (seed 42, compared with `main`'s own build). The default, sky, down-slope and three materials views are re-placed on the new world. `Vista.Tests`
has 784 tests (769 before); `--gpu-selftest` passes 62 checks.

*What was built:*

- **The footing** (`Vista.Core/World/WorldFooting.cs`): a world's lowest valleys, the height of its ground's 5th percentile (rank
  round(p/100·(n − 1)), exactly, by a two-pass histogram select over the 16.8 M samples), and its lowest point. Every
  `TerrainWorld` carries its footing. The 5th percentile, not the lowest point, so a single pit or the base level at the grid's
  edge cannot move the world.
- **The anchor** (`LinkedSliders.Resolve(…, footing)`): the altitude slider is still the altitude of the world's height 0; its
  auto value is now the place's valley floor less the footing (never below sea level: held there, and said so), and a pinned
  altitude stays pinned. The weather chain reads the air at the **valley floor** — the base altitude plus the footing — so the
  temperature, the humidity, the freezing level and the visibility are the place's at its valley floor whatever the world's
  footing (`SliderState.FootingMetres`, `.ValleyFloorMetres`). Without a footing (callers without a world) height 0 is the valley
  floor, as before. The preview and `--diffuse-compare` pass their world's.
- **The strata in three dimensions** (`Vista.Core/World/RockHardness.cs`), each part a setting that is off by default (0) and on in
  `ErosionSettings.Mountains`:
  - **A dip and a strike per region** (`StrataRegionalDipDegrees` 40°, `StrataRegionMetres` 10 km): gradient noise scaled so the
    beds have the setting's dip at every lattice point, one of eight strikes there, blending into the next region's in between.
  - **Long folds** (`StrataFoldMetres` 600 m, `StrataFoldWavelengthMetres` 5 km): a periodic wave across a seeded direction, a
    quintic so its hinges are smooth and no transcendental moves its bits, its axes wandering by 0.35 of a wavelength over twice the
    wavelength and its height swelling and fading between 0.3 and all of the setting over three wavelengths. Its steepest limbs dip
    about 40°. M1.11d's planar dip (up to 8°) and its slow 120 m warp stay under them.
  - **Formations** (`StrataFormationMetres` 300 m): packages 0.3–1.7 times that thick, each with its own mean bed thickness (0.15–2
    times the 60 m bed, thin-bedded formations more common), its own lean toward hard or soft rock (±0.35), its own contrast between
    its beds (0.6–1 of M1.11d's) and its own tint, which its beds share for 65% of theirs: a hard limestone, a soft shale, a banded
    flysch.
  - **Massive rock** (`StrataMassiveShare` 0.3): a seeded mask (two octaves at 9 km, its threshold the noise's quantile over the
    default world's square, so every seed's world is close to the share) fades the beds into one rock: `MassiveTable`, about 0.6
    hard and changing smoothly with height (±0.3 over 350 m of strata), with ±0.15 of its own lateral variation at 900 and 290 m on
    top of the mottle, so its slopes do not settle into one talus plane. Hardness = bed + (massive − bed)·m + mottle, which with m = 0
    is the bed's to the bit.
- **The erosion reads it** (`StreamPower.Coarse`, `ErosionReference.Hardness`, `Shaders/VistaErosion.hlsl`): the incision's
  lookup and the GPU's carry the massive weight; the strata buffer is three floats a cell (offset, mottle, massive) and the bed
  table two an entry (the bed, massive rock) — `StrataGrid.Massive`, `StrataGrid.Bands`. The self-test's parity patch runs from
  layered to massive across it, so the blend is compared at every weight.
- **The materials read it** (`MaterialField`, `Shaders/VistaMaterials.hlsli`): three words a sample (the massive weight a byte in
  the third), massive rock's hardness in the bed table's fourth float; where the rock is massive its hardness moves toward massive
  rock's and the beds' tints, laminae and laminae's steps fade.
- **The contour-line measures** (`Vista.Core/World/LedgeStats.cs`): of the rock cells (the rules' rock weight ≥ 0.5) steeper than
  20°, the quarter where a quantity changes fastest count as its lines, and a line runs along the contour when the quantity's
  direction of change points down the slope, the angle taken on the slope itself (seen from above a steep face is foreshortened
  and every line on it looks closer to the contour than it is). **Bands** (the measure): the beds' outcrops, where the hardness at
  the surface changes fastest. **Ledges**: the lines of high curvature. `--erosion-measure` prints both at 8 m and 16 m, the footing,
  the share of massive rock, and the materials' shares at the altitude the sliders anchor the world at.
- **The cache** (`ErosionCache.PassesVersion` 4): the strata's shapes are code as well as settings, so worlds eroded before are not
  read as these. With the strata off the settings keep M1.11d's hash (`8e167baaa8d10c3f`), so `main`'s worlds would still be found by
  a build that kept passes version 3.
- **The sidecar** (schema 20): `world.footing` (the percentile, its height, the lowest point), `sliders.weather.valleyFloorMetres`
  and `.footingMetres`; the altitude's origin names the footing; the erosion's settings carry the strata's four new ones.
- **The views re-placed** (`vista-views/default.camera.json`, `sky.camera.json`, `down-slope.camera.json`, `materials/*.camera.json`,
  `forest/forest-crest.camera.json`): M1.11d's default spot is 184 m underground on the new world, the down-slope one 90 m, the
  meadow and scree views 60–70 m, and the rock face floated 86 m over a valley. Chosen by a search over the eroded seed-42 world
  (scripted outside the repo, as M1.11d's): the default 3 m above rock the GPU erosion moved by 0.3 m, at (−250, −5125), 1645.3 m,
  again looking 20° west of north at the range (45% sky, the range 1.4–3.8 km off; M1.11d's 46% and 1.6–3.9 km); the sky view
  there pitched up 45°; down the slope 3 m above a lit 35° face at (−6725, −375), 1510.8 m, looking down it toward the golden-hour
  sun; the scree cone, rock face and meadow 2 m above valley fill where rays from the frame's middle land on that material 40–500 m
  off (by the rules at the anchored altitude); the forested crest lifted to 28 m so CI's 20 m clearance holds. The self-test's camera
  and its loupe window (291, 139) moved with the default.

*M1.12c decisions (revisit freely):*

- **The footing is the 5th percentile of the whole grid**, the user's call (2026-10-23, §11). The edges hold the incision's base
  level, so the percentile includes some of the grid's rim; seed 42's is 696 m above height 0 where its lowest point is 392 m.
- **The altitude slider stays the altitude of height 0**; a pin keeps its meaning (M1.12b's sheets' `--altitude 1000` is the same
  scene's height 0 as before), and only the auto value moves. The weather moved to the valley floor so the temperature a user reads
  is the valley's, which a footing of 700 m would otherwise have warmed by 4.5 °C.
- **Sea level holds the anchor.** A world whose footing stands above a coastal place's valley floor keeps its height 0 at sea level
  (Lofoten, Fiordland: valleys some hundreds of metres up) rather than sinking the ground sphere below the sea.
- **Strata dip like the hillsides.** Beds dipping 15–25° still cross 35–50° slopes close to their contours; only dips like the slopes'
  own take the bands off the contours (the sweep in the table above). 40° at the regions' centres, blended between, with the folds'
  limbs up to about 40°.
- **The measure is the beds' outcrops, on the slope.** The ledge measure as first written (lines of high curvature) read 3–5% for
  every variant, below random: the sharpest-curved rock is the erosion's gullies and ribs, running down the slope. The beds' own
  outcrops are what the eye reads as contour lines, and their angle belongs on the face, not the map (a 10° window on the map is about
  15° on a 50° face). Even folded beds keep a little lean toward the contour (an outcrop's direction is the slope's plus the bed's
  dip), so random (11.1%) is a floor, not a target: **the target is within 1.5 points of random, at most 12.6%, on all four seeds**.
- **Massive rock varies.** Massive regions of one hardness made talus planes (the facet measure 11.9% with the mask alone); a smooth
  profile with height and ±0.15 of lateral variation keeps their slopes varied.
- **Formations cap their beds at twice the mean bed:** thicker ones stood as single talus planes.

*M1.12c gotchas:*

- **Constants in code move the world without moving its key.** The cache key holds the settings and `PassesVersion`; changing a
  constant in `RockHardness` mid-tuning served a stale world under the same key. Bump `PassesVersion` (done: 4) whenever the field's
  shape changes, and delete worlds eroded while tuning.
- **The disk is full.** The machine's C: drive had no space left; every eroded world is 285 MB. The measure and the variants keep one
  world at a time in a scratch cache and delete it after (`strata/render.ps1`'s `-Worlds`).
- **The self-test hard-codes its camera** (`GpuSelfTest.SelfTestCamera`), apart from `default.camera.json`: re-placing the view means
  moving both, or the self-test renders from underground (its loupe then sees no sky).
- **Tests read the cameras from their build output:** re-placing a camera needs a rebuild of `Vista.Tests` before its check sees it.
- **A seed now moves the air.** The footing is the world's, so another seed moves the valley floor and the weather read there: the
  air's tables rebuild with the world (`PreviewSessionTests`).
- **Bash heredocs** again cut a Python edit script short at a quote; scripts went through files.

*Known limits:*

- **The facet measure rose** (12.4–13.8% against M1.11d's 8.6–10.8%): the new world is steeper (44–51% of its 8 m slopes over 40°
  against 33–49%) and its slopes gather more closely around 47–49°, though not at their own talus angle (5–7% within 2° of it, M1.12b
  6–11%): dip slopes and middling rock, not M1.11a's talus planes (36–39%). The hardness lost some of M1.11d's split into hard and soft
  (13–18% soft and 27–33% hard, against 28–43% and 38–45%): formations of one character and massive rock are middling more often.
- **Folds are upright**: a bed is a surface over the ground's plan (σ = y + F(x, z)), so no overturned or recumbent fold, no thrust
  repeating a bed, no fault offsetting it.
- **The beds are thickest across the dip**: a steep dip thins a bed's outcrop on the slope (its vertical thickness is the table's);
  thin beds at steep dips read as laminae.
- **More rock, less meadow:** the steeper world is 45–48% rock against M1.12b's 36–48%, and the forest takes what is left below the
  tree line.
- **Coastal places** keep the world above the sea when its footing exceeds their valley floor.
- **The tools that bypass the sliders do not anchor**: `--sampling-compare`, `--atmosphere-compare`, `--denoise-compare`, the mesh
  measure and the self-test take their base altitude from `Places.BaseAltitude` (height 0 at the valley floor), as before; they
  compare estimators on a fixed scene. Previews, sheets, watch sessions and `--diffuse-compare` anchor.
