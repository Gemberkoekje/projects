# Vista M1.10a — Exposure & display

The slice's goal, deliverable and verify are in [`vista-plan.md`](../vista-plan.md) §10, with a status line that links here.
Section references (§n) are to the plan; "the cost ledger" is [`cost-ledger.md`](cost-ledger.md); sheets cited by name are in
[`vista-views/sheets/`](../vista-views/sheets/README.md).

*M1.10a status (2026-10-21): done.* Every deliverable and check is met. Nothing in `RayTracer.Core` / `RayTracer.Gpu` changed, nor the
integrator's `CSMain`: no EXR moved, and `--meter average --display aces` writes `main`'s PNG byte for byte. `Vista.Tests` has 613
tests (596 before); `--gpu-selftest` passes with 4 new checks (54 in all). What the review has to judge is the look, and three
findings bear on it (the tables below): **the washed-out slope was the meter's doing** — down the slope the card exposes 2.24 EV below
the log-average, and the sunlit faces drop from near white to mid-tones with their colour; **AgX is flatter and less saturated than
the ACES fit at the same exposure** (the slope's brightest tenth at saturation 0.23 against 0.34; skies greyer), since at the card's
exposure the slope is no longer on ACES's shoulder; and **skies come out darker** under the card (the sky-only view 2.49 EV, its sky
2.5 stops under a sun-facing grey card), while dawn and dusk come out 1.1 EV brighter than the log-average had them.

*What was built:*

- **The incident meter** (`Vista.Core/Output/IncidentMeter.cs`, `CSIncidentMeter` in `Vista.App/Shaders/VistaIntegrator.hlsl`), the
  default meter: an 18% grey card where the camera stands, facing the sun's azimuth and tilted up to its elevation (upright while
  the sun is below the horizon, never facing the ground), lit by the sun through the air (the visible part of its disk over the
  ground sphere, `SunVisibility`, times the columns LUT's transmittance) and by the camera's own sky view (slice 0, the haze's phase
  at the exact angle) over the card's hemisphere — below the horizon the lit ground sphere that view holds. The terrain is never in
  the card's way. A Lambertian card of albedo ρ under illuminance E has radiance ρE/π, so the exposure that puts the 18% card at
  0.18 scales the image by π/E. The GPU integrates the sky over `SkyDistribution`'s cells (128 zenith bands × 64 azimuth columns ×
  4² points, mirrored about the sun's vertical plane, where the card lies) per band and group of four wavelengths, and the sun once;
  the CPU weights the bands by their cells' solid angle, adds them in double and weighs the spectra with the integrator's own CIE
  ȳ and correction, so the card's illuminance is in the image's Y units. About 1 ms of GPU a view.
- **AgX** (`DisplayTransform.Agx`), the default display curve: three.js r169's `AgXToneMapping` ported line for line (Blender's
  AgX base after Filament: linear Rec.709 to Rec.2020, the inset, a log₂ encoding from 10 stops under middle grey to 6.5 over,
  Wrensch's sixth-order sigmoid fit, the outset, a 2.2 power, back to Rec.709, clamped), then the sRGB transfer as before. Middle
  grey lands at sRGB 128.
- **The options:** `--meter incident|average` (M1.3's log-average meter stays, unchanged), `--display agx|aces` (the per-channel
  Narkowicz fit stays, unchanged), on `--preview`, `--watch` (and its options file) and `--sheet`, and as sheet axes
  (`--vary meter=average,incident`, `--vary display=aces,agx`). The EXR never changes. **Sidecar schema 11:** `exposure.meter`, the
  reading in `exposure.incident` (the card's elevation and altitude, its illuminance in Y and lux, the sun's and the sky's parts) or
  `exposure.average` (the log-average and the meter render's size, moved there), `exposure.meteredEv` (the meter's own stops), and
  `image.display`. Sheet labels and titles read them, and count older sidecars as ACES and the average meter.
- **Re-exposing a kept render:** the session keys the incident measurement on the air's frame (the sun, the camera's altitude, the
  air), so a camera turn or a new size keeps it; changing the meter or the curve in a watch re-exposes and re-encodes the kept
  render (only the meter's stage runs, and the denoised image when the meter changes, since its input scale is the meter's).

*M1.10a decisions (revisit freely):*

- **The card stands at the camera, in the open.** The meter reads the light of the place and the hour, not the framing: no
  terrain shadows it, so a camera in a valley's shadow is exposed for the sunlit land as one in the sun is (the shadow then reads
  as shadow). Its altitude is the camera's, so the sky view it reads is the one the camera's rays see.
- **The card faces the sun, never the ground.** Facing the sun is the incident meter's convention for a sunlit subject; with the sun
  below the horizon the card stands upright toward the sun's azimuth, where twilight is brightest, and the reading runs on smoothly
  through sunset as the sun's term fades with its disk. A card facing the camera would make the exposure depend on the framing again.
- **The ground below the horizon counts.** The card's lower half sees the ground sphere as the sky view holds it (lit by the sun and
  the sky at the model's ground albedo), as a real card sees the land; leaving it out would under-read a low sun's card by the
  ground's share.
- **The quadrature is the sky distribution's cells**, per band so the readback is 26 KB and the bands add up in double on the CPU,
  in a fixed order: the reading is the same bits every time. The C# reference is the same sums in double (`BandSky`, `SunOnCard`).
- **AgX from three.js, not from Blender's LUT.** Blender's AgX is an OCIO configuration with a 3D LUT; three.js's (after Filament,
  after Blender 4) is a closed form that could be run, as published, on a GPU in a browser for reference values. Both curves keep
  their own linear-in, linear-out contract, so the sRGB transfer after them is the one the PNG always had.
- **The old look is one option away and unchanged:** `--meter average --display aces` writes `main`'s PNG byte for byte.
- **The incident meter reads the LUTs** whatever the image's atmosphere, as the average meter renders through them: a reference
  image and its LUT twin are exposed alike.
- **EV100 for the incident meter uses C = 250 lux·s** (ISO 2720's flat receptor), the average meter keeps K = 12.5.
- **Left for the review, not decided here:** AgX stays the default as planned, though at the card's exposure the ACES fit keeps more
  colour (above). If the review wants more colour, the candidates are measured the same way: AgX with its "punchy" look (Blender's and
  Filament's: power 1.35, saturation 1.4 after the sigmoid), or the ACES fit under the incident meter; and if skies read too dark, a
  default compensation. Materials (M1.12) and snow (M1.13) should be tuned only after that choice.

*M1.10a gotchas:*

- **`SunVisibility`'s float floor:** with the disk wholly below the horizon, `0.5 + asin(−1)/π` in float is about 3·10⁻⁸, not 0, so
  4° after sunset the card's sun is 3·10⁻¹² lux where the C# reference says 0. A billionth of the sky's light; the LUT build shares
  the function, so changing it would move every LUT and image. The self-test checks the sun's share is under a millionth.
- **AgX's sigmoid cancels in float:** its terms run to 40 and sum to about 1, so the browser's GPU and .NET differ by up to 1.4·10⁻⁵
  (at grey 8), a three-hundredth of an 8-bit step; the test allows 2·10⁻⁵.
- **A PowerShell function returns everything it does not capture:** the render script's per-variant lines went into the function's
  return value with the cell paths; they go to `Write-Host`.
- **`[IO.File]` with a relative path resolves against the process's directory, not PowerShell's `cd`:** in this session's shell
  that is the shared `C:\git\projects` checkout, which one such write touched (caught and restored). Use absolute paths.
