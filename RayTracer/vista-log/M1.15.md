# Vista M1.15 — Time & VRAM estimate

The slice's goal, deliverable and verify are in [`vista-plan.md`](../vista-plan.md) §10, with a status line that links here.
Section references (§n) are to the plan; "the cost ledger" is [`cost-ledger.md`](cost-ledger.md).

*M1.15 status (2026-10-27): done.* **`--estimate` says how long a final will take and what video memory it needs, and it
was right on every test view.** It runs every stage the final repeats, as the final will, and times it (at 4K and 64 spp that is most of
the run: 20–40 s of 27–64), traces the final's own first passes at its size and on its mesh until they settle and projects the rest,
times the files on the probe's own, renders the preview for the noise in steps of the 8-bit PNG, and plans the video memory from the
sizes of the buffers the render will make, before any is allocated. On seven views (the sky, the day's terrain, golden hour, dawn, snow,
dense mist), a fresh round after the design settled: every final's whole run inside its range and 1–3% from the middle, every trace
inside (−7% to +8%), every peak planned within 1 MiB (1.0–2.9 GiB of a 7.1 GiB budget). Every image render now plans its memory first
(refused over the budget, warned over 80%) and shows a live ETA that refines after every dispatch; no image moved. **Limits:** the
estimate holds while the GPU's other load stays as it was (a quarter's swing between two runs was seen); an estimate costs about what the
final's setup does (28–46 s), which the window (M1.16) will keep instead of repeating.

*What was built:*

- **`--estimate`** (`Vista.Core/Cli/PreviewSession.Estimate.cs`): a preview request estimated instead of made. A 4K final at 64 spp
  is mostly setup (the down-slope view: 62 s, of which the trace is 23 s, the mesh 13 s, the air's tables 9 s, the pools' shader 6.5 s,
  the world 3 s, the files 3.5 s), so the estimate runs every stage the final repeats, as the final will, and times it: the world,
  the pools, the auto camera, the mesh at the final's size, the device, the shaders, the upload, the acceleration structures, the air's
  tables, the exposure meter. It writes `<preview>.estimate.json` (sidecar kind `estimate`, schema 26) and nothing else, and prints
  the whole run as a range with its parts.
- **The probe** (`BatchPlannerOptions.StopAfterGpuSeconds`, `BatchPlanner.Stopped`, `SamplesCompleted`): the final's own first passes
  over the whole frame, at its resolution and on its mesh, with its outputs. The planner's full-frame passes grow as its cost estimate
  falls from its cautious 2 µs (one sample a pixel, one, two, three, four...), so the planner stops at a pass's end once at least
  1 s of GPU time is measured, at least two passes are done, and the pass that ended is as large as the passes settle at (the
  dispatch budget over the cost that pass measured); at 10 s it stops anyway. A cheap view's probe is the whole final (the sky view:
  64 spp in 0.15 s of GPU). Its image, divided by the samples asked for, is rescaled to those it traced before its files are written as
  the final's will be (and removed).
- **The projection** (`Vista.Core/Rendering/RenderEstimate.cs`, `TraceProjection`): the probe's passes as measured (they are the
  final's first passes), plus the final's remaining samples at the last pass's GPU nanoseconds per pixel-sample (over the passes of its
  size, the ramp's first pass left out) and the median wall seconds per GPU second of the passes after the first; rays per sample and
  rays per second beside them. Its margin is the larger of ±20% and twice the spread among the passes of the last one's size.
- **The ranges** (`TimeRange`, `FinalEstimate`): the setup ±15%, the trace by its margin, the files ±25% (timed on the probe's own),
  denoising ±30% (a centred crop a sixteenth of the frame, its filter scaled by the pixels, its device and commit not, plus the
  denoised PNG and EXR at the beauty's time), summed end to end. `startup` (the process's start to the renderer, from `Program`)
  counts in the setup.
- **The noise** (`NoiseMeter`): the preview at its own size, mesh and samples (64 to 1024) with its variance; a pixel's noise after
  one sample in steps of the 8-bit PNG is `255 · slope · σ / Y`, the slope the display curve's under the exposure (a central difference
  of ±5% of the light, on the encoded luma), so clipped highlights and black show none; the frame's is the 95th percentile;
  n samples' is that over √n. `--noise <steps>` adds the samples that meet the target and their time. A final with `--debug variance`
  records the noise its own pixels show (`render.noiseSteps`), against which the preview's is checked.
- **The memory plan** (`Vista.Core/Rendering/MemoryPlan.cs`, `D3D12VistaRenderer.PlanMemory`): every buffer the render will make,
  from the sizes it will make it at (the mesh's arrays, the acceleration structures' prebuild sizes, the image buffers by output, the
  pools, the scattering orders' scratch, the small tables), each rounded to 64 KiB, by the stages it lives through (mesh, acceleration
  build, air, trace), on a base measured when the renderer is made (the device, its pipelines and fixed tables, and the driver's own
  memory for acceleration builds, taken by a build of one triangle). The peak is the fullest stage; over DXGI's budget a render is
  refused before anything is uploaded (`InsufficientMemoryException` with the plan's numbers), over 80% of it warned about at once and
  in the sidecar. Every image render plans first and records its plan with the peak it measured (`render.memory`): read after each of
  its allocations and polled every 2 ms (`RenderStats.PeakVideoMemoryBytes`, `VideoMemoryBudgetBytes`).
- **The live ETA** (`Vista.Core/Rendering/TraceClock.cs`): the planner raises `Issued` and `Completed`; the clock keeps every row's last
  measured GPU cost per pixel-sample and the samples it still needs, so after every dispatch the wall time left is their sum times the
  wall seconds per GPU second so far (null during the first pass, when some rows have no cost). `Vista.App` prints a line every 5 s
  of a trace (`trace 45% · 10 s · about 13 s left`); the sidecar records the ETA at 10, 25, 50, 75 and 90% beside what was in fact left
  (`render.trace`).

  - *The final's own passes for 0.5 s*: the GPU within 1–2% on the first views, but the dawn view 6.6% low: its probe stopped among the
    growing passes (one sample a pixel at 11.4 ns, the next pass of two at 13.0).
  - *The final's own passes until they settle, ±15%* (two rounds, 14 runs): every whole run inside its range; 12 of 14 traces. The GPU
    time −8% to +16% of the projection; the misses were fog-sea's second final, 16% slower on the GPU than its probe a minute before (the
    desktop's load rose; 1.7% past the high end), and down-slope's second, whose probe of three passes read 2.63 wall seconds per GPU
    second where its final ran 2.27 (one pass interrupted). From these the probe measures at least a second, the wall ratio is the
    median of its passes, and the trace's margin is ±20%.
  - *The final design, a fresh round:* all seven whole runs inside their ranges and 1–3% from the middle (the finals 26.8–63.5 s); all

    a pixel on the costly views, four to six on the cheaper ones); the sky view's probe was its whole final (64 spp, 0.17 s).
- **Peak video memory**: the plan from the buffers alone stood 45–47 MiB under every measured peak (−1.6% to −4.5%), the same on every
  view; a memory trace through a render showed the plan exact stage by stage (the mesh +386.0 MiB against 386.1 planned, the
  acceleration build +229.6 against 229.7) and the driver keeping 37.7 MiB of its first build and 7.5 MiB from its first size queries.
  With the base measured after a one-triangle build, the plan meets every final's measured peak within 1 MiB (−0.06% to +0.02%; 998
  to 2926 MiB of a 7250 MiB budget), within the stated 3%. `--gpu-selftest` checks it with a fresh renderer (86 checks pass; its
  640×360 frame planned at 1470.1 MiB, measured at 1469.8).
- **The live ETA**: at 10–50% of each final's trace its prediction was within 2–9% of what was in fact left on the fresh round, and
  within 12% on every view but the sky over all 35 finals; the sky view's 0.4 s trace read up to 26% off. It says nothing during the
  first pass, as built.
- **The noise** (the fresh round's finals again with `--debug variance`, against their estimates' preview): the final's own noise at
  64 spp was 1.00 of what the preview said on the sky view (its pixels trace one ray a sample: their noise is the wavelengths', the same
  at any size), 0.98–0.99 in the low sun's shade and the fog (down-slope, fog-sea),
  0.89 at dawn, 0.70 and 0.62 on the day's terrain and 0.59 on the sunlit snow (default, aerial, snow), where a preview pixel spans
  edges and texture. So the samples a target needs are 0.34–1.00 of the preview's count; the estimate gives a third to all of it, and
  its noise at the final's samples is the high end.
- **The preview's cost is no guide**: the final ran at 0.10–0.72× the preview's cost per sample (320×180, 64 spp, the GPU at half its
  time), on every view; recorded as `preview.finalCostRatio`.
- **No image moved**: the four ledger views (640 px, 16 spp; the down-slope view with every debug output) rendered by `main`
  (7f87e220) and by this slice are byte-identical, PNG and EXR, sixteen files.
- **What an estimate costs**: 28–46 s, most of it the setup it times (the down-slope view: the mesh 13 s, the air's tables 9 s, the pools'
  shader 6.5 s, the world 3 s), the probe 0.4–3.2 s, the files 3.3 s, the preview under a second.

*M1.15 decisions (revisit freely):*

- **The final's own first passes, not random crops** (§7.2 planned "a handful of random crops at the true final resolution"): the
  cost of a sample depends on how the dispatches carry it (with the same rays, the snow view's sample cost 5.2 ns in passes of one
  sample a pixel, 5.9 in a pass of two, 6.2 of three and 6.7 of four; the aerial view's 5.0, 5.4, 5.5 and 5.65), and crops cheap enough
  to save anything hold too few pixels to fill the GPU. At 4K one pass over the whole frame costs 4–170 ms of GPU; a probe of the
  final's own passes traces exactly what the final will, dispatch for dispatch. Crops come back with M11's tiles, where a whole pass of
  a 16K frame is too costly.
- **Until the passes settle, and the rest at the last one's cost**: a first probe of four passes of one sample read the multi-sample
  views' finals 4–17% low; a probe of 0.5 s still stopped among the growing passes (the dawn view's last pass, of two samples, cost 14%
  more a sample than its passes of one, and the projection from their mean read 6.6% low). The planner's passes settle where its
  estimate meets the cost a pass measures, so the probe runs to that size (an equal size twice is not enough: the passes went 5, 5,
  then 6 on the planner's tests).
- **The estimate runs the setup instead of modelling it**: at 64 spp the setup is most of a 4K final (20–40 s of 27–64 s), and its
  parts (a CPU mesh of 22 M triangles, a shader compile, the air's tables at half the GPU) have no simple model. Running them costs the
  estimate as much again (an estimate takes 28–46 s), the price of measuring rather than guessing; a watch session or the window (M1.16)
  keeps them, and its estimate only needs the probe.
- **The preview is not the calibration**: at 320 px its dispatches cannot fill the GPU, and the final ran at 0.10–0.72× its cost per
  sample, so a preview alone would have been off by up to ten times. The estimate records the ratio (`preview.finalCostRatio`) and uses
  the preview for the noise only.
- **A range summed end to end** rather than a root sum of squares: the parts' errors share a cause (the GPU's other load, the CPU's)
  and may all fall the same way.
- **Noise in steps of the 8-bit PNG at the 95th percentile**: what a viewer sees, through the exposure and the display curve, so
  black and clipped highlights cost nothing, and a few fireflies do not set the samples for the rest. The PNG's own dither adds about
  0.4 of a step, so a target under that buys nothing visible.
- **The base after a one-triangle build**: the driver takes about 38 MiB at a process's first acceleration build and keeps it (and
  7.5 MiB at its first size queries), the same for every mesh; a plan from the buffers alone stood 45–47 MiB under every measured peak
  (−1.6% to −4.5%). Built into the base, the plan meets the peak to the MiB, on any driver that behaves so, without a constant.
- **Refused over the budget, warned over 80%**: DXGI's budget already leaves out what other programs hold; past it Windows pages the
  render's memory out (a crawl or a device loss), so the render is refused with the plan's numbers. Between 80% and 100% it fits only
  while nothing else grows.
- **`--estimate` only with `--preview`**: a contact sheet estimates many renders at once, and a watch keeps the stages an estimate
  times; both refuse it.

*M1.15 gotchas:*

- **A sample's cost depends on the dispatch**: rays per sample are identical, but a pass of four samples a pixel cost up to 29% more a
  sample than a pass of one (the snow view). A probe must trace the final's own dispatches, up to the size its later passes keep.
- **The GPU's other load moves a final's trace by a quarter**: the default view's 64-spp final took 2.63 s of GPU in one run and 2.11 s
  in the next, minutes apart, with the same image. The probe measures the load of its moment; the trace's ±20% covers what changes.
- **A shared GPU's wall time depends on the dispatch's length**: `GpuShare` pauses after each submission, and `Thread.Sleep` rounds to
  the timer's 15.6 ms, so the sky view's dispatches of 2–5 ms took 3.3–5.4 wall seconds per GPU second where a final's 30 ms ones take
  2.1–2.4. Another reason the probe traces the final's own dispatches.
- **Freed memory is not always free**: the first acceleration build's driver memory stays with the process (above). DXGI's
  `CurrentUsage` otherwise follows a `Dispose` at once.
- **A game on the GPU can hang the self-test's erosion**: rerun on the final build (whose only change from the verified one is the noise
  range's constant) while a game held the GPU at 77% and 6.4 GB, `--gpu-selftest` lost the device (`DXGI_ERROR_DEVICE_HUNG`) in the
  erosion of its world, which this slice does not touch; it had passed on the verified build half an hour before.
- **`TimeSpan.FromSeconds` truncates to ticks**: 3e-4 s became 2999 ticks in a test; the clock's tests feed whole ticks.
- **The Edit tool can leave a CRLF file LF-only**: `D3D12VistaRenderer.cs` and `FakeRenderer.cs` came out LF; git normalises them,
  but the working copies were set back to CRLF before the commit.

*Known limits:*

- **The estimate is of a fresh CLI run on this machine**, with the GPU's other load as it was while it ran; a game started between the
  estimate and the final falls outside the range.
- **The auto camera is timed by running it** (about 40 s), and the final runs it again unless it is given the pick's camera file.
- **The denoiser's own memory on CUDA is not in the plan**: Open Image Denoise allocates outside D3D12 after the render; its time is
  estimated, its memory not.
- **No estimate for a contact sheet or a watch**; the window (M1.16) will estimate on its session's kept stages.
- **The noise is the luminance's**: colour noise (a pixel's hue flickering) is not counted.
- **Tiles**: 8K and 16K need M11's tiled rendering; the plan refuses a 16K frame's image buffers on 8 GB.
