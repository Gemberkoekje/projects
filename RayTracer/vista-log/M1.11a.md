# Vista M1.11a — Drainage

The slice's goal, deliverable and verify are in [`vista-plan.md`](../vista-plan.md) §10, with a status line that links here.
Section references (§n) are to the plan; "the cost ledger" is [`cost-ledger.md`](cost-ledger.md); sheets cited by name are in
[`vista-views/sheets/`](../vista-views/sheets/README.md).

*M1.11a status (2026-10-21): done, by the plan's fallback.* The flow accumulation came first and showed what was wrong: **the ridged
base terrain is half closed basins** (43–51% of the grid lies in hollows deeper than 10 m), and M1.7's erosion leaves 41–49% of them,
so no channel runs more than 1.6–1.9 km before it ends in one. Coarse-to-fine erosion could not fix that: tried at its coarsest level
first, sixteen times M1.7's steps on a 32 m grid still left 41% in basins, because the pipe model's rain evaporates within about a hundred
steps and never fills a basin hundreds of metres deep. So the slice built the plan's fallback, **stream-power incision** (Cordonnier et
al. 2016's model, Braun and Willett's solver) on a 64 m grid, on the CPU, before the GPU erosion: every closed basin drains, and on the
eroded world the longest channel without a lake runs **38–51 km** where M1.7's ran 1.6–1.9, and **98%** of the grid drains into a
channel of 1 km² against 3–6%. The GPU erosion's own sediment then dammed the new valleys (7% of the grid in basins again), so the pools it leaves fill with
sediment to their outlets, as the incision's lakes do. Nothing in `RayTracer.Core` / `RayTracer.Gpu` changed, nor the integrator or the
erosion passes. `Vista.Tests` has 684 tests (669 before); `--gpu-selftest` passes (55 checks, one new). Left for the review: the look
(`m1-11a-seeds.png`, `m1-11a-views.png`, `m1-11a-drainage.png`), the flat floors the pool filling makes, the fluting M1.7's passes still cut into the faces, and the 45° talus facets the
incision leaves on some peaks.

*What was built:*

- **The drainage** (`Vista.Core/World/DrainageMap.cs`): each cell's water leaves by its steepest way down (D8) over the surface with every
  depression filled first, by **Priority-Flood+ε** (Barnes, Lehman and Mulla 2014) in double precision: a hollow fills to its lowest rim plus
  one ulp per cell inward, so flats drain toward their outlet and every inner cell has a lower neighbour. The edge is the world's outlet. The
  map keeps the **directions** (a byte a cell), the **fill depths** (halves: where a lake would stand) and, worked out from the directions in
  Kahn's order, the **upstream area** of every cell (an integer count, so exact) and that order. A pure function of the surface's bits:
  3 s for the world grid. `DrainageStats` measures it (below).
- **Every eroded world carries its drainage**: `ErosionResult.Drainage`, in its hash, and in the cache (format 2: the directions and the
  fill depths after the maps, 252 MB a world; the upstream areas follow from the directions on reading). A render that finds the world
  in the cache spends 2.3 s on the world stage (M1.7: 0.85 s): the bigger file, the drainage's order, hash and measures. The sidecar
  (schema 13) records the world's drainage measures in `world.erosion.drainage`, and the cost of the incision and of the maps and
  drainage in its stats. A dry run writes `<out>.drainage.png` beside `<out>.erosion.png`: the upstream area in blue from 0.05 km² to
  the largest river, lakes over 1 m dark blue, each pixel the largest area among the cells it covers (so a channel one cell wide
  survives the reduction).
- **The incision** (`Vista.Core/World/StreamPower.cs`), run by `WorldBuilder` between the base terrain and the GPU erosion, on the CPU:
  - the base terrain is generated on a **64 m grid** (513², with the octaves that grid holds) and incised for **300 steps** of
    `∂h/∂t = U − K·√A·S`: each step routes the rain over the filled surface (`DrainageMap.Of`), then solves every cell implicitly from the
    outlets upstream, `h = (h + F·h_receiver) / (1 + F)` with `F = K·Δt·√A / L` (`IncisionRate` 0.03), so a basin's whole catchment
    drains through its lowest rim and that rim cuts down fastest;
  - **lakes** do not incise; they **fill 5% of their depth a step** with sediment (`IncisionLakeFill`) while the outlet cuts;
  - cells draining less than **0.1 km²** are hillslope and do not incise (`ChannelAreaKm2`); every cell keeps within **45°** of the cell it
    drains into (`IncisionTalusDegrees`), so incised valleys widen into V shapes;
  - an **uplift** of 2 m a step at the base terrain's highest point, in proportion to its height elsewhere, keeps the ranges where the base
    terrain put them (`IncisionUplift`); the result is mapped back to the base terrain's range of heights, a linear map that moves no
    drainage direction, so the world does not rise under the cameras;
  - back on the 8 m grid (`Refine`): bilinear upsampling, then every **channel link carved** as a straight falling line into the fine grid
    (`CarveLinks`; bilinear interpolation leaves a saddle of half the valley's depth in every coarse cell of a diagonal channel), then the
    base terrain's finer detail added back where the incision left the ground as it was (fading out over 25 m of change).
- **The pools fill** (`ErosionResult.From`, `PoolFillMetres` 100 m): after the GPU erosion, the hollows its sediment left fill to their
  outlets (at most 100 m deep), the fill counted as loose material and their standing water gone: flat valley floors behind the fans of
  side valleys, which the water crosses.
- **Settings** (`ErosionSettings`): `IncisionSteps`, `IncisionCell`, `IncisionRate`, `ChannelAreaKm2`, `IncisionTalusDegrees`,
  `IncisionUplift`, `IncisionLakeFill`, `PoolFillMetres`, in the hash and the cache key, overridable by `VISTA_EROSION_SETTINGS` and
  `--set`. The mountains run all of it; the hills run neither (`IncisionSteps = 0`, `PoolFillMetres = 0`: M1.7's erosion, untouched).
  `--set IncisionSteps=0;PoolFillMetres=0` is M1.7's world, bit for bit (the passes did not change).
- **`--erosion-measure`** reports the incision (time; the drainage of the coarse grid before and after it and of the ground handed to the
  GPU) and the drainage of the base and the eroded world, with their drainage images; `--incise-only` stops before the GPU.
- **`--gpu-selftest`** compares the drainage in its bit-identity and cache checks, and checks that every committed view in `vista-views/`
  stands above the eroded seed-42 ground.

- **Before the pool filling** (the same four seeds, incised, then M1.7's erosion alone): 9.5–10.9% lakes and 6.7–7.9% closed basins,
  the longest lake-free channel 1.4–2.3 km, 10.6–13.9% draining into a channel of 1 km² (47–51% into one of 0.1 km²). The incision had
  left 0.1–0.3% lakes on the 8 m grid; the erosion's sediment dams the main valleys at the mouths of side valleys (`m1-11a-drainage.png`
  shows the filled floors). Lower evaporation in the erosion did not help (above).
- **What the measures could not tell apart.** D8 over any filled surface makes a connected network: the longest route *through* lakes is
  30–37 km on the base terrain and on M1.7's world, and the Strahler orders (4–6) and bifurcation ratios (3.6–4.8, inside
  natural networks' 3–5) barely move (Horton's laws hold for random networks too). The lakes and the length of channel between them are what
  separate a terrain that drains from one that does not, so the lake and basin shares and the lake-free channel were added to the plan's
  measures.

*M1.11a decisions (revisit freely):*

- **Stream power, not coarse-to-fine**, by the measure above and the plan's own condition ("only if that does not give branching
  valleys"). Coarse-to-fine was tested at its coarsest level instead of being built: if the 32 m level cannot drain the basins, the 16 m and
  8 m levels cannot.
- **The incision runs on the CPU**, single-threaded where order matters (the flood and the implicit solve run in the drainage's order). It
  costs 13–15 s a seed, once, before the 19 s of GPU erosion; a world comes from the cache after that. A GPU port (Schott et al. 2023's
  iterated drainage) would need its own parity; not worth it at this cost.
- **From the base terrain, with a little uplift, not from flat.** The recipe's massifs stay where the base terrain puts them, and the slopes
  keep the noise's shape where the rivers did not cut, rather than becoming talus planes.
- **Lakes fill slowly during the incision, pools fill at once after the erosion.** During the incision a lake filled at once becomes a plain
  that never dissects; filled 5% a step, its outlet drains it first. After the erosion nothing cuts any more, so the pools its sediment left
  fill to their outlets: the fan of a side valley across the main valley, with a flat floor behind it.
- **The drainage is D8 over the filled surface**, edges as outlets. One way down per cell gives the network the measures need (orders,
  lengths); M1.12 may want a spread flow (multiple directions) for wetness on hillslopes, which can be derived from the same surface.
- **Directions and fill depths in the cache, upstream areas derived**: 50 MB more a world (252 MB, from 201), where the areas would add
  67 MB more, and they are exact integers either way.
- **The hills are left alone** until their own milestone: no incision, no pool filling; their worlds equal M1.7's.
- **The sheets show the terrain in a low afternoon sun** (the day recipe at `--sun-elevation 12`): golden hour at +2° (M1.11) leaves most of
  the ground in shadow.

*M1.11a gotchas:*

- **Bilinear upsampling dams diagonal channels:** the middle of a coarse cell averages the two valley walls in, a saddle of half the
  valley's depth (30 m here). 4% of the 8 m grid became lakes until the channel links were carved.
- **Carving every link grooves the faces:** on a hillslope the links run side by side; only channels are carved.
- **The flood's pit queue is not in height order** with ties, so a cell's receiver may come later in the pop order; the upstream areas are
  summed in Kahn's order from the directions instead.
- **PowerShell's variables ignore case, again:** `$S` (a settings string) overwrote `$s` (the scratch folder), and `--erosion-measure` wrote
  its images into a folder named after the settings inside the worktree. Name locals apart.
- **Building Vista.App while a measurement runs** fails to copy the locked dll: the build is stale until it runs again.
- **A heredoc holding a Python triple-quoted string** broke bash's quoting ("unexpected EOF"); edit through a script file.

*Known limits:*

- **M1.7's fluting is still there**, now on drained valley walls: the pipe model's rills run side by side down every face, since its
  water never gathers across one.
- **Talus facets:** where the incision's 45° limit holds a slope (some peaks between deep valleys), the face is a plane along one of the
  eight directions (the default view's left peak).
- **The valley floors are flat where pools filled**, behind the fans of side valleys; a river channel through them is M3's.
- **The edge is the base level**: rivers leave the grid at whatever height the base terrain has there.
- **The CLI's default camera** (`DefaultCamera.For`, no `--camera`: 1.7 m above the ground at a fixed spot) now stands on a valley floor
  of seed 42 facing a wall, with no sky in frame; the self-test pins M0's pose for seed 42 instead (`default.camera.json`, now 532 m above
  that floor). The auto camera (M1.14) replaces both.
- **The committed views look from higher up:** the default and down-slope views stand 532 and 552 m above a valley floor (8.2 and 28 m
  above M1.7's ground), so their pixels show the range from above, not from the ground; M1.9's off-screen-shadow view no longer stands in a
  valley shadowed by a ridge behind it, so `vista-views/mesh/render.ps1`'s shadow check needs a new pose before it means anything again.
