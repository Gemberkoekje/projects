# Vista M1.11c — Output finish

The slice's goal, deliverable and verify are in [`vista-plan.md`](../vista-plan.md) §10, with a status line that links here.
Section references (§n) are to the plan; "the cost ledger" is [`cost-ledger.md`](cost-ledger.md); sheets cited by name are in
[`vista-views/sheets/`](../vista-views/sheets/README.md).

*M1.11c status (2026-10-22): done.* The PNG's finish: the punchy look's saturation held back per pixel where it would take a channel
below 0, a triangular dither before the 8-bit rounding (`--dither off` for none), and `--png16`. The dither does what it is for: on a
3840×2160 sky at 1024 spp, denoised, single 8-bit levels ran 260–530 rows down a column, and dithered no run is longer than 27. The
held-back saturation does what the plan asked but not what the review hoped: the sky view's red channel is still 0 (96.6% of its
pixels, as before), because this sky's colour under the punchy look lies on Rec.709's edge; it moves 2–4 levels toward green, its hue
2–3° toward cyan. Only the sky view and the deepest shadows down the slope change; the other four views have no clipped pixels. The
EXR is unchanged; `--look base --dither off` writes `main`'s PNG byte for byte. `Vista.Tests` has 706 tests (690 before);
`--gpu-selftest` passes (55 checks, none new: the finish is CPU-only).

*What was built:*

- **Gamut-safe punchy saturation** (`DisplayTransform.GamutSafePunchy`): a colour whose PNG linear Rec.709 keeps every channel at or
  above 0 under Filament's saturation of 1.4 gets exactly M1.11b's look. Otherwise the look's saturation is lowered just enough that
  no channel goes below 0, never below 1 (the power alone; a colour that clips even there is left to the clamp, as the plan says).
  The look is linear in the saturation and so is the outset after it, so an outset channel's zero is a closed form; but it only
  bounds the answer (see the gotchas), so it is the top of a 14-step bisection through the rest of the curve, which keeps the side
  that does not clip (the saturation to 2.4·10⁻⁵). `AgxLinear` returns the PNG's linear Rec.709 before the clamp and the
  saturation each colour got, for the tests.
- **The dither** (`PngDither`, `DisplayTransform.DitherOffset`): before the 8-bit rounding, a triangular (TPDF) offset on (−1, 1)
  step in the sRGB-encoded value, the difference of two 24-bit uniforms from one `StableHash.Lattice` of the frame pixel and the
  channel (its own stream, `"png.dither"`). It is keyed by the frame pixel, so a loupe is still an exact crop of the frame; black
  and white (the curve's clamp) stay exact, so a clipped sky or a black frame is not speckled. On by default for the beauty and the
  denoised PNG; `--dither off` rounds as M1.11b did.
- **The 16-bit PNG** (`--png16`): `DisplayTransform.ToSrgb16` (the same exposure, curve and look, rounded to 65 535 steps, no
  dither) and `PngEncoder.Encode16` / `Write16` (big-endian samples, the filters on whole 6-byte pixels). `PngDecoder` reads 8- and
  16-bit files: `PngImage.BitDepth`, `Samples16`, and `Pixels` rounded to the nearest 8-bit step so `--sheet-from` tiles a 16-bit
  render. Debug images and sheets stay 8-bit.
- **The display transform** is split into `Encode` (exposure, curve, look and the sRGB transfer, in parallel blocks of 16 384
  pixels, which changes no bit) and the quantisation (`ToSrgb8` undithered, `ToSrgb8(…, dither, window)`, `ToSrgb16`).
- **The options:** `--dither on|off`, the flag `--png16`, the sheet axis `dither=off,on` (one render serves both cells) and both in a
  watch's options file. **Sidecar schema 15:** `image.dither` (`triangular`, `off`, or `none` for a 16-bit PNG) and
  `image.bitDepth`. Sheet labels read both; older sidecars count as `off` and 8 bits, so a title says "no dither" for them.

*M1.11c decisions (revisit freely):*

- **Just enough, as the plan says, and so the colour sits on the edge.** A held-back colour's lowest channel is 0 (within the
  bisection's step): the most saturated colour of its hue inside Rec.709. That removes the clamp's arithmetic but not the
  "red channel at 0" the review saw in the sky view: that is the punchy look's own colour for this sky, not the clamp's (the
  checks). Keeping red above 0 would need a margin or a knee that stops short of the edge, which is a change of look: after the
  review (2026-10-22) it is one line of M1.17's re-judgement of the look, not a slice of its own.
- **The dither is per channel and keyed by the frame pixel, not by the seed or the view.** Two renders of one frame get the same
  noise pattern; a loupe gets the frame's. Black and white are left undithered, so the TPDF's zero mean holds everywhere else.
- **16 bits has no dither and does not reach the sheets.** At 257 times finer steps banding is below any display; a contact sheet
  is a comparison tool and stays 8-bit, reading a 16-bit cell rounded.

*M1.11c gotchas:*

- **An outset channel at 0 is not a Rec.709 channel at 0.** The plan expected the closed form at the look to suffice where only
  the outset clips. It never does: where an outset channel is 0, the Rec.2020 → Rec.709 matrix's negative off-diagonal terms
  (−0.5876, −0.0728 for red) take that Rec.709 channel below 0 by themselves. So the closed form is the bisection's upper bracket.
- **The look's saturation also sets the greys' tone.** Filament's saturation is taken about the luma before the power, so for a
  grey it scales the power's darkening: middle grey's sRGB 88 needs the full 1.4. A grey clips only in the shadows the look
  crushes (the sigmoid's output under 0.028: linear greys under 0.0033, 5.8 stops under middle grey), where the held-back
  saturation lands exactly on 0, the byte the clamp gave: greys are unchanged. A dark colour there keeps its other channels
  above 0, but at 10⁻⁵ and less: the same bytes.
- **Windows PowerShell leaves `$PSScriptRoot` empty in a parameter's default when a mandatory parameter is given**: the render
  script sets its `-App` default in the body.
