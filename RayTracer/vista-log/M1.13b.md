# Vista M1.13b — Terrain-following valley mist

The slice's goal, deliverable and verify are in [`vista-plan.md`](../vista-plan.md) §10, with a status line that links here.
Section references (§n) are to the plan; "the cost ledger" is [`cost-ledger.md`](cost-ledger.md); sheets cited by name are in
[`vista-views/sheets/`](../vista-views/sheets/README.md).

*M1.13b status (2026-10-27): done.* **Each valley holds its own pool**: a pool map per world (the floor its air drains to, how
well it holds a pool, how deep its walls allow), cached with it and reaching 65 km into the far field, so some valleys fill and
others drain; M1.13a's chain sets the scene's pool and each valley takes its share by its shape. **Its haze thins upward from its
floor, and where its air cools to the dew point** (per-place humidity normals, each sourced) **mist forms**: uniform under a sharp
top, a strong deck's top flat over the valley and frayed where it laps the slopes or where it is weak (the Sunnegga webcam). **The
march takes them in three dimensions** through a sunlight volume over the low air (the terrain's shadow, the pools' haze and the
mist toward the sun): the mist scatters in octaves weighted by the sun's elevation (fitted to a Monte Carlo of a slab: 0.6–1.9× of
it where plain octaves ran to 5.5×), within 2–3% of the reference without the terrain's shadow and about 10% on a deck. Peaks rise
out of a mist lake at Fiordland's autumn dawn, which breaks up through the morning, and Zermatt at the webcam's 07:40 shows a flat,
opaque fog sea under clear peaks. **Limits:** a ridge's shadow leaves the march +19% over the shadowed reference (the sky's light
there is still the open sky's); views with pools cost more than the plan's 2× (the golden hour's haze pools 1.84× `main`, a strong
misty pool 3.1× the flat layer), views without them as before; both parked (§10). The GPU now runs at half its time by default.

*What was built:*

- **The pool map** (`Vista.Core/World/PoolMap.cs`, cached with the world in `WorldCache.Pools`): for every 64 m of the world (and
  every 256 m of the far field's base terrain out to 65 km, so valleys beyond the world's tile hold their pools too) the valley floor
  its air drains to, how well that valley holds the night's pool and how deep a pool its walls allow.
  - *The floor*: at night the cooled air slides down the fall line to the valley floor; a cell's floor is the first cell on its
    way down (the drainage) that drains at least 1 km², or that lies in a closed hollow at least 10 m deep (filled flat to its rim),
    the height above it the terrain's height above its nearest drainage (HAND, Rennó et al. 2008). Both slopes of a valley drain to
    the same floor, so a pool's top lies level across it and follows the valley down its length.
  - *The hold*: `1/(1 + (g/0.06)²)` with `g` the floor's gradient over the next kilometre downstream: cold air drains out of a steep
    valley as a shallow down-valley flow and pools deep on flat floors and in basins (Whiteman 2000, ch. 9).
  - *The cap*: how far the valley's surroundings (the ground smoothed over a Gaussian of σ 1.5 km) stand above its floor, about half
    a V-valley's depth.
  - *The far field*: its base terrain is not eroded, and its hollows are noise: there every floor is the ground's own height, never
    a hollow filled to its rim (filled, they drowned 68% of it).
- **Each valley's pool at the hour** (`Vista.Core/Weather/ValleyPools.cs`): M1.13a's chain sets the night's pool over the scene's
  valley (its depth, the sun's heat against its deficit); a valley holds its hold's share of it, at most its cap; its cooling and
  heat deficit scale with its depth, so what the sun leaves of it is `1 − q/r²` (`q` the sun's heat against the full pool's deficit,
  `r` its depth over the full pool's) and a shallow pool breaks sooner; after the day's high it forms again with the night's cooling.
  Its air: the free air is the temperature slider's (inside the scene's valley's pool) warmed by that pool's cooling; a valley's floor
  is colder by its own pool's; the air warms up through a pool at the inversion's 10 °C/km. Its haze: the valley's own haze (thinner
  on a higher floor), held under the lid (up to twice by dawn) and grown by the humidity at each height in the pool against the day's,
  so densest at the humid floor and thinning upward; a pinned valley haze sets the floor's density of the densest pool. Its mist:
  where the air is colder than its dew point, from the floor up to where the warming air and the dew point (falling 1.8 °C/km in the
  pool's mixed air) meet; its water 0.06 g/m³ per °C below the dew point (about a sixth of what the cooling condenses near 0 °C; the
  rest settles as dew) up to 0.075 g/m³ (about 200 m to see: fog proper is M4's), its extinction Kunkel's (1984) `0.1447·LWC^0.88`
  per metre. The tables' flat layer (below) is the pools' haze averaged over the world, matched by a box with the same column, mean
  altitude and spread.
- **Humidity normals** (`ClimateNormals.VapourPressure`, `DewPointOn`; `ClimateTable`): each place's monthly mean vapour pressure,
  with a source per place: four places' own stations (Banff, Zermatt, Punta Arenas; Milford Sound's 9 a.m. vapour pressure, 1981–2010),
  four near stations' (Reykjavík, Aviemore, Dobbiaco, Asahikawa) and NASA POWER's climatology (MERRA-2, 2001–2025, dew point converted)
  at Yellowstone, Yosemite and Lofoten, where no station near enough publishes humidity normals. Carried from the station's altitude
  to a valley floor, and between floors, by Hann's formula (vapour tenfold per 6.3 km, about 5 °C per km near 0 °C). The mist reads
  them; the humidity slider and the haze keep the day's low as their dew point (M1.11).
- **The low air on the GPU** (`Vista.App/Shaders/VistaLowAir.hlsli`, the float twin of `Vista.Core/Atmosphere/LowAir.cs`): the pool
  cells of both levels (four words a sample: the floor, the pool's and the mist's depths, the haze at the floor and the top, the
  mist's water), read bilinearly, the world's level blending into the far field's over the world's outer 2 km as the terrain does;
  the density at a point (the haze's profile under a logistic top 30 m soft; the mist uniform under one 8 m soft, as a fog layer's
  own turbulence mixes it, Roach et al. 1976; both ending 25 m below the floor, under the ground); a 3D
  gradient noise (Perlin's 2002 gradients, wavelengths 220 m across and 60 m up) moving both tops by up to 25 m (the wisps), and a 2D
  one (1.8 km) moving the mist's top by up to 0.8 of its depth, never above its pool (the patches), the mist taking them only where
  it frays: within 80 m of its top the ground rises through it (its edge, where it laps the slopes; each cell carries how far the
  mist's top stands above its ground), or where it is weak (less water than the cap); and a ceiling map (1 km blocks, the
  highest any pool reaches over a block and its neighbours). All in one buffer, the integrator's last root slot (t24: 64 of 64 words).
- **The sunlight volume** (`Vista.App/Shaders/VistaLowAir.hlsl`, `D3D12VistaRenderer.LowAir.cs`): built once a render (new pools,
  terrain or sun) over each level, every second cell (128 m, 512 m), in 32 layers following the floors: 24 from the pool's floor to
  its top plus 150 m, 8 from there up to the pools' highest reach (the low air a ridge may shade); per voxel the terrain's shadow (a ray toward the sun against the render's own terrain, from the ground
  above the voxel when it lies under it), the pools' haze column toward the sun less the tables' layer's and the mist's optical depth
  toward the sun (48 steps growing from the voxel, the sun's height at the voxel), and the mist's optical depth straight up; halves,
  two voxels an element.
- **The march** (`LowMarch`, `AirAlong` in `VistaIntegrator.hlsl`): a segment's part below the pools' highest reach is marched
  through the pools in steps of 150 m through their haze (or 1.5% of the distance, whichever is longer), and near a mist shorter: in
  the band its top can reach at most 50 m and an optical depth of 0.5 of the mist the last step found (about 34 m in the densest),
  above the band at most to it; a bounce's steps 300 m, at most 8; a camera ray's to the sky at most 24, growing by 3% of the
  distance. Each segment's steps are jittered by one random number so their edges turn into noise. Where the ray runs above every
  pool near it (the ceiling) it strides to where it could meet the ceiling, at most a ceiling block (1 km over the world, 4 km over
  the far field); the parts above take M1.4's march. In a step the sun reaches the point through
  the Earth's shadow and the clear air (the columns LUT, which carries the tables' layer) and the volume (the terrain's shadow, the
  pools' haze less that layer, the mist); the air and the haze scatter it once and the tables' multiple scattering (dimmed by the mist
  above), and the mist scatters it in octaves (Wrenninge et al. 2013: six, each half the light of the one before, seeing half its
  optical depth toward the sun, with half its asymmetry; the second weighted 2·μ₀^¼ and the rest, nearly diffuse, 1.6·μ₀, μ₀ the
  sine of the sun's elevation: the decision below) and the sky's light from the tables. A surface in a pool takes the sun
  through the pool's haze and the mist's total transmission (`1/(1 + ¾(1 − g)τ)`, its forward scattering keeps most light going) and
  the sky through the pool's haze above it less the tables' layer's; a camera's ray to the sky from below the pools' reach is marched
  through them with the tables' layer taken out.
- **Two integrators**: the pools' code is compiled (`LOW_AIR`) only into the variant a render with pools uses, on its first such
  render; a render without them runs the M1.13a integrator, at its speed.
- **The tables' layer**: the atmosphere's tables (sky view, scattering orders, columns) carry the pools' haze as one flat layer, their
  altitude average (the decision below); every march here leaves it out and takes the pools instead.
- **The low air in a ridge's shadow**: every step of the march under the pools' reach (in a pool, in the air over it, or striding)
  reads the volume, so the terrain shades the sun's light scattered once there, not only in the pools; and the tables' light
  scattered many times keeps half of itself in full shadow (`ShadowedScattering`).
- **The reference** (`ATMOSPHERE_REFERENCE`): the pools' haze and mist are part of the medium (a third scatterer, the mist's
  Henyey–Greenstein lobe), the majorant bounding their densest where a segment dips below their reach, ratio tracking through them
  toward the sun; with the terrain's shadow (`referenceShadowed`) every event below their reach checks it, not only the first.
- **The sliders and the options**: `Slider.Mist` (`mist` in the slider file: km, 0 for none; `--mist <km> | off`, 0.2–5 km), the
  valley haze's reading now the scene's own valley's pool ("6 km at the floor, its top at 2054 m"); `--pools on | flat` (flat:
  M1.13a's single layer, for comparisons); the sidecar (schema 23) records the pools (`sliders.weather.pools`: how they were rendered,
  the weather they were told, their stats, the tables' layer); a dry run writes `<preview>.pools.png`, the pools from above.
- **The checks' tools**: `--low-air-check` (the low air's parity checks alone, on the cached seed-42 world; part of `--gpu-selftest`
  too), `--pools-compare` (one view as the preview renders it, through the march, the reference and the shadowed reference, by thirds
  and by distance; a reference's raw image kept beside it and read back), `VISTA_LOW_AIR` (the march's settings from the environment)
  and two diagnostic defines (`LOW_AIR_NO_SHADOW`, `LOW_AIR_NO_SKY_MARCH`).
- **The GPU's share** (`Vista.Core/Rendering/GpuShare.cs`; asked for mid-slice: the dev box is the user's own PC, and a long
  reference held it at 100% for minutes): Vista.App runs below normal priority and, by default, on half the GPU: it pauses after
  every submission as long as the GPU worked (`VISTA_GPU_SHARE`, 0.05–1, `1` the old full speed), and splits each planned batch into
  dispatches under 30 ms by rows or samples (images bit-identical), a render's first a probe of one row and one sample and each next
  at most four times the last's (a reference through dense mist costs 40 times a clear view's sample); the LUT, volume and erosion
  chunks shrink with it. Timings in the
  sidecar are the GPU's own, unchanged; only the wall time doubles.

*M1.13b decisions (revisit freely):*

- **Where the air pools comes from the drainage, not from a fill of the whole terrain**: a cell's floor is the first on its way down
  that drains 1 km² (or lies in a closed hollow at least 10 m deep), so both slopes of a valley share a floor and a pool lies level
  across it. Filling every hollow (a priority flood) made the floors the hollows' rims: puddles of 1–3 m on the eroded world's
  ridges held pools as deep as the valleys'; 10 m keeps the real basins and leaves the puddles out.
- **The far field holds pools too, from its base terrain**: without it every valley beyond the world's 32.8 km stood clear and the
  pools ended at the tile's edge, in the views across it. Its hollows are noise, so none is filled there.
- **Each valley holds a share of the scene's pool, not its own chain**: M1.13a's chain (the night's cooling, the inversion, the sun's
  heat) runs once for the scene's valley; the others take its depth times their hold, at most their cap, and the sun's heat breaks a
  shallow one sooner (`1 − q/r²`). One chain per valley would need a temperature per floor that the sliders do not hold.
- **A strong deck's top is flat; its edges and a weak mist fray** (from the user's plan update of 2026-10-26 and the Sunnegga
  webcam's `12-10-2026-0740` and `25-10-2026-1700`): under a strong inversion the fog's top is flat and sharp and its body opaque,
  its edges frayed where it laps the slopes. The mist's wisps and patches take the larger of its edge's share (1 where the ground
  rises to its top, 0 80 m under it) and its weakness (1 less its water over the cap), so a deck at the cap over a valley's middle
  takes none of them, its edges and a thinner mist all. The haze's top keeps its wisps everywhere: a pool of haze is the weak case.
  The noise's own strength (25 m wisps of 220 m, patches of 0.8 over 1.8 km) stays as built: at the sheet's 320 px the stronger
  ones barely showed (`m1-13b-noise.png`), and the webcam's fog asks for a flat top, not a stronger one.
- **The mist is uniform under a sharp top, not thinning to it**: a fog layer deeper than a few tens of metres is mixed by its own
  turbulence (Roach et al. 1976), and from above it shows a surface. A mist whose water fell to none at its top read as more haze.
- **Its water is capped at 0.075 g/m³ (about 200 m to see)**: denser fog, ground fog that buries the camera and the sea of clouds
  are M4's; a pinned `--mist` takes 0.2–5 km and fills 60% of the scene's pool.
- **The pools end 25 m under the floor**: the march never looks under the ground, but the reference, which ignores the terrain in
  the air, found pools hundreds of metres deep under the floors and scattered light from them.
- **The tables carry the pools' average as their flat layer**: on the misty ridges the march ran +2.4% against the reference with
  it and +5.6% with twice it, the haze alone +3.7% and +6.8%. Twice it had looked right (the march 4–8% dark with the average alone)
  while the reference still found pools hundreds of metres deep under the floors and scattered light from them; with the pools
  ending 25 m under the floor, the average is the better.
- **The volume reaches the pools' highest reach, and the tables' light scattered many times keeps half of itself in full shadow**:
  the plan's "lifts M1.4's lit-air limit for the low air". On the misty ridges at 07:30 the shadowed reference, which shades every
  event under the pools' reach, is 24% darker than the plain one; shading the air over the pools as well as in them moved the march
  only from +24.0% to +23.3% over it, so most of the gap is light scattered many times. Dimming the tables' share of it in shadow
  to 0.5 took the march to +19%, to 0 +15%; the rest is the sky's light on the surfaces and along the horizon, which the sky view
  lights as if no ridge stood (a limit below). Half, not none: a mist in a ridge's shadow keeps the sky's light, white-grey under it
  in the webcam's frames, and none would leave it dark.
- **The mist's light scattered many times is weighted by the sun's elevation** (`ForwardLobeWeight` 2·μ₀^¼ on the second octave,
  `DiffuseWeight` 1.6·μ₀ on the rest): the octaves were made for clouds lit along the sun's path; a deck is a slab wider than it is
  deep, which catches the sun per unit of its top. Against a Monte Carlo of a slab (`vista-views/pools/mist-slab.py`: g 0.85,
  optical depth 1–6, sun 3–45°, views 3–12° down at every azimuth) plain octaves ran 0.52–5.53× the truth (the worst a low sun
  behind the camera), the weighted ones 0.61–1.94×. A δ-Eddington diffuse field instead missed the views toward the sun by half (its
  two terms cannot hold a lobe of g 0.85). Against the 3D reference over the mist lake (a deck 243 m deep, 600 m to see, side-lit)
  the weighted octaves ran −8% at a 6° sun and +12% at 25° on the frame, the plain ones +11% and +10% (with the tables' layer then
  twice the pools' average; with the final settings −8.8% and +10.2%); no weighting of the octaves and the sky's light did better at
  both (the misfit is in the limits below).
- **Steps near a mist shrink to its density** (an optical depth of 0.5 of the mist the last step found, at most 50 m in its band):
  the plan's "more steps inside the mist"; through the haze alone a step is 150 m. With coarser steps on bounces and toward the sky
  this took the misty ridges from 2.8× to 2.2× the flat layer (both in one shader then), the deck checks unchanged (+12.3% and
  −7.9%).
- **The sunlight volume is every second cell, 24 layers following the floors**: 128 m over the world and 512 m over the far field,
  0.02–0.08 s of GPU a render; its shadow agrees with a height-field march from the same voxels at 95% of them (the rest along the
  shadows' edges, where a voxel straddles them).
- **The pools' code is a shader variant, not a branch**: compiled in, it cost every view 40% of its speed, pools or none (the aerial
  view 9.9 against `main`'s 7.0 ns a sample, `--pools flat` too), its registers spent whether a branch took it or not; out of the
  plain integrator, a view without pools is back to 7.0.
- **The GPU takes half its time by default** (asked for mid-slice): `VISTA_GPU_SHARE=1` gives the old speed. The images are the
  same bit for bit; only the wall time doubles.

*M1.13b gotchas:*

- **The atmosphere's constants outgrew 256 bytes**: the layout check (`CheckConstantLayouts`) caught it, and their buffer is now
  512 bytes. The integrator's root signature is full (64 of 64 words): the low air took its last slot (t24), one buffer for the
  cells, both volumes and the ceiling map.
- **The volume's haze channel is a difference of two large columns**: the pools' haze column toward the sun less the tables'
  layer's, each about a hundred times their difference near the floor, so its parity check scales its tolerance with the layer's
  column; the GPU's sun at a voxel also tilts with the Earth's curvature (a frame point 30 km out sees the sun 0.27° higher), which
  the C# twin (`LowAir.LocalToSun`) had to follow before the columns agreed.
- **The sky counted the pools twice**: the sky view carries the tables' layer, and the sky's light on a surface in a pool added the
  pool's own column on top (+6.4% on the misty ridges' sky); the surface's sky now takes the pool's column less the layer's, and a
  camera ray to the sky marches the pools with the layer taken out.
- **A path-traced reference through dense mist costs forty times a clear one**: a path scatters hundreds of times (albedo 0.9999)
  and the majorant holds the mist's densest over every segment below the pools' reach; a 96 px reference of a deck 300 m to see was
  stopped after 40 minutes. The deck checks run at 64 px and 256 samples, 600 m to see.
- **The GPU's timings drift with its share and its other programs**: alternate rounds and medians; one render of the misty view
  read 44.7 ns a sample and the next 22.9. A render's first dispatch was a whole planned batch (1.7 s on a deck's reference) until
  it became a probe.
- **Fiordland's autumn is April**: the October recipes put its views in spring (with an "implausible" warning); its checks pass
  `--date 2026-04-15`.
- **Do not build while a measurement runs**: the running Vista.App holds its DLLs and the build fails to copy them (MSB3021).

*Known limits:*

- **The deck's brightness is within about 25% of the reference, not 3%**: over the mist lake the weighted octaves and the sky's
  light run +10% on the frame at a 25° sun (+17% on the deck 2–4 km out, +9% on the sky, where far decks lie on the horizon) and
  −9% at 6° (−26% on the nearest deck). The sky's light on the mist is the tables' haze radiance dimmed by the mist above, about 40%
  of a deck's light: it weighs the horizon's bright sky that a deck's own body hides. A deck's light per direction (a table of the
  slab's Monte Carlo, or a two-stream field with its forward lobe) is M4's, with fog proper.
- **The sky's light in a ridge's shadow is still the open sky's**: the sky view and the surfaces' sky light know no terrain, so a
  shaded valley's floor and the horizon over shaded valleys take the sky of a world without ridges. On the misty ridges at 07:30
  (a low October sun, the pools reaching 3.7 km, most of the low air in shadow) the march stands +19% over the shadowed reference,
  the sky +11%, where the volume and the halved light scattered many times have taken their share. Parked (§10).
- **Thin mist, not fog**: at most 0.075 g/m³ (about 200 m to see); ground fog that buries the camera and the sea of clouds are M4's.
- **The pools' depth comes from one chain**: every valley takes a share of the scene's valley's pool by its shape; a high basin as
  cold as a deep one, or a valley drier than its neighbour, is not modelled.
- **The far field's pools are coarse**: 256 m cells of the base terrain, which is not eroded; its floors are the ground's own
  height, so a far pool follows the terrain more than a valley's level floor.
- **Over the plan's cost on a strong pool**: the misty ridges with a strong pool and mist pinned cost 3.1× M1.13a's flat layer
  for the same air (22.5 against 7.2 ns a sample), the golden hour's haze pools 1.84× on the down-slope view; views without pools
  are unchanged. Most of it is the pools' variant itself, not its steps: with one step a segment the golden-hour view still ran 20.1
  ns against 10.5 (the pools' registers in every path's loop, and the pool and volume lookups at each surface). Parked (§10): a
  leaner variant (the march's state in fewer registers, one pool lookup a surface).
