# Vista M1.12a — Colour quadrature

The slice's goal, deliverable and verify are in [`vista-plan.md`](../vista-plan.md) §10, with a status line that links here.
Section references (§n) are to the plan; "the cost ledger" is [`cost-ledger.md`](cost-ledger.md); sheets cited by name are in
[`vista-views/sheets/`](../vista-views/sheets/README.md).

*M1.12a status (2026-10-23): done.* Colour integrates true. Vista traces its own 50 wavelengths: the medians of 50 bins of equal
colour-matching mass (x̄ + ȳ + z̄), 413–661 nm, each weighted by its bin's own integrals of x̄, ȳ and z̄. Every spectrum the CI
checks (flat, M1.12's ground, water, the sun through 1 to 20 air masses, a Rayleigh sky) integrates within 0.93 ΔE₇₆ and 0.44% in
luminance of dense 1 nm integration, where the engine's table missed by 2.3–8.3 ΔE₇₆ and up to 2.7% (each colour judged at the
luminance of white). The twilight light on a 0.9 reflector misses by 1.08, because the last wavelength stands for everything past
650 nm; a last bin of three quarters of a bin's mass would take it to 0.92 (named here, not taken). In the renders each view's colour
moves as its light predicts: the sky 4.16 ΔE₇₆ (a Rayleigh sky predicts 4.14), the daylit ground 3.25–3.32, down the slope at golden
hour 3.96, M1.12's close-ups 2.06–2.60, always away from green; the incident meter's exposure moves 0.001 EV, and the sky view's red
channel at 0 falls from 95.3% of its pixels to 86.2%. `--quadrature engine` renders `main`'s images byte for byte, and a sample costs
what it did. `Vista.Tests` has 758 tests (747 before); `--gpu-selftest` passes 61 checks (58 before).

*What was built:*

- **Vista's own wavelength table** (`Vista.Core/Rendering/WavelengthTable.cs`): `WavelengthTable.Bins` cuts the CIE 1931 2° functions
  (the engine's own 1 nm file, read in double precision by `CieObserver`) into 50 bins of equal x̄ + ȳ + z̄ mass. Each 1 nm sample holds
  its mass evenly over its nanometre, so a sample a bin edge crosses is shared between the two bins. Each entry is the whole nanometre
  holding its bin's median (413, 423, 428, … 643, 661 nm, ascending), weighted by its bin's own integrals of x̄, ȳ and z̄; the
  correction is 50 / Σȳ, so a flat spectrum of 1 is luminance 1 as before and Y keeps its meaning (the ȳ-weighted mean radiance:
  `LuminancePerY` is unchanged). `WavelengthTable.Engine` is the engine's table as `SpectralResourceBaker` bakes it for the maze, to the
  bit; the engine's `WavelengthLookup` is untouched (§9.2). Each table carries its multiple-scattering positions (`MsPositions`, which
  were a static of `AtmosphereScattering`) and its bins' edges.
- **Everything that read the engine's cycle reads the scene's table**: the integrator's CIE table and correction and the materials'
  spectra (`IntegratorTables.Build(materials, wavelengths)`), the colour-to-reflectance basis (`ReflectanceBasis`, a port of the
  engine's `RgbToReflectanceBasis`, whose constructor Vista cannot reach: the same arithmetic step for step, so on the engine's table it
  is the engine's basis to the bit, a test checks), the atmosphere's tables and so its LUTs (`AtmosphereTables.Build(model, table)`),
  the multiple-scattering positions (the C# references `SkyMarch`, `SkyViewTexel` and `GroundIrradianceEntry` take the position as
  `AerialPerspective.Integrate` already did), the incident meter (its card's light is measured at the scene's wavelengths and weighed
  with the scene's tables) and the albedo output (a material with a spectrum gets its colour through the render's table, so the
  albedo the denoiser reads is the colour it renders with; the catalogue's own albedo is the default table's). The shader does not
  change: it already sums `Cie[i]·L(λᵢ)` under one correction and reads each wavelength from `Cie[i].w`.
- **`--quadrature bins|engine`** (default bins) on the preview, a watch's options file and the sheet axis `quadrature=engine,bins`;
  `VistaScene.Quadrature`, in `PreviewSession.RenderKey`. The renderer keys the atmosphere's tables, scattering orders and sky view on
  the quadrature as well as the model (a change of quadrature rebuilds them and the sky distribution, whose weights are the tables'
  too); the session keys the incident measurement on it. **Sidecar schema 18:** `image.quadrature` and `image.wavelengths` (count,
  first and last nm); each material's albedo in `materials.table` is now the one the render used. Sheet labels read it; an older
  sidecar counts as `engine`, so a sheet's title says "engine wavelengths" for it, as it says "lambert diffuse" for pre-M1.12 ones.
- **The colour check** (`QuadratureCheck`): fifteen spectra — flat, M1.12's five ground spectra, ten and two metres of pure water,
  the solar spectrum through 1, 2, 5, 10 and 20 sea-level air masses of the default air, a Rayleigh sky (the solar spectrum times
  (550/λ)⁴) and the twilight light on a 0.9 reflector — each integrated through a table and by dense 1 nm integration
  (`CieObserver.Integrate`), the difference as ΔE₇₆ and a luminance ratio. Each spectrum is scaled to luminance 1 by the dense integral
  and judged against equal-energy white, so a light and a surface are judged alike (the cast a white card would take under that
  light). The twilight light is the sun 1° below the horizon seen from 4000 m: the solar spectrum through the default air along the
  ray toward it, which grazes the air about 3 km above sea level (columns integrated in 20 m steps over the curved Earth), about 22
  sea-level air masses at 550 nm.
- **`Vista.App --quadrature-compare`** takes the preview's own options and renders the view twice in one session, through the engine's
  table and the bins (each with its own incident meter, each writing its usual files), then writes a map of every pixel's ΔE₇₆ between
  them (judged as the check judges a spectrum, against the bins' pixel) and prints the per-pixel mean, the ΔE₇₆ of the frame's mean
  colour and which way it moved, both exposures, and each undithered PNG's share of red channels at 0; it first prints the shift the
  CI's spectra predict for each light. `PreviewSession.LastRender` hands it the render. `vista-views/quadrature/render.ps1` runs every
  check below.
- **`--gpu-selftest`** runs every atmosphere check at the scene's wavelengths, the bins now, and its watch checks grow by three: a
  switch to the engine's table and back each rebuilds the wavelength tables and everything after them and renders as a cold render
  would, and the engine's image differs from the bins' while the bins after it are the first render's bits (61 checks; 58 before).

*M1.12a decisions (revisit freely):*

- **Judged at the luminance of white.** ΔE₇₆ grows with lightness (its chromatic terms scale with the cube root of Y), so the check
  scales every spectrum to Y = 1 against equal-energy white: the strictest reading, and one that treats a light and a surface alike.
  The plan's figures from M1.12 (a flat grey 2.6, the ground 1.7–2.9, water 3.0, the sky 3.4, the sun at air mass 20 4.1, luminance
  within 1.7%) were measured on spectra and at a lightness that log does not record; at a grey of Y = 0.5 the flat case, the ground and
  the sky reproduce them (2.62, 1.80–2.91 and 3.32 here), and the sun and the water there were evidently other spectra (the sun's
  luminance error alone, which the lightness does not touch, is 2.71% here against "within 1.7%"). At Y = 0.5 every figure here is
  0.79 of its value at Y = 1 (the bins' worst held case 0.73, the twilight case 0.86); the ordering and the gain are the same.
- **The wavelength is the whole nanometre holding the bin's median**, as the plan says, so the engine's integer-wavelength
  conventions (`Cie[i].w`, material spectra sampled at it) hold, and the 50 entries stay distinct: the narrowest bin is 3 nm wide.
- **Equal bins, as the plan asked; the twilight case recorded, and its fix named, not taken** (the checks): a narrower last bin is a
  change of the deliverable's "equal mass", and snow's own spectrum (M1.13) re-runs the case. After the review (2026-10-23) the user
  kept the bins equal: the three-quarter bin is a patch that clears only the case it was tuned on (deeper twilight, −1.5° and
  −1.8°, still misses by 2.3 and 6.1 with it). The red end is M1.13's to decide; its attention point in the plan holds the options
  measured that day (more wavelengths, more of the 50 in the red tail, a table tuned to the scene's light or to a blend).
- **The catalogue's albedo is the default table's; a render's is its own.** `VistaMaterial.LinearRgbAlbedo` is the bins' colour of a
  spectrum, and `IntegratorTables.Build` takes it through the render's table, so `--quadrature engine` writes `main`'s albedo output
  and so `main`'s denoised image (the checks).
- **The basis is ported, not shared.** The engine builds `RgbToReflectanceBasis` only from `SpectralResources`, whose constructor is
  internal to `RayTracer.Core`; an `InternalsVisibleTo` would be an engine change for a 40-line fit. The port keeps the engine's float
  arithmetic, so a test holds the two to the bit on the engine's table.

*M1.12a gotchas:*

- **A 16-sample render does not complete the cycle**: each pixel makes 64 visits to the 50 entries, unevenly, so its colour carries
  the quadrature's sampling noise, and a ΔE map of two quadratures at 16 spp is noise. At 50·k samples every pixel completes the
  cycle k times; the sheets use 100.
- **Bash heredocs lose backslashes** (again, twice): a Python edit run from one turned `\\n` in a C# string literal into a newline,
  and its assertion stopped it before anything was written; later a fix to an edit script, run from a heredoc, failed the same way and
  the unfixed script ran after it. Edit scripts with backslashes go through the Write tool, and a scripted edit is read back.
- **A `tail -f` on a log holds it on Windows**: a progress monitor tailing the checks' log made the script's next `Add-Content` fail
  ("in use by another process"), and stopping the monitor left the `tail` running; it had to be killed by hand.
- **`main`'s build has no OIDN** (its binaries live in this worktree's ignored `.deps` and bin): to compare denoised images, copy
  `OpenImageDenoise*.dll` and `tbb*.dll` next to the other build's `Vista.App.dll`.

*Known limits:*

- **The last wavelength stands for everything above 650 nm.** The twilight light, nearly all of it past 650 nm, misses by about a
  ΔE₇₆ (the checks), and so would anything deep red or reflecting strongly in the near infrared at the edge of vision.
- **The maze keeps the cast.** The engine's table is untouched; the engine's `plan.md` could take the same bins (its `DeterXYZ` and
  correction are all it would change).
- **The display still has no white balance** (M1.17): a flat grey, now neutral in XYZ, still renders warm through the display's D65
  matrix; judge greys against that.
