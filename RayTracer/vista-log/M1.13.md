*M1.13 status (2026-10-25): done.* **Glaciers carve the valleys they run in:** after the incision, three glacial cycles of
steady ice (balance flux, shallow-ice thickness) lower the bed under each glacier by its sliding speed where it is thick enough to
slide, and the troughs round from V's into U's: at 24 sections per seed, cut across the valleys glaciers enter, the width at half the
trough's bottom 100 m over its width at 100 m (a V 0.5, a parabola 0.71) has medians 0.80, 0.62, 0.69 and 0.84 on seeds 42, 7, 1234
and 2026 against 0.44–0.48 uncarved, 20–23 of 24 sections rounder (`m1-13-valleys.png`). The relief grows (the 1 km median 541 m
against 486 m on seed 42), the summits nearly keep their height (medians 19–30 m lower), the drainage holds (95.2–96.7% into a
channel, M1.12c 95.8–97.4%), and the footing drops by 45–89 m. **Today's glaciers** are the recipe's (their line 1800 m
above height 0), laid on the eroded world: 2.4–10.8% of it under ice, firn above the line and bare grey ice below it, crevassed
where they steepen (`m1-13-maps.png`). **The season's snow** lies by rule above the snow slider's snowline, lower on poleward faces,
in hollows and in the lee, shed from faces steeper than 35–45° and scoured from ridges, wet below the freezing level; forest stays
dark with a dusting. In April the range views' faces steeper than 50° are 12–17% white where flats are 78–86%

Zermatt's year). **Snow and ice spectra** come from ice's absorption and grain size. **The red end** is decided: a fixed table tuned to

*What was built:*

- **The steady ice** (`Vista.Core/World/GlacierFlow.cs`): the glaciers a climate keeps on a surface, solved for their steady state
  (ice adjusts to its climate in centuries, far faster than it carves). A climate (`GlacierClimate`) is an equilibrium line, a
  mass-balance gradient (0.007 m of ice a year per metre), and caps on a year's accumulation (2 m) and melt (10 m). **Where the ice
  goes** is the balance flux: each cell adds its year's balance times its area to the ice arriving from upstream and passes what is
  left downhill over the *ice's* surface, shared among every lower neighbour by the drop toward it (multiple flow directions), after
  that surface's hollows are filled (the drainage's Priority-Flood+ε), so ice spreads across the valley it fills and spills over
  hollows; a flux that melts to nothing is the snout. **How thick** follows from the shallow-ice approximation: Glen's law (n = 3,
  A = 2.4·10⁻²⁴ Pa⁻³ s⁻¹ for temperate ice, Cuffey and Paterson 2010) and as much again sliding, H = (q / (K·S³))^⅕, iterated with
  damping because the thickness and the surface's slope depend on each other. The target surface is averaged over a 5 × 5 tent (320 m
  at 64 m, about two ice thicknesses), which a real glacier's longitudinal stresses do and without which ice piled up in spikes along
  the thalwegs. Ground steeper than 40° holds no ice (its snow avalanches into the cell below). Each cell
  carries its flux, its thickness, its speed (the flux per metre over the thickness, averaged across the glacier over a 9 × 9 tent:
  a valley glacier slides as a plug, not fastest along its thalweg) and its glacier's **body** (the thickness averaged the same way,
  so a valley glacier's walls share its middle's). Pure and deterministic: one routing order, and the fifth root by Newton's iteration
  from two correctly rounded square roots, so no library's last bit moves a world.
- **Glacial carving** (`StreamPower.Incise`, `Carve`; `ErosionSettings.Glacial*`): after the incision's last fluvial step and its
  remap to the base terrain's range, on the finished world in its own metres and without uplift, 60 steps (three glacial cycles of
  20) each solve the steady ice of their climate on the incision's 64 m grid, starting from the step before's ice, and lower the bed
  under it. The cycle's equilibrium line swings smoothly from 900 m above height 0 (the glaciers at their largest, 900 m below
  today's line, as the Alps' stood about a thousand metres lower at the last glacial maximum) to 1500 m and back (`CarvingClimate`, a
  smoothstep of a triangle wave: arithmetic, no cosine). The cut is proportional to the sliding speed
  (Humphrey and Raymond 1994: 12 m a step at 100 m a year) where the glacier's body is thick enough to be warm at its bed (full from
  150 m), over the bed slope's cosine on a trough's walls (up to twice), less on hard rock (the hardness erodibility, as the rivers'),
  fading out within 80 m below the spill of the hollow it digs (`OverdeepeningMetres`) and over the glacier's last 8 cells
  (`EdgeCells`); every cut is worked out first and then applied, so no cell sees a neighbour's half-made step. The rivers cut where
  the ice is not (10 m or less), every slope keeps its talus, and the erosion's pools fill the overdeepenings with sediment: flat
  floors, and a few shallow lakes (0.0–0.2% of the grid). The GPU erosion that follows rounds what the carving left at 8 m as it always did.
- **Today's glaciers** (`Vista.Core/World/Glaciers.cs`, `ErosionResult.Ice`, `ErosionSettings.GlacierElaMetres`): the recipe's own,
  their equilibrium line in the world's frame (1800 m above height 0 for the mountain set pieces, `Hills` none), so they stand
  whatever the place, the season and the snow slider. Solved (60 iterations) on the eroded 8 m ground averaged onto the incision's
  64 m grid, and laid on the 8 m ground as the smooth surface bilinear between those samples, faded in over the solve's first 5 m:
  rock ribs stand through the ice and its edges meet the ground. Where the laid surface holds a closed hollow the ice fills it to its
  spill (a steady glacier's surface has none: its ice would flow into it). The world's surface is the ground plus the ice (the mesh
  and the materials see it); its drainage stays the ground's, so meltwater and the rivers the ice would dam run under it, along its
  bed, and leave at its snout. The ice's thickness is a field of the world (`ErosionResult.Ice`), cached with it (world file format
  4, which still reads format 3's worlds as glacier-less) and in its hash. `WorldBuilder` lays them after the GPU erosion
  (`ErosionStats.GlacierSeconds`).
- **The season's snow and the glaciers by rule** (`Vista.Core/World/MaterialRules.cs`, `MaterialField.cs`, the shader's twin
  `Vista.App/Shaders/VistaMaterials.hlsli`; parity-checked by the self-test, whose probe now reads snow, firn and ice): three new
  weights over the ground the rules already lay.
  - *Snow* lies above the snow slider's snowline (above sea level, the weather chain's), over a 60 m band: 250 m lower on a fully
    poleward face (the hemisphere from the latitude) and as much higher on an equator-facing one; 150 m lower in a full hollow (the
    ground 6 m below its 72 m surroundings) and higher on a full ridge, which also loses 60% of its snow to the wind; 100 m higher
    on a face fully into the prevailing westerlies and lower in their lee; ragged by a coarse noise (±120 m at 60 m) and a fine one
    (±40 m at 11 m, twice that where the snow is wet: patchy melt near the freezing level). It sheds from slopes between 35° and 45°
    (8° steeper in a full hollow: couloirs keep their snow).
  - *Wet or dry:* wet below the freezing level, dry above it, over 300 m (two of the catalog's four snows).
  - *Firn and ice* lie where the world's glaciers are (from 0.5 m of ice, all of it from 4 m), firn above the glaciers' own line
    (the recipe's, at the scene's altitude, ±40 m and a ±60 m noise), bare ice below it, grey with debris where it thins below 60 m
    toward the edges and snout, and cracked into crevasse fields where it flows over ground steeper than 8–14° (to 32–40°; in the
    shading only). The season's snow lies over them as over the rock.
  - *The forest* keeps its ground and its canopy under snow cover; the snow shows in 15% of its texture (the gaps between crowns),
    as in the user's April photos.
- **The spectra** (`Vista.Core/Rendering/SnowSpectrum.cs`, `MaterialCatalog` ids 6–9: snow, glacier ice, firn, wet snow): ice's
  absorption (Picard et al. 2016 from 300 to 600 nm, Warren and Brandt 2008 beyond) through Kokhanovsky and Zege's asymptotic
  radiative transfer, r = exp(−√((α_ice + α_imp)·13.1·d)), with dust as an absorbing impurity: dry snow 0.2 mm grains, wet snow
  1.5 mm, firn 2.5 mm, glacier ice 25 mm (dusty, about half its light). Snow bumps little (smooth wind-packed noise); the BRDF is
  Lambertian or nearly (M1.12's rough diffuse with a small roughness).
- **Per-place precipitation** (`ClimateNormals.Precipitation`, `ClimateTable`, `SnowCover`): each dropdown place carries twelve
  monthly totals (1991–2020 where the station has them), with a source and the precipitation station's altitude; the degree-day
  snowpack takes each day's rate between the months' middles, 2.5% more per 100 m above the station (Huss and Hock 2015's upper
  value), up to twice the station's, and counts snowfall 1.2 times what the gauge caught (Sevruk 1985). A custom latitude keeps
  M1.11's 3 mm a day everywhere.
- **The red end** (`WavelengthTable.Blend`, `TwilightLight`, `QuadratureCheck`): the default quadrature is now the blend (below);
  M1.12a's equal bins stay as `--quadrature bins`, the red-tail split as `--quadrature redTail`.
- **The measures:** `ValleyShape` and `--valley-measure` (the troughs where glaciers run, carved against uncarved: the width at half of
  the trough's bottom 100 m over the width at the whole of it, and Harbor's power b from the same two widths); `SnowBySlope` in the
  sidecar's `materials.snowBySlope` (the share of the terrain's pixels that are snow, firn or ice in slope bands 0–30–40–50–90°, from
  the material-id and normal outputs); `--erosion-measure` lays the glaciers and prints the glaciated share and the snow, firn and
  ice shares at the place.
- **The sidecar** (schema 21): `world.glaciers` (their line, the share of the world they cover, their volume, the thickest ice),
  the snow's sliders and the freezing level, `materials.snowBySlope`, the quadrature `blend`; the erosion's settings carry the
  glacial ones.
- **`--snow on | off`**: off lays no seasonal snow (the glaciers stay); the `snow` sheet axis.
- **The views re-placed**: the carving buried or left high M1.12c's default, sky, down-slope and materials views and the forested
  crest. The default stands on a meadow at (4450, −8825), 1698.5 m (6 m above the eroded ground, 2 m above the incised ground CI

*M1.13 decisions (revisit freely):*

- **The carving glaciers are large: their line swings from 900 to 1500 m above height 0**, 300–900 m below today's 1800 m. With
  the line at 1500 m (1200–1800) the troughs rounded only a little (the half-depth ratio's median 0.61, 0.48, 0.57 and 0.51 on the
  four seeds against 0.44–0.49 uncarved) and twice the cutting rate barely helped (seed 42 0.62, seed 7 0.51); the carving's glaciers
  were too thin to be warm at their beds over most of their valleys. Lowering the line: 1300 m gave 0.72, 0.55, 0.65 and 0.78, 1200 m
  0.80 and 0.62 on seeds 42 and 7. On seed 42's whole world 1200 m is also the best of the three: the most relief (1 km median 541 m
  against 528 m at 1500 m), the summits kept (median 1500 m against 1485 m), the fewest walls at their talus angle (11.0% within 2°
  against 13.5%), the drainage the same (95.7%).
- **The carving comes after the incision, on the finished world.** Carving interleaved with the incision, under its uplift, planed
  the massif: the remap to the base terrain's range then compressed the relief (42% of seed 42 glaciated, its footing up from 696 to
  1090 m). After the remap, in the world's own metres, the glaciers carve the relief the world will have, and the peaks keep their
  height.
- **Steady glaciers, not their history.** Each carving step solves the ice its climate keeps; no ice is advected between steps. The
  carving's sixty steps stand in for three glacial cycles; the swing of the equilibrium line moves the glaciers' snouts up and down
  their valleys so the whole of each trough is carved, not one band of it.
- **The glacier carves as a plug.** Carving by the local speed (fastest along the thalweg) cut slot canyons; real valley glaciers
  slide nearly as fast at their margins as in their middle, which is what widens a trough into a U. The speed and the warm bed are
  the glacier's, averaged over its width; the wall factor (1/cos, up to 2) cuts a wall along its own slope as the ice presses on it.
- **Overdeepenings stop 80 m below their spill**, then fill with sediment: real troughs hold overdeepened lakes and flat floors,
  but an unlimited cut dug pits 535–1281 m deep where ice converged.
- **The water runs under the ice.** With the drainage taken over the laid ice, seed 42's share draining into a channel fell from 95.8%
  to 87.3% and lakes covered 0.62% of the grid: 52 806 cells of ponds on the ice's own surface (where the smooth surface dipped
  between the solve's samples or behind ribs) and about 35 000 cells of new lakes on ground the ice dammed. The carved ground alone
  drains 95.5%, lakes 0.09%. Filling the ice's hollows took the ponds away (89.4%, lakes 0.30%); the ice-dammed lakes are real things
  but short-lived, and their water mostly finds its way under the ice, so the drainage follows the bed: 95.7%, lakes 0.0%.
- **Today's glaciers are the recipe's**, in the world's frame, not the place's or the season's: the user's call in the plan (snow at
  zero leaves them). A Zermatt July and a Lofoten January show the same ice; the firn line is theirs too.
- **Snow is a material, not geometry**: no depth, no cornices, no drifts as shapes (M7's close-up snow).
- **The forest keeps its colour** under snow cover with a 15% dusting, as in the user's photos; no snow on branches (M7).
- **The red end: the blend** (§11): its table is tuned to white light and 30% of the −1.8° twilight sun, the deepest twilight the
  renders show; the check's other twilights, snow and ice and twice-bounced grass were not used to tune it. It costs nothing per
  sample (the same 50 wavelengths, other places and weights), and it integrates every material and the skies away from twilight
  within 0.19 ΔE₇₆ of dense integration (the equal bins within 0.93).
- **The alpenglow sheet is M1.10a's sweep** (the range view 3.4 km up on a July evening, the sun setting from +4° to −3°), not the
  default view at dawn: the default faces north-north-west, away from a July dawn's sun, so its summits stay in shadow until the sun is up.
  Dawn and dusk are the same light mirrored.

*M1.13 gotchas:*

- **Constants in code still move the world without moving its key.** Every carving constant tuned served stale worlds and stale
  incised caches under the same settings hash (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Vista\worlds` and the measure's camlab cache); they were deleted after
  each change. The shipped settings hash anew (the glacial settings are in it), and with them off the hash and the world are
  M1.12c's.
- **A measure stored a glacier-less world under the glaciated key**: `--erosion-measure` eroded without `Glaciers.Lay` while the key
  said glaciers. It lays them now, as the builder does; so does the self-test's second erosion (its bit-identity check failed until
  then).
- **The watch check expected the bins**: it switches the engine's wavelengths on and back to the default's, which is now the blend.
- **Re-placed cameras are underground in `main`'s world**: `main`'s cells in the views sheet and its cost runs use `main`'s own camera
  files (`render.ps1`'s `$beforeViews`, beside the `-Before` build); the first views sheet had a black cell.
- **The valley measure moved with the carving**: sections taken where the carved world's glaciers ran moved with every change, and
  cuts along the drainage's eight directions crossed troughs obliquely. The sections are taken on the uncarved world's glaciers, in
  its mountain valleys (300 m of relief within 1 km), and each is cut in the narrowest of twelve directions.
- **Windows PowerShell's `*>` writes UTF-16**, and its pipes turn `°`, `³` and `≥` into mojibake; the scripts' own lines go to
  `snow.log` in UTF-8.
- **Re-carving moved the views again**: with the carving's line at 1200 m the default's ground rose 17 m and the meadow, rock-face
  and scree-cone views ended 36–57 m underground; the default and sky views were raised, and the meadow and scree views re-aimed by a
  sweep of yaw and pitch at their spot until the frame's middle landed on their material (the scree cone's old spot has none in any
  direction now).
- **Watching the script's log locks it on Windows:** a `tail -F` on `snow.log` held the file, and the script's next `Add-Content`
  stopped the run twice (its `$ErrorActionPreference` is `Stop`). Watch the processes, not the log.
- **Bash heredocs** cut Python edit scripts short at backslashes again; they went through files.

*Known limits:*

- **Cirques, arêtes and horns are not built as such.** The carving deepens glaciers' heads where their ice is thick and the
  ridges between neighbouring troughs sharpen as the troughs widen, but nothing shapes a cirque's bowl or a horn, and no measure
  checks them.
- **The smallest glaciers barely carve**: seed 7, the lowest world, rounds its troughs only halfway (0.62); a glacier carves where it
  is thick enough to slide on a warm bed (full from 150 m).
- **Trough walls stand at their talus angle** more often: 11.0–15.3% of the 8 m slopes within 2° of it, against 5.3–7.2%; the
  carving cuts walls and the talus rule lays them back.
- **Glaciers are steady**, the carving's and today's: no moraines, no trimlines, no hanging valleys made on purpose (tributaries carved
  less may hang), no proglacial lakes; the ice's water runs under it.
- **Today's ice is one smooth surface**: no icefalls or seracs as geometry, crevasses only in its shading, debris only at its thin
  edges; its edges fade over 5 m of the solve's ice.
- **Today's glaciers do not follow the place**: their line is the recipe's, 1800 m above height 0, so a sea-level Lofoten world keeps
  glaciers whose line stands 1800 m up, and a July and a January share them.
- **Snow is a material on the ground** (M7's close-up snow): no depth, no cornices or drifts as shapes, no sparkle, no snow on
  branches. One prevailing wind (westerly) for every place.
- **The sheets cannot show the deepest twilight**: the highest summits in view are lit at −0.5° and dark at −1°, so the red end's
  choice rests on the spectral check.
- **The tools that bypass the sliders still do not anchor** (M1.12c): the roulette measure ran at the valley floor's base (1608 m),
  where more of the world is above the snowline than at the anchored base.
