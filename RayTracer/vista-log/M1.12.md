# Vista M1.12 — Materials by rule

The slice's goal, deliverable and verify are in [`vista-plan.md`](../vista-plan.md) §10, with a status line that links here.
Section references (§n) are to the plan; "the cost ledger" is [`cost-ledger.md`](cost-ledger.md); sheets cited by name are in
[`vista-views/sheets/`](../vista-views/sheets/README.md).

*M1.12 status (2026-10-22): done.* The ground has materials by rule: **rock** (a pale hard rock and a dark soft one, mixed by the
hardness of the bed the surface cuts, so the bands line up with the ledges M1.11d's hardness shaped), **scree**, **soil** and
**grass**, and how **wet** it is, blended per hit from the slope, the altitude, the aspect, the erosion's loose material, the land
draining through a place and the water that stood there. The rules live in `Vista.Core` (`MaterialRules`) and the shader evaluates
the same arithmetic per hit from constants C# derives once; `--gpu-selftest` checks the two agree at 4096 places of the world
(worst 0.0013 of a weight). Their spectra are built in (tier 5) and recorded per material in the sidecar (schema 17, with the rules,
the grass line and, for a dry run, the shares of the world each material covers). Diffuse surfaces are rough by default (Oren–Nayar
in Fujii's form; `--diffuse lambert`). Near the camera the mesh adds detail by material — sharp crests on rock (8 m to 2 m), stones
on scree (1.6 m), M1.9's smooth noise on soil and grass — each kind's error bound measured as M1.9's was, blended by the rules'
weights and bounded per region; below 2 m the shading carries the roughness (a bump: ridged on rock, stones on scree, tufts on grass,
and the laminae stepping hard rock faces). `--materials off` renders `main`'s image to float rounding (the default view's PNG byte
for byte). `Vista.Tests` has 747
tests (717 before); `--gpu-selftest` passes 58 checks (55 before).

*What was built:*

- **The rules** (`Vista.Core/World/MaterialRules.cs`): `MaterialRules` holds the thresholds (degrees, metres, hardness), `Evaluate`
  the rule in single precision, `MaterialConstants` the 16 rows of the shader's `MaterialConstants` cbuffer derived from them (and
  from the scene: the grass line, the pole, the noise's seed; the field: its map and bed table; the camera: its place split into whole
  10 cm cells and the rest, and its pixel's angle, for the bump). `MaterialNoise` is gradient noise on the integrator's 32-bit PCG
  hash, so the shader computes the very same breakup.
- **The world's field** (`MaterialField.cs`): per world-grid sample the bed-table coordinate of height 0 and four byte codes (loose
  depth, upstream area, water depth, mottle), the bed table with each bed's hardness and two tints (`RockHardness.Tints`, new), the
  detail kinds' weights per sample, and per cell the kinds and weight steps the regional bound reads. `Inputs` decodes as the shader
  does; `SampleWeights` evaluates every sample for the dry run's map and shares. Built once per world in `WorldCache.Materials()` (the
  world's rock laid out again from its seed and frame, `WorldBuilder.StrataOf`), about a second for the 4097² grid: the hierarchy's
  3.0–3.5 s with `--materials off` become 4.1–4.3 s.
- **The shading** (`Vista.App/Shaders/VistaMaterials.hlsli`, included by the integrator): the noise, the decode, the rules
  (`MaterialWeightsOf`), the boundaries sharpened, the colours (rock by bed, stony soil, drier meadow patches, wet darkening), the
  bump, the material id (the largest weight; rock hard or soft by its bed) and the rough diffuse BRDF's two terms per companion
  wavelength. The integrator shades every hit through it (`SurfaceAt`) and uses the bumped normal on the first. `CSMaterialProbe`
  evaluates the rules at given places for the self-test. Three root parameters (the map `t22`, the bed table `t23`, the constants
  `b3`): the root signature is at 62 of its 64 words.
- **Spectra** (`MaterialCatalog.Ground`): rock.hard, rock.soft, scree, soil and grass, each a list of (nm, reflectance) points, tier
  5; `IntegratorTables` samples them at the hero wavelengths, derives their albedo through its own quadrature, and carries each
  material's roughness in the albedo buffer's fourth float.
- **The detail by material** (`MaterialDetail.cs`, `RockDetail.cs`, `ScreeDetail.cs`, `ISurfaceDetail.cs`): the hierarchy and the mesh
  take any `ISurfaceDetail`; `TerrainHierarchy.SubGridError` asks the detail for its bound in a box around each vertex below the grid.
- **CLI, sidecar, sheets**: `--materials on|off`, `--diffuse orenNayar|lambert` (and as sheet axes, `materials` and `diffuse`); the
  dry run's `<out>.materials.png`; sidecar schema 17 (`materials`: by rule or not, the table with roughness and albedo, the rules, the
  grass line and its source, the pole, the detail's amplitudes and gradients, a dry run's shares; `image.diffuse`; `world.mesh.materials`);
  `--debug material` in the legend colours; `--diffuse-compare`; the sheet labels name the materials and the diffuse model.

*M1.12 decisions (revisit freely):*

- **The rules run per hit, from one definition.** `MaterialRules.Evaluate` and the shader's `MaterialWeightsOf` are the same
  operations in single precision, on constants C# derives once (`MaterialConstants`, the shader's `b3` cbuffer, now full at 256
  bytes); the inputs are read alike (`MaterialField.Inputs`, `MaterialInputsAt`): the slope from the world grid's normal map (not the
  detailed shading normal, so a material does not follow the detail's bumps), the maps bilinear on their codes. A mesh vertex's
  detail weights come from the same rule at the grid's samples.
- **The world carries its maps to the GPU, 8 bytes a sample** (`MaterialField`): the bed-table coordinate of height 0 (a float: the
  beds dip and fold and rise with the uplift, so a coarser grid would bend them by metres) and four codes, log-scaled loose depth and
  water depth (1 cm to 100 m), upstream area (10 m² to 10⁹ m²) and the beds' mottle; 134 MB for the 4097² grid, plus the bed table
  (hardness and two tints per entry, 256 KB). The maps fade into the defaults over the grid's outer 2 km, as its surface fades into
  the base terrain's. A world that was not eroded still has its beds. A cached world's rock is laid out again from the seed and the
  frame its incision left (`WorldBuilder.StrataOf`, `StreamPower.UpliftField`): no cache format change.
- **The rules.** Bare rock above 40° ± 5° for rock of middling hardness, 6° earlier on the hardest beds and 6° later on the softest,
  and hard beds crop out through thin cover from 24°; scree where the erosion's loose material (15 cm to 80 cm and more) lies at the
  talus angle (24–30° and up) or in a stream's channel (10⁵ to 10⁶ m² upstream); the rest meadow below the grass line on slopes
  under 36–46°, with bare patches, else soil — stony (coloured toward scree) above the line and on slopes too steep for grass; wet
  in streams that are not faces and where the erosion's water stood 0.5–1.5 m deep. Two noises (60 m and 11 m) move every threshold.
- **The grass line comes from the place's warmest month** (`GrassLine`): where its normal mean, along the lapse rate, falls to 6 °C
  (Zermatt 2892 m), 200 m lower on a fully poleward face and higher on an equator-facing one. It is the scene's (`GroundCover` on
  `VistaScene`, part of the render key), not the world's: the detail treats soil and grass alike, so the mesh does not depend on it.
- **Rock takes its colour from its bed:** the bed's hardness mixes the soft rock's spectrum into the hard rock's, the bed's two tints
  (`RockHardness.Tints`, from the beds' own stream) brighten it ±25% and warm or cool it ±15% per 150 nm, and each lamina (about a
  metre) is ±12% lighter or darker. Thin dark partings between laminae were tried and dropped: on a smooth face they read as lines
  ruled on paper.
- **Built-in spectra, physically plausible rather than display-tuned:** rock and debris nearly flat (gneiss and schist debris is grey),
  soil rising toward the red, grass with chlorophyll's absorption and green peak. Through the display, which has no white balance,
  a flat grey comes out warm (equal-energy white as Rec.709 1.20, 0.95, 0.91), so the ground reads beige-grey under a high sun;
  balancing for daylight is a display question for M1.17, not a reason to tint the spectra.
- **Rough diffuse surfaces by default** (Oren–Nayar in Fujii's form, `f = ρ·A·(1 + σ·s/t)`, A normalised so the albedo stays near ρ;
  roughness rock 0.6, scree 0.75, soil 0.5, grass 0.4). The bounce still samples the cosine lobe and weighs the direction it chose;
  Russian roulette decides on ρ·A. With Lambert (σ = 0, or `--materials off`) the integrator computes exactly M1.11d's products.
- **Detail by material in the mesh, to 2 m; roughness in the shading below.** Rock's crests (ridged noise folded about zero, the crest
  rounded over 0.12 of the noise's range) from the grid's cell down three octaves, 5% of each wavelength high; scree's stones (one per
  1.6 m cell, a squat rounded pyramid of its own size, height, stretch and turn, smooth-maxed with its neighbours) on half the smooth
  detail; soil and grass M1.9's noise. Each kind's midpoint envelope was measured as M1.9's was (2 M random segments at eleven lengths,
  ×1.15; below the first, the curvature bound with the kind's measured largest second derivative ×1.15) and a test checks it on fresh
  segments. Finer crests and stones were tried and cut: their bound alone set the finest triangles over everything near the camera.
- **The kinds blend by the rules' weights, averaged over 7×7 samples** with weights under 5% dropped, so they change slowly and an
  outcrop's trace does not spread over its meadow; the bound adds each kind's amplitude times how far its weight can change over half
  an edge. **Bounded per region:** a vertex below the grid takes the kinds and the weight gradients of a box around it that holds its
  edge and every descendant's (half-widths 2^(t+1) − 1 and 3·2^t − 1 sub-units), so the boxes nest and the mesh stays crack-free; a
  meadow near the camera is refined as M1.9's smooth detail was. Grid-level vertices keep the bound everywhere.
- **Below 2 m the shading carries the roughness**, on a path's first surface only (later surfaces see the mesh's normal): a triplanar
  bump of ridged noise on rock and scree and smooth noise on soil, tufts (0.1 m and 0.2 m) on grass, scree's stones as facets of
  their own tilt (0.4, 0.8 and 1.6 m) with slightly dark gaps, and on hard rock the laminae stepping the face (its slope swung by ±45%
  through each lamina: treads and risers). Each octave fades where a pixel covers more than a quarter of it, the footprint stretched
  as the view grazes the ground; a bumped normal is turned back until it faces the camera. Strata steps in the geometry were rejected:
  a terrace of height grows its bound with the slope squared, which would refine every steep face near the camera to the finest level.
- **Boundaries are sharpened in the shading,** after the checked rule: each weight to the sixth power, renormalised, after a fine noise
  (2.5 m and 0.6 m) pushes rock and scree against the ground, so a boundary near the camera is ragged and crisp instead of an 8 m blend.
  A meadow is patchily drier (toward the soil's colour, a 7 m noise).
- **`--materials off` is M1.11d's renderer:** M0's single material, Lambert, and M1.9's smooth detail (the hierarchy is built per
  materials setting).

*M1.12 gotchas:*

- **`line` is a keyword in HLSL** (geometry shaders' primitive type): a local called `line` fails to compile.
- **HLSL's `smoothstep` is undefined for a falling edge** (`a > b`); the rules use one, so both sides use their own `SmoothStep`.
- **The 50 hero wavelengths span 419–651 nm**: a spectrum's shape beyond them (grass's red edge) never reaches the image.
- **A flat spectrum renders warm** (the display has no white balance); judge greys against that, not against sRGB grey.
- **Bump maps at a grazing view shade facets black** where their normal turns away from the camera (a real stone would be hidden by
  its neighbour); and an isotropic footprint aliases into streaks there. Turn the normal back, and stretch the footprint by 1/cos.
- **Front light flattens a bump**: the scree loupe looked like paving with the sun behind the camera and like rubble with it ahead and
  to the side. The loupes use 10:30 in July.
- **The default and down-slope views stand above the grass line** (3215 m and 3509 m at Zermatt): they show debris and rock, and the
  meadows only in the valleys of the aerial and range views.
- **The averaging of the detail weights spreads every outcrop** three samples around it; without the 5% floor the mesh refined the
  meadows around rock as rock (the meadow close-up: 33.5 M triangles at 4K against 22.8 M).
- **PowerShell flattens `@(@(...))`**: a one-element list of lists becomes the inner list (`@(, @(...))` keeps it).
- **Rebuilding `Vista.App` while a script renders with it** kills the script mid-run.
- **`Vista.Tests` copies only some view folders** (`vista-views/*.json`, `atmosphere/`, `aerial/`, and now `materials/`); a view
  elsewhere is not checked by CI.
- **Bash heredocs lose backslashes** (again): a Python edit run from one dropped `\\n` from a C# string; write such scripts with the
  Write tool.

*Known limits:*

- **Grass is a material**, its blades M5's: near the camera a meadow reads as a carpet; no flowers, no season (its spectrum is July's).
- **The grass line follows the warmest month alone**, so maritime places put it high (Lofoten 1211 m; its alpine vegetation ends
  lower), and wind, snow lie and soil are not in it.
- **The bump is the first surface's only**; a reflected or bounced path sees the mesh's own normals. Scree's stones and rock's steps
  have no silhouettes or shadows of their own below the mesh's detail.
- **Wet ground is only darker**: no sheen, no puddles (water is M3's).
- **The mesh costs more near the ground**: 1.5–2.0 times `--materials off`'s triangles for a 4K view near the ground (the default view
  15.5 M against 10.3 M, the scree cone 24.2 M against 13.0 M), and the world's field takes a second more to build.
- **A sample costs 40–55% more on the ground** (the cost ledger), nearly all of it the materials' evaluation at each hit; fewer reads
  and noises per hit are the levers.
- **The colour quadrature leaves a cast** (§11, measured here): 1.7–4.1 ΔE₇₆ on these spectra and the sky against dense integration,
  0.0–0.9 with the same wavelengths weighted by their bins; proposed as a slice of its own.
