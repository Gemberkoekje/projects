# Ground and water: the project's first shaders

**To understand this, start by reading [`unity/Assets/RF/Art/Shaders/RF_Water.shader`](unity/Assets/RF/Art/Shaders/RF_Water.shader),
then [`unity/Assets/RF/Scripts/Levels/SurfaceLook.cs`](unity/Assets/RF/Scripts/Levels/SurfaceLook.cs)
for every number those shaders are handed, then
[`unity/Assets/RF/Art/Shaders/RF_Mark.shader`](unity/Assets/RF/Art/Shaders/RF_Mark.shader)
for the scorches and wheel tracks.** The rest is wiring: `GeneratedMaterials.EnsureSurfaces`
puts the right shader on each surface, and `SurfaceMesh.Coast` is the one new number the
geometry had to start carrying.

This is [MASTER_PLAN.md § 7](MASTER_PLAN.md#7-ground--water-material-upgrade), all four
bullets, and it is the first time this project has had a shader of its own at all.

---

## How to see it

`Tools > IronFlag > Render Coast Preview` — a new still, and the only view in which any of
this can be judged. The two shots the project already had between them cannot show the
water: the split-screen still frames whichever vehicle the staged match put on the field and
both of them are inland, and the level overview is orthographic from two hundred metres up
with the fog switched off, which is a picture of a map rather than of a sea.

`Tools > IronFlag > Build Particle VFX Preview Scene` grew a fifth row for the marks: one
burn on sand, on grass and on a road — the three should read as *one* stain on three grounds,
which is the whole claim of a multiply-blended mark — and then the wheel-track material as a
strip.

For the marks in motion you have to play. `Sandbox.unity`: drive the tank onto the beach and
put a shell into the sand. The ruts behind you are deep on sand, faint on grass and barely
there on the road, and the shell leaves a burn that fades over twenty-two seconds. Flatten a
building and the burn under the rubble stays for the rest of the match.

EditMode 498/498, PlayMode 176/176.

## What the four bullets turned into

| Plan bullet | What shipped |
|---|---|
| 1. Water shader | `RF_Water.shader`: three crossing swells, fine chop, a sharp sun glint, a fresnel sheen that warms towards the sun, foam, and a shoreward wash. Opaque; no depth texture. |
| 2. Ground micro-detail | `RF_Ground.shader`: two octaves of procedural value noise perturbing the normal, per-surface scale and strength. No albedo change and no texture. |
| 3. Decals | `RF_Mark.shader` + `GroundMark` + `TyreTracks`: multiply-blended flat meshes rather than URP Decal Projectors. |
| 4. Coastline foam | Driven off `SurfaceField.Shore`, baked into UV1 of the water sheets by `SurfaceMesh`. |

Three of the plan's open questions were settled before any code was written, and the fourth
turned out to be wrong.

## The decisions

### Hand-written HLSL, not Shader Graph

The plan flagged this as worth deciding deliberately, and the argument that settled it is one
the project has already made twice: `DebrisBurst.cs` refuses a particle system on the grounds
that it is *"an asset nobody can review in a diff"*, and every tuning table in the game exists
because a handful of numbers read against each other is something a diff can show. A
`.shadergraph` is a serialised node blob — closer to the thing that objection is about than a
texture would have been. So: four plain-text files, and every wave, every threshold and every
falloff is a line somebody can argue with.

### Still no textures — but the reason is not the one you would guess

The project's rule was never "no binary assets". It is *every asset is generated from
committed source*: `.glb` from Blender-Python, `.wav` from SuperCollider `.scd`. A `.png`
generated by a committed C# generator would have been perfectly in keeping, and that was put
on the table explicitly.

What settles it is that there is nothing for a texture to tile against. The map is 240 m
across and the detail on it wants to be measured in tens of centimetres, so a tiling detail
map would repeat some tens of times across one frame and the repeat is the thing everybody
sees. Two octaves of value noise on the world position cost less than the sample would and
cannot be laid at the wrong scale, because the coordinate *is* metres. See
[`RF_Noise.hlsl`](unity/Assets/RF/Art/Shaders/RF_Noise.hlsl), which is the whole of it in
fifty lines.

### The depth-texture shore fade cannot work here, and the map already knew the answer

Plan bullet 1 asks for "depth-based blending via URP's camera depth texture, for a soft shore
edge". It cannot be done on this map, and the reason is geometry rather than pipeline
configuration: **there is nothing underneath the water to fade against.** The sea is a slab
whose *top face* is the surface, so a water pixel's depth and the scene depth behind it are
the same number. Making the water transparent to get around that would put the largest
surface on the map into the transparent queue alongside the smoke and dust particles, and the
band it would buy — the submerged part of the bank, 0.5 m tall, seen at 32 degrees above the
horizontal — measures about half a metre wide.

The soft edge came from somewhere better. `SurfaceField` already computes a signed
distance-to-coast for every cell of the map, for beach widths, shelf widths and every
placement margin; `SurfaceMesh` now writes it into UV1 of the sheets drawn over water, and
the shader drives both the foam line and the shoreward wash off it. That costs no depth
texture, works identically on `Mobile_Renderer` (which has `RequireDepthTexture: 0`), and
puts the foam *exactly* on the coastline rather than approximately near it. It also merges
plan bullets 1c and 4 into one mechanism instead of two.

### No decal projectors, because the ground is genuinely flat

URP has a Decal Projector and neither renderer asset carries its renderer feature. A
projector exists to lay a mark across geometry whose shape is unknown, and the shape here is
known exactly: every square metre of ground a vehicle can reach is at `y = 0` and flat,
because `CombatPlane` resolves every round on that plane. A quad a centimetre above it is the
same picture with no renderer feature, no depth sampling and no texture.

`RF_Mark.shader` **multiplies rather than blends**, which is what makes one material serve
grass, sand and asphalt at once — including across the seam between two of them. A stain
darkens what is under it, so multiplying is what a stain actually does; it needs no lighting
of its own, because whatever it sits on has already been lit; and white is the identity, so
fading a mark out and switching it off are the same operation.

## Two things worth knowing that nobody would guess

### The sea is too dark to shade

The first tuning pass added a swell and it did nothing. The reason is that the two waters are
the darkest colours on the map by a long way — the open sea's base colour is `(0.035, 0.075,
0.135)`, which is about `(0.001, 0.004, 0.014)` once it is linear. Tilting a normal by 19
degrees moves diffuse shading by a quarter, and a quarter of nothing is nothing. Measured off
the coast still, the deep water had a standard deviation of **1.8 grey levels** across the
whole lower half of the frame.

So on this map, everything that can show the sea is *additive*: the fresnel sheen and the sun
glint. Retuning around that took the same measurement to **4.1**, with the mean moving only
three levels. That is the difference between water and paint, and none of it is diffuse.

### The sun glint provably cannot appear in the gameplay view

The glint is a sharp specular off the wave normal — deliberately sharp, because the surfaces
table refuses a gloss on both waters and means it: a broad lobe over one enormous flat sheet
is exactly what made M7's first sea read *lighter* than the land it has to contrast with.

Where the highlight can appear is decided by two angles that were both fixed long before this
pass. The sun is at 52 degrees, yawed −30 (`LightingTuning`); the gameplay camera looks down
at 58 (`TopDownCameraRig`). Their half-vector is **34 degrees off vertical**, and the swell
can only tilt a normal by 19 — so `pow(N·H, 220)` comes out at 1e-18 and there is no
highlight anywhere in the frame, at any strength. From directly overhead, where the level
overview and the map view look from, the half-vector is 19 degrees off vertical and the swell
reaches it exactly. That is why the sea sparkles in the map view and not in the game.

The fix is not to widen the lobe, which would lift the value of the whole sea. It is that the
fresnel sheen lerps its colour towards the sun where the reflected view direction points at
it:

```hlsl
half sunward = saturate(dot(reflect(-viewDirectionWS, normalWS), sun.direction));
half3 sheen = lerp(_FresnelColour.rgb, _GlintColour.rgb * sun.color, sunward * sunward);
```

That is inside the fresnel, so it is zero when the camera looks straight down and largest at
the grazing far end of the gameplay frame — which is where sun on water actually reads.

## Gotchas

### A shader's default only reaches a material on the day it is created

The first cut left the shared water numbers — foam colour, glint sharpness, fresnel power —
as defaults on the shader, on the argument that a number no row disagrees about is a constant
rather than a column. That was wrong in a way that is invisible until you try to change one:
**Unity writes every property into the `.mat` when the material is created and never consults
the shader's default again.** A "default" in a shader is a constant nobody can change without
deleting the material, and deleting a generated material hands out a fresh GUID and unbinds
every level catalog row that named it.

So `SurfaceLook` holds the shared numbers as constants and `GeneratedMaterials.Paint` writes
every one of them. `SurfaceLookTests.TheSharedWaterNumbersReachTheMaterial` is the test that
would have caught it.

### A prefab cannot carry a mesh that is not an asset yet

`BuildScorch` generates its quad in code — four corners, two triangles, the only geometry in
the project that does not come out of Blender. The first version called
`SaveAsPrefabAsset` and *then* `AddObjectToAsset(mesh, saved)`, which looks right and is not:
the `MeshFilter` is serialised before the mesh exists to be pointed at, so the prefab on disk
had a null mesh in it and a spare mesh hanging off the side of the asset. It cost one test
run rather than an afternoon, and only because the test asked whether the scorch had any
geometry at all.

The mesh is now written as its own asset first, and **copied into the existing one** with
`EditorUtility.CopySerialized` rather than replaced — `CreateAsset` over an existing path
deletes the file and its meta, which is the fresh-GUID trap again.

### A trail renderer keeps its points in world space

Which is exactly right until a vehicle stops travelling through the world to get somewhere.
Being shot sends the wreck home to its bunker; deploying puts a new one on the pad. Either
way a naive `TrailRenderer` draws a ribbon straight across the map. `TyreTracks` cuts both
trails when the vehicle moves more than `Teleport` (4 m) in one frame — nothing in the game
covers a fifth of that under its own power at sixty frames a second.

### Nothing spawns an effect outside play mode

`GroundMark.Spawn` refuses when `Application.isPlaying` is false. That is a lesson from the
doors pass, which lost an afternoon to a `DebrisBurst` frozen at the origin of every structure
a preview had knocked down and read it as the rubble being a metre tall — nothing steps a
spawned effect in the editor, so anything spawned there parks itself in the scene and reads as
geometry. A scorch would fail the same way and be harder to spot, because a dark patch on the
ground looks like a dark patch on the ground. A preview that wants one builds it by hand with
`Configure`, the way the dust trail is posed rather than driven.

### Foam traces every tooth of the coastline's staircase

The first foam was near-white and continuous, and it turned the coast into a ribbon of pack
ice. Worse, it made visible something nobody had had to look at before: a natural coastline is
quantised to half a cell (`SurfaceField.Outline`), and a bright line drawn along it traces
every one of those teeth. Dialling the foam back — a paler colour and a much higher threshold,
so the band breaks up into surf rather than running solid — fixed both at once.

### `Awake` does not run in the editor, and white is the identity of a multiply

The marks contact sheet came out completely blank on the first try and looked exactly like a
shader that was not drawing. `GroundMark` read its stain colour off its material in `Awake` —
which never runs for a component a preview scene instantiates, so the colour stayed at its
`Color.white` default, and white multiplied against the ground is the ground. Reading it
lazily on first use fixed it. A blend would have failed visibly (a white patch); a multiply
fails by being perfectly correct and invisible.

### `ComputeFogIntensity` answers zero when fog is switched off

The second reason the same sheet was blank. `RF_Mark.shader` scaled its stain by the fog
intensity, on the reasoning that at the far end of the draw distance there is no ground behind
the haze left to stain. But URP's `ComputeFogIntensity` returns **0.0 when no fog mode is
enabled at all** — the same number it returns for *fully fogged out* — and the art preview and
the level overview both switch fog off deliberately (see `LIGHTING_NOTES.md`). Every mark in
those views was multiplied by zero.

`MixFogColor(stain, white, fogFactor)` is the form that is right either way: handing it white
as the fog colour is how you say "fog whitens a multiplier", and with fog disabled it returns
its argument untouched.

### The working tree mixes LF and CRLF *within one file*

`GeneratedMaterials.cs` has LF line endings in one region and CRLF in another. Any scripted
patching has to match `\r?\n` rather than `\n`, or an exact-match replace silently finds
nothing. This is a sharper version of the per-file warning already recorded from the turrets
pass.

### A scorch could have quietly disagreed with its own flash

Caught by adversarial review, not by a test: `Explosion.Spawn` clamped the flash's own
`radius` to a 0.1 m floor but sized the scorch off the raw, unclamped `blastRadius` argument.
Both of today's callers always pass a positive radius, so nothing actually misbehaves - but a
future zero-or-negative caller would have gotten a visible flash and a silently skipped scorch
(`GroundMark.Spawn`'s minimum-size guard would have rejected it) that disagreed with each
other for no reason a reader could see. Now both read off the already-clamped `burst.radius`.

## What this does not do

- **No night.** The lighting pass left time-of-day as the designed-for next step and this
  pass does not take it. The water's fresnel colour is a written-down pale sky blue rather
  than the sky material's own, so a night sky and a night sea would have to be moved
  together — one more row in a table, not a rewrite.
- **No amphibious anything.** The water still drowns you. This pass changed no rule and broke
  no balance test; every number in `SurfaceTuning` is exactly as the surfaces pass left it,
  including both waters' smoothness of zero.
- **The wheel tracks have never been photographed, and cannot be.** Everything else in this
  pass is in a still. A `TrailRenderer` builds its ribbon during the game loop, and a headless
  one-shot render never runs one — so a posed track has all its points (154 baked vertices, a
  correctly placed flat mesh, the right material) and draws nothing at all. `BakeMesh` did not
  rescue it either: the baked mesh does not render in this pipeline even with an opaque
  material on it, which is how it was established that the problem is the render path rather
  than the shader. What *is* verified is everything either side of the gap — the rig, the
  widths, the exclusion of the helicopter and the surface-to-darkness derivation by test, and
  the mark shader's ribbon branch by the contact sheet's fourth cell, which is that material
  laid as a strip and is honestly labelled as not being a wheel track. How two ruts read
  behind a moving jeep is the one thing here that has only been reasoned about.
- **The generator still places no walls**, and this pass does not change that either — see
  `WALLS_NOTES.md`.
