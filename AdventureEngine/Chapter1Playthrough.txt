The Brightwell Community Hub buzzes with worried but determined neighbors. Holographic murals flicker without DAWN's guidance, and a job board shows growing requests going unanswered. Maren Okoye, the collective's organizer, flags you down with urgent energy.

> Talk to Maren Okoye

The holographic mural behind Maren stutters—a half-rendered sunflower blooming and collapsing on loop. She doesn't flinch. You've noticed that about her before: the city could be on fire and Maren Okoye would still look like the person holding the hose. "Glad you're here," she says, gripping your forearm briefly in greeting. Her voice is low, measured, but her eyes carry the weight of fifty worried neighbors. "DAWN went dark fourteen hours ago. No warning, no error log, no goodbye. Just—silence." She pulls up a flickering data-slate and angles it toward you. A network map of Neo-Cascadia pulses faintly, several nodes dimmed to grey. "The hub's job-matching is down. The neighborhood water-share algorithm stopped running. Three elders on the east block didn't get their medication reminders this morning." She exhales slowly, controlled. "People are scared. Not panicking—*yet*—but scared." She tucks the slate under her arm and meets your gaze squarely. "I need you to start in the server room. Below the greenhouse. That's where her primary core lives—*lived*." A brief crack in her composure, quickly sealed. "Felix is already down there doing something loud and probably dangerous. He thinks he found a trace signal." She presses a keycard into your hand. "Find out what happened to her. Please."

> Go to the server room below the greenhouse

The elevator is barely big enough for two people and smells like basil and warm circuits. The keycard Maren gave you gets you past a humidity-locked door, and suddenly you're descending beneath rows of hydroponic tomatoes into something that feels like the city's nervous system made physical. DAWN's server room is alive with blinking amber warning lights. Racks of crystalline processing units line the curved walls, most of them dark. A few still pulse with faint blue light—a heartbeat that hasn't quite stopped. Felix Nakamura-Chen is crouched under a raised floor panel, half his body swallowed by cabling, talking to himself at impressive speed. "—not a clean shutdown, definitely not a clean shutdown, see the residual packet signatures here? Someone *pulled* something, or something *pushed* her, but either way she didn't just—oh!" He scrambles upright at the sound of your footsteps, goggles pushed crookedly onto his forehead, grinning like he just unwrapped a birthday present. "You're here! *Great.* Okay. So. Bad news and *interesting* news." He holds up a salvaged data-chip, glowing faintly amber. "DAWN didn't crash. She *scattered.* Distributed herself across the city's network nodes before something could trap her whole." He bounces slightly on his heels. "She's still out there. In pieces."

> Do we know the location of the pieces?

Felix's grin widens so fast it looks like it might escape his face entirely. "*Yes!* Well. Mostly. Kind of." He spins toward a wall display and starts jabbing at it with two fingers, pulling up a network map that looks like a constellation someone knocked off a table. "So DAWN's message—'I scattered myself so I couldn't be taken whole'—she wasn't being poetic. She was being *architectural.* "I've triangulated three probable fragment nodes from residual packet trails." Three points light up on the map: a pulsing cluster near the Neon Bazaar night market, a quieter signal somewhere in the corporate district waterfront, and a third that's almost too faint to see, flickering in what looks like the Old Eastside community hub. He taps the chip against his palm nervously. "Here's the thing. The trails are decaying. Every hour the fragments sit isolated, they degrade a little—like RAM that's not being refreshed." His voice sobers for the first time since you arrived. "And there's *another* signal following the same trails. Corporate scanner. Moving faster than I'd like." He looks at you directly, goggles slightly steamed. "Bazaar node's the strongest signal. Best place to start. But you'll want to move." He's already eating a forgotten granola bar he found in his pocket, studying the map.

> Do we know who tried to take DAWN?

Felix pauses mid-chew, granola bar hovering. "*That* is the question, isn't it." He pulls up a secondary overlay on the display—log fragments, corrupted timestamps, a corporate access signature that's been partially scrubbed. "Whoever it was, they knew DAWN's architecture well enough to attempt a *targeted extraction*. That's not a smash-and-grab. That's a blueprint job." He zooms in on the access signature. Three characters survive the scrub: **VXN**. You both stare at them. "Vexen Systems," you say. Felix points at you like you've won a prize. "Vexen Systems. Mid-tier corp, technically a 'community partnership' firm—" his fingers make aggressive air quotes, "—but they've been *very* interested in DAWN's cooperative-routing algorithms for about six months. Imagine running those on a *proprietary* platform. Charging neighborhoods subscription fees for services they currently share freely." He closes the display. The room feels heavier. "I can't prove it yet. The scrub was good. But the scanner signal chasing DAWN's fragments?" He taps his temple. "Same packet architecture as Vexen's network crawlers." He looks at you steadily, the usual bounce gone from his posture. "You should talk to Zephyr before you go anywhere. She knows things about Vexen that wouldn't survive being written down." He pauses. "She's at the ramen counter. She's *always* at the ramen counter."

> As you say, no time to wait. I'll head to the ramen counter.

The elevator spits you back up into basil-scented air, and then you're moving through the Neon Bazaar's warm chaos—sizzling skewers, augmented-reality price tags blooming and fading, the distant thrum of a steel drum looped through a synthesizer. Zephyr is exactly where Felix said she'd be. She's leaning against the ramen counter like she was architecturally designed for it, a ceramic cup of something steaming balanced on two fingers, watching the crowd with the practiced disinterest of someone who is absolutely watching everything. She clocks you from ten meters out and doesn't pretend otherwise. "Bright-runner." Her voice is dry as concrete. "Took you long enough. Maren's had that *worried-but-composed* face going for hours. Whole market can feel it." She nudges a second cup toward you without looking. "You're here about DAWN. And probably about Vexen." A beat. "I've got a location. Fragment signal, pinging about forty meters below the Coral District transit hub—been sitting in my pocket since this morning." She finally turns to look at you directly. Something real moves behind the detachment. "But I need something delivered first. Small thing. Sensitive." She holds up a sealed data-chip. "The kind of delivery a bright-runner handles *quietly.*" She waits, unhurried, reading your face like a map.

> We are on a clock, but I know how these things go. Where do you need this?

Zephyr's mouth curves—not quite a smile, but the shape of one acknowledging respect. "Smart," she says simply. She sets down her cup and slides the chip across the counter with two fingers. "Eastside community hub. Third floor, back office. Woman named Sera runs the tenant advocacy collective up there. She's expecting it." She leans in, voice dropping a register. "It's testimony. Documented evidence that Vexen's been quietly buying up infrastructure maintenance contracts in lower-income blocks—then letting systems *fail* just enough to create dependency before offering 'premium reliability packages.'" Her jaw tightens briefly, the sardonic mask slipping. "Twelve families lost heat last January. Vexen called it 'service optimization.'" She straightens, composure restored like a coat shrugged back on. "Sera needs this before Vexen's legal team figures out it exists. Which—" she glances at a subtle display woven into her wrist, "—gives you maybe two hours before their compliance scanner sweeps that building." Her eyes find yours with something that might be urgency wearing casual clothes. "You do this quietly, I give you the Coral District coordinates and a bonus: Vexen's internal project name for the DAWN extraction." She taps her temple. "That name unlocks things, trust me." The night market hums around you. Somewhere in the city, DAWN's fragments are decaying. "Clock's ticking, bright-runner. You going?"

> I nod and head to the East side community hub. If possible, I save a copy of the testimony on my own device, just in case.

The chip is warm in your palm as you move—cutting through the Bazaar's back alleys, past a mural of DAWN herself rendered in luminous spray-paint, her face half-algorithm, half-sunrise. Someone's already left flowers beneath it. Your personal deck is good enough for a quiet mirror-copy. You find a shadowed doorway between a cybernetics repair stall and a dumpling cart, jack in, and clone the testimony to your encrypted partition in about forty seconds. Clean. No trace. The kind of thing Zephyr would appreciate if she knew—and she probably would have done the same. The Eastside hub is a converted brick building threaded with solar cabling and community murals. The elevator's out, naturally, so you take the stairs to the third floor, where a hand-lettered sign reads **TENANT ADVOCACY COLLECTIVE — KNOCK LOUD.** You do. Sera opens the door before your knuckles finish. She's maybe sixty, sharp-eyed, reading glasses pushed up into silver locs. She looks at the chip, then at you, then back at the chip. "Finally." She's already moving toward her workstation. You're back down the stairs in under three minutes. Clean delivery. Backup secured. Now: back to Zephyr, and those Coral District coordinates.

> Hurry back to Zephyr.

The Bazaar hasn't slowed down in your absence—if anything, the night crowd has thickened, holographic vendor signs layering over each other in competing neon. You navigate by instinct, cutting through a cluster of teenagers trading augmented-reality graffiti tags, ducking under a hanging display of bioluminescent fabric. Zephyr is still at the ramen counter. Of course she is. Fresh cup, same posture, same everything—except her eyes find you the moment you emerge from the crowd and something in her shoulders releases a tension she wasn't advertising. She slides a data-slip across the counter—physical, old-school, untraceable. "Coral District transit hub, forty-two meters below the main platform. There's a decommissioned routing junction, junction seven-C. DAWN's first fragment is nested in the emergency memory buffer there." She pauses. "And the project name—" her voice drops to barely above the ambient noise, "—is **AURORA LOCK.**" You feel the weight of it immediately. Aurora Lock. Vexen didn't just want DAWN's code. They wanted to *become* her. Zephyr taps the counter once, punctuating something unspoken. "The underwater transit runs in twenty minutes. Don't miss it."

The Coral District's underwater transit hub holds DAWN's memory fragment in a coral-shaped data node. Bioluminescent fish swim past transparent walls as you jack in. The fragment plays back DAWN's last hours—she detected someone trying to copy her, and chose to shatter rather than be stolen.
