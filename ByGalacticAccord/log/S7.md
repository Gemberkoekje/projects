# S7 — Ships from parts

The step's goal, deliverable and verify are in [`plan.md`](../plan.md) §3, with a status line that links here.
Section references (§n) are to the design, [`by_galactic_accord_gdd_v0.3.md`](../../by_galactic_accord_gdd_v0.3.md).

*S7 status (2026-11-05): done.* Every ship is now built from parts, and ships are made, ordered, bought and sold through
contracts like everything else:

- **Parts are goods.** There are eight part kinds (design §15.4), each at grade A, B or C, which makes 24 goods. A part
  takes one cargo unit. It sits in stock, storage or a hold, and is sold and hauled like food. Grade B scales the part's
  main stat to 96% and its value to 85%; grade C to 92% and 70%.
- **A ship is a frame, a drive and a module or none.** Its stats are computed from its parts and explained by them:
  cargo, speed (power over mass), toughness, reliability, refrigeration, running cost and value. Five combinations are
  the standard designs (design §6.2).
- **Three factories** make the parts from ordinary goods: Tycho Frameworks (frames), Meridian Drive Works (drives) and
  Halcyon Modules (modules). They make to stock, and what is wanted first. A factory short of an input for 72 hours
  makes the part without it, a grade lower for each input missing. It buys its inputs with wanted Sales and sells its
  parts as a producer sells goods.
- **A Build orders a ship.** The client names the parts, price, deadline and terms and posts it. Yards that see it bid
  from what they know. Tycho Yards is cheap, quick and lenient; Helios Orbital Yards is dear, slower and strict.
  - On signing, the client pays the yard a 20% deposit and locks the balance and tax in escrow. The yard locks a bond.
  - The yard orders each missing part with a wanted Sale delivered to its yard, which anyone can fill or haul. It
    assembles one ship at a time, then hands it over.
  - The Build's package sets its substitution policy. Strict allows none. Standard allows one grade lower at 20% of
    the part's price off. Lenient allows any lower grade at 15% a grade.
  - A part that can't come in time is a supply failure. The yard proposes more time with compensation, a part a grade
    lower at a discount, or the money back with compensation. The client chooses: an AI by utility, the player at a
    hard pause.
  - An idle yard builds a standard design on spec and sells it from its lot.
- **Ships change hands by Sale,** at signing.
  - Traders buy when their fleets have been busy (or order a Build), and sell their idlest ship when they have been
    idle.
  - Edge Salvage buys cheap, ferries the ship home and sells it second-hand at the frontier.
  - Every ship keeps a history: commissioned (with what was substituted), changed hands (who, to whom, for what),
    incidents, broken up.
- **The client** has the ship sheet, the build composer, the yards' bids as threads, a Build's orders tree, and ships
  for sale on the board.

**Over ten seeds at a million ticks every invariant holds.** That includes goods conservation through parts into ships,
and every part built in found in a ship. The traders' fleets grow from 9 ships to 15–16 and ships change hands 19–218
times a run. **16% more contracts are fulfilled than in S6b, more on all ten seeds, and 1.2% of those signed fail or
are cancelled (S6b 5.1%).** Walking away, the defect open since S3, fell from 3,251 a run to 204, because the bigger
fleets have hours to carry. Helios Prime gets 99–100% of its machinery and 98–100% of its food. **The market reader
beats the board taker on all ten seeds, at 1.93× its credits**, but that ratio is inflated: the taker went broke on two
seeds (₵0 and ₵89), and both bots end poorer than in S6b, most likely because more AI ships compete for the same work.

- The fee share, growth and delays stay.
- The AI's budget became a total per post rather than a limit per ring: 5% of the price for goods and hauls, 1% for a

## What was built

**Parts and ships** (`Domain/ShipPart.cs` and `Domain/ShipDesign.cs`, new; `Domain/ShipType.cs` is gone):

- `PartKind` holds the eight kinds:
  - frames: light, bulk, courier;
  - drives: standard, fast;
  - modules: refrigeration unit, armour plating, cargo pod.
- `ShipPart` is the table. Each kind has its cargo, mass, toughness, power, reliability, preservation, whether it runs
  hot, a base price (₵2,500–12,000) and its main stat. `Grade` scales that stat (`StatScale`) and the value
  (`ValueShare`).
- `GoodType` gains the 24 part goods. `Goods.Info` prices each at its base price times its grade's share.
- `ShipParts` is a frame, a drive and an optional module. `ShipStats` computes from it:
  - cargo: the frame plus modules;
  - mass, and speed as power over mass;
  - toughness: the frame times the armour;
  - reliability (from the drive) and preservation (from a refrigeration unit);
  - running cost: 0.003% of the parts' base price an hour;
  - value: the parts plus ₵2,000 of assembly.
- Each stat carries the `StatTerm`s that make it, so the sheet can say why. An incident's chance is 1/reliability and
  its severity 1/toughness (`IncidentResolver`).
- `Designs` are the five standard ships:

  | Design | Frame | Drive | Module |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Light Freighter | light | standard | — |
  | Bulk Hauler | bulk | standard | — |
  | Fast Courier | courier | fast | — |
  | Refrigerated Transport | light | standard | refrigeration unit |
  | Armoured Freighter | light | standard | armour plating |

  The starting ships are built from these designs at grade A.
- `Ship` keeps its parts, its stats and a typed history (`ShipRecord`, `ShipEventKind`: Commissioned, ChangedHands,
  Incident, Scrapped). It also tracks its utilization over a window and whether it is listed for sale.

**Factories** (`Actors/DecisionEngine.Factories.cs`, new; `PartRecipe` and `FactoryProfile` in `Profiles.cs`):

- `RunFactory` runs each heartbeat:
  - `FinishWork` produces the part.
  - `StartWork` picks the next part with `NextPart`: the oldest wanted Sale it knows of for a part it makes and doesn't
    hold, otherwise the kind it holds least of, under the recipe's stock cap. It consumes the inputs and sinks the labour.
  - `BuyInputs` posts wanted Sales at its door.
  - It then sells like a producer: `TryAnswer` at its door, and `SellDelivered` for each part in stock.
- `ActorState.Makes(good)` and `ProfileFor(good)` let the producer code serve a factory's several goods.
  `PriceModel.PartAsk` is the factory's price: its cost plus its margin, 10% over when out of stock, 10% under when full.
- What an actor knows of prices is now keyed by producer *and* good (`ActorKnowledge.Stored(producer, good)`), because
  a factory sells several goods.

**Builds** (`BuildContract` in `Domain/Contract.cs`; `Actors/DecisionEngine.Yards.cs` and `Simulation/Market.Ships.cs`,
new):

- *Posting:* `Market.PostBuild` shows the Build at the place it is posted. Its jurisdiction stays provisional until a
  yard signs.
- *Bidding:*
  - An AI client's Build is taken by a yard that sees it, at its price if the yard's `BuildCost` fits, otherwise in
    closed form.
  - The player's Build reaches the yards it is shown to, and each counters with `BuilderQuote`: its own costs, its
    habitual package and a deadline it can make.
- *Signing* (`Market.TakeBuild`):
  - `PerformUnder` sets the yard's jurisdiction.
  - The 20% deposit goes to the yard (`TransferUpTo`), the balance and tax into escrow (`Contract.EscrowedPart`), and
    the yard locks its bond.
  - A `YardJob` is queued.
- *Building* (`RunYard`):
  - A signed Build goes before a spec job. Each job takes the parts in stock that its policy allows; the jobs ahead get
    theirs set aside first.
  - It orders each missing part with `Market.PostPartsOrder`: a wanted Sale of one part delivered to the yard
    (`SaleContract.ForBuild`), whose cause is the Build. `ChooseGrade` picks the grade, and the deadline comes from where
    the part can be had.
  - Every job sources its parts at once. Only assembly is one ship at a time.
  - `Finish` builds the ship with `SimulationContext.BuildShip`, which moves the parts out of stock and books them as
    built in.
- *Handover* (`Settlement.HandOverBuild`):
  - `Terms.Substitution` compares the ship with the spec.
  - Within the policy, the client gets a discount.
  - Outside it, the client inspects: an AI with `WouldRejectShip`, the player with a `DecisionKind.Inspection`.
  - If the ship is accepted, escrow pays the yard less any discounts and late fee, and the ship transfers. If it is
    rejected, everything goes back and the ship stays the yard's.
- *Supply failure:*
  - An order that ends without its part is reordered if there is time. Otherwise `ProposeSupplyFailure` puts the
    remedies to the client.
  - `Market.ChooseRemedy` carries out the choice: `Extend` (a later deadline and compensation), `Substitute` (the slot's
    grade lowered, a discount, a sooner deadline) or `Cancel` (`CancelReason.SupplyFailure`).
- *Cancelling* (`Settlement.CancelBuild`): if the client cancels at will, the yard keeps the deposit. If the yard
  cancels, it returns the deposit and pays as much again.
- `Terms.ForBuild` sets the packages: grace of 3, 7 and 14 days; a late fee of 1%, 0.5% and 0.25% a day, capped at the
  bond (15%, 10%, 5%).

**Ship sales, fleets and salvage** (`ShipSaleContract`; `Actors/DecisionEngine.Ships.cs`, new):

- *Selling:* `Market.PostShipSale` lists a free, empty ship, which is laid up and runs at a quarter of its cost until it
  sells. `Market.BuyShip` signs, and `Settlement.HandOverShip` hands the ship over at once: through escrow in Accord
  space, or paid at handover on the frontier, where a buyer that can't pay defaults.
- *A yard's lot:* `SellLot` prices a spec ship at its value plus half the yard's margin, 5% less each fortnight, and
  never below half. A spec ship that sells within the window tells the yard to build another (`YardState.SpecSells`).
- *Fleets:* `ManageFleet`, the end of `RunTrader`, measures `FleetUtilization` over 30 days.
  - A company busier than 80% wants a ship, a pilot busier than 90%, each eased by its growth ambition. It must be
    under the fleet cap (3 for a company, 2 for a pilot) with nothing on order.
  - `Acquire` buys a ship of its busiest design at up to 95% of the value, or another design at 85%. If no such ship
    is for sale, it posts a Build.
  - Its working capital keeps the price back meanwhile (`GrowthFund`), so the money `PayOut` would sink goes into the
    ship.
  - A fleet idler than 40% sells its idlest ship once it has sat free ten days, one sale at a time.
  - A pilot whose one dear ship barely works, and whose savings are worth less than the ship, sells it to buy a
    cheaper one (`Uneconomic`).
- *Edge Salvage* (`RunSalvage`):
  - It buys at no more than 45% of a ship's value and ferries the ship home (`Market.Ferry`, a job with no cargo).
  - It asks 75% of the value, falling to 50%.
  - It breaks up a ship unsold for a year (`ScrapShip`; the parts are written off as lost).
- *Invariants:*
  - Goods conservation is produced = consumed + held + lost + built in, per good.
  - The parts of every ship built in this world add up to what is built in (`PartsInShips`).
  - Every ship is in its owner's fleet and no other.
- *Journal:* six new entries: `ShipBuilt`, `StockShipBuilt`, `ShipHandedOver`, `SupplyFailure`, `RemedyChosen` and
  `ShipScrapped`. There are new decision kinds and choices (`SupplyFailure`; `Extend`,
  `Substitute`, `TakeRefund`), and new failure kinds for the soak (`SupplyFailure`, `Substituted`,
  `CancelledOnSupplyFailure`).
- *Advertising:* the AI's budget is now what it has paid to show a post in all (`Contract.AdFeesPaid`), against
  `Advertising.Budget`: `AdBudgetShare` of 5% for goods and hauls, `AdAssetBudgetShare` of 1% for a Build or a ship for
  sale.
- *Soak harness:* a ships block (parts made by grade, builds, ships built, supply failures and remedies, ships sold, ships
  in the sector, ships broken up, fleets), and a "built in" column in the goods table.

**Presentation and client:**

- `Views/ShipView.cs` (new):
  - The ship sheet: parts, each stat with the parts that make it, where the ship was built and what was substituted,
    its history.
  - A Build's orders tree: each part, its order, who took it and what carries it.
  - The design and part choices for the composer.
  - `Components/ShipPanel.razor` (new) draws the sheet. A ship in the ledger or on a contract opens it.
- The composer gains the kinds Build (a design or a custom combination, with the ship's stats and the Build's terms)
  and Ship (sell one of your ships).
- The contract panel:
  - draws a Build as a document with its orders tree;
  - shows the yards' bids as threads you can accept, decline or counter;
  - offers a ship for sale with Buy and the ship's sheet;
  - puts a supply failure or an out-of-policy ship to you with what each answer does.
- The board lists ships for sale (with the tax) and Builds. The ledger lists your ships.
- About 250 new templates (675 → 924), under these keys:
  - `Ship.*`, `Stat.*`, `Part.*`, `Design.*`, `Orders.*`;
  - the Build and Ship forms of the contract entries;
  - `Decision.SupplyFailure.*`, `Remedy.*`, `News.*.Build` and `News.*.Ship`.
- `GameSession.Grant` and the client's `?credits=N` give the player more starting credits. The browser check uses it
  to order a ship (a check aid only).

## Decisions taken in code

- **A Build is shown where its poster posts it** (design A.2; the client chooses the place in the composer) and is
  performed where its yard is. Its jurisdiction is provisional until a yard signs, and then becomes the yard's
  (`PerformUnder`). The hand-over place is the yard's.
- **The AI's ad budget is a total per post,** not a limit per ring. Under S6b's rule an AI paid for each ring that cost
  under 5% of the post's price, and paid again every time it reposted. On ships worth ₵8,000–20,000, reposted each
  fortnight, that helped drain the yards and Edge Salvage to ₵0 in early runs. A total of 5% still reaches four jumps
  for goods. For Builds and ships it is 1%. The fee share, growth and delays are S6b's.
- **Substitution is the Build's alone.** Its package sets the policy for all its parts (design §3.4). Design §3.4 also gives
  Sales of graded goods a substitution policy; here a yard's parts order asks for one grade, a different grade is a
  different good, and the Build's policy decides what the yard may fit.
- **A part is one cargo unit,** whatever its kind. A bulk frame in a courier's hold is odd, but it keeps parts movable
  by any ship.
- **Every job sources its parts at once,** and only assembly waits its turn. With one job at a time, a queued Build's
  parts were ordered late, and in early runs Builds were cancelled. A parts order's deadline is tight (how long the part
  takes to come from where it can be had, plus a margin), so a failed order leaves time to reorder.
- **An AI client in default waits while its yard still has the job,** rather than cancelling for the bond. In early runs
  AI clients cancelled Builds their yards were still building. The player is asked as with any default.
- **A ship laid up for sale costs a quarter of its running cost.** This isn't in the design, whose §6.5 has every ship
  cost the same each day whether or not it earns. At full cost a listed ship bled its seller for months, and the yards
  and Edge Salvage went to ₵0.
- **Edge Salvage breaks up a ship unsold for a year.** This isn't in the design either. Without it, a ship nobody would
  buy stayed on Edge Salvage's lot for good, costing its laid-up running cost.
- **Fleet caps:** 3 ships for a company, 2 for a pilot. The design names no cap; these are hand-set.
- **Spec designs:** Tycho Yards builds Light Freighters, and Helios Orbital Yards Refrigerated Transports and Light
  Freighters. These are hand-set too.
- **A yard builds on spec only while its spec ships sell** (`SpecSells`): one that sold within the window lets it build
  another, and one that sat stops it.
- **Ship stats.** Incident chance is 1/reliability and severity 1/toughness: design §8.4 says armour reduces severity
  and a less reliable drive raises the odds. Before S7 the Armed Freighter had 0.6× the chance and 0.5× the severity;
  Kael's Armoured Freighter now has 1× and 0.625×. Running cost is 0.003% of the parts' base price an hour, and value
  is the parts plus ₵2,000, which matches the rough prices of design §15.6 within ₵500, except the Bulk Hauler (₵17,000
  against ₵18,000). The Armoured Freighter now costs ₵0.42 an hour, not ₵0.60.
- **A ship for sale hands over at signing,** so its Sale has no grace, late fee or bond. The buyer reads the sheet and
  history first and takes the ship as it is (design §6.4).
- **Starting ships are built at grade A** from the designs and enter history "in service before the records begin". Their
  parts never count as produced, so conservation counts only parts made in the run.

## Gotchas

- **Knowledge had to be re-keyed** by producer and good. Keyed by producer only, a factory's asks for its several parts
  would overwrite one another.
- **A pilot listed the same ship twice** and then sold every ship it had: the check "do I have a spare?" counted ships
  already listed. Now it counts unlisted ships, with one sale up at a time.
- **Clearing a supply failure's proposal before cancelling** made the refund's compensation ₵0 (`Settlement.Cancel` reads
  it). It is cleared after.
- **The player's strict-inspection test was pre-empted** by a cancel-for-default decision raised the same hour. The
  test answers Wait first.
- **The browser check's ledger.** The ledger now lists the player's ship first. Its old check (goods aboard) became
  info, since at that point the ship may still be on its way to the goods; the check now asks that the ledger lists the
  ship, which opens its sheet.
- **A Build must be shown where the yards are.** The player's Build reaches only the yards it is shown to, so the
  composer pre-ticks the systems with a yard (₵8 each in the screenshot), and the player can untick them.

## What the step showed

- **More ships fixed what more information didn't.** S5 and S6 tried to fix walking away (a producer's Sale left
  untaken by carriers) with haggling and limited sight. Fleets that grow to their work cut it from 3,251 to 204 a run,
  and the share that fails or is cancelled from 5.1% to 1.2%.
- **The fleets fill to their caps.** Every seed ends with 15 or 16 traders' ships against a cap of 16. Once the fleets
  are full, ships are ordered rarely (5–13 Builds in 114 game years), which is why the long runs meet no supply
  failure.
- **A busier sector is harder for a lone player.** The board taker ends with about a third of its S6b credits and the
  market reader with about half, and the taker went broke twice. My reading, not measured: more AI ships take the easy
  work first.
- **Ads matter less when more ships carry:** 13.8% of signed contracts were found only through an ad (S6b 24.8%), and
  fees fell 28%.

## Questions for review

- **The Build board** (design A.2): a Build is shown where its poster posts it and performed at the yard. Is that what
  you want? The mockup assumed the poster's location, which it is when the player posts at home.
- **The ad budget as a total per post,** 1% for Builds and ships. It deviates from D38's text ("as long as that ring
  costs no more than 5%"). I noted it under the design's open question on showing a post further rather than rewriting
  §7.1. Should D38 say "in all"?
- **No supply failure in the long runs.** Each path is tested, and S10's beat 4 has to make one likely (plan S10 says so).
  Or should parts fail more often now, for example by making the frame maker's inputs scarcer?
- **Pilots end broke:** 3 of 70 traders over ten seeds (seed 3: Kael ₵44, Pell ₵66; seed 7: Sable ₵0). Each had one
  or two ships and no work worth its running cost. S8's insolvency would end them. Is that the intended story, or
  should a pilot sell sooner?
- **Kael's armour.** Kael's running cost is now right, and it can sell its ship. Following design §8.4, armour now makes
  incidents milder but not rarer (before S7, 0.6× as often and half as severe; now as often and 0.625× as severe), so its
  freighter's edge on red routes is smaller. Its end credits range from ₵44 to ₵35,986 (S6b ₵46,820–51,000). Keep
  §8.4 as it is, or should armour also cut the chance?
- **The bots' 1.93×** is inflated by the taker's collapse on two seeds; without them it is 1.70×. Should S10's bots
  start with more credits, or is a taker going broke a fair result?
- **Parts as one cargo unit, substitution only on the Build (not on a parts order), and the `?credits` check aid:** fine
  as they are?
- **Two mechanisms not in the design:** a ship laid up for sale at a quarter of its running cost, and salvage breaking up
  a ship unsold a year. Should the design take them in?
- **Fleet caps (3 and 2) and spec designs (Light Freighter, Refrigerated Transport)** are hand-set. Fine for Slice 1?
